# The ICP audit of 2026-10-13 — where it lives, and what it did not fix

Written 2026-10-14 by the autopilot debrief agent, because reconstructing this
cost an hour of hand SQL across four repos, a search of the whole home
directory, and reading a rubric out of a folder no repo knows about.

## What happened

At **16:26 UTC on 2026-10-13** a single statement set **881 leads** to
`review_status='rejected'` and `signal_score=0`. Two minutes later **684 live
SmartLead sequences** belonging to those leads were paused. **720 of the 881
had already been emailed**, 436 of them earlier that same day.

This was **correct and deliberate** — Casey's own ICP cleanup, run from a
Claude Code session in this repo. It is not a fault and must not be "repaired".
It is why `approved_hold` fell 7,604 → 6,804 in the 10-14 cycle.

The four rejection patterns, all of which sit **inside otherwise valid niches**
(Real Estate & Property, Coaching & Consulting, Tax & Accounting):

| Pattern | Who it removes |
|---|---|
| `product_seller` | money comes from selling a product or inventory, content shows it off |
| `showcase_no_teaching` | tours, vlogs, reviews, reactions; nothing taught |
| `direct_competitor` | teaches YouTube growth or sells video/content services |
| `low_ticket_only` | sells courses, books, programs; no high-ticket service |
| `faceless_view_monetized` | no person fronting it, monetised on views |

## Where the artifacts are

**`/home/casey/backups/icp-audit-2026-10-13/`** — outside every repo, which is
why nothing links to it:

- `rubric.md` — **Casey's ICP in his own words**, with the four channels he
  flagged and why each fails. The most reusable thing in the folder.
- `icp-undo.sql` — a working undo. It restores `review_status`,
  `signal_score` and `signal_score_v2` from
  `signal_v2_components->'not_icp'->'prev'` and strips the notes stamp. Keyed on
  `at = '2026-10-13'`, so it reverses this audit and nothing else.
- `before.csv`, `verdicts.json`, `decisions.jsonl`, `held.json` — the inputs and
  the per-lead verdicts.
- `live-sequences.json`, `paused.jsonl`, `pause-run.log` — the SmartLead
  sequences paused afterwards (`paused ok 684 failed 0`).

Every affected row carries `[NOT ICP 2026-10-13]` at the front of `notes` and a
`not_icp` key in `signal_v2_components`, so the population is findable in SQL
without the folder.

## What is now enforced, and what is not

**Enforced.** Siege will not email them again. Its selector is
`review_status IN ('approved_hold','approved','needs_contact')`
(`automator/scripts/siege_tiers.py`, `SIEGE_REVIEW_STATUSES`), so `rejected`
drops out of every future plan. No money-path risk is left open.

**Not enforced.** **No code anywhere implements the rubric.** The five patterns
exist only as prose in that folder and as a stamp on 881 rows. The scoring
skill's `isExcludedCategory` cannot catch them, because these leads scored into
valid categories — that is the whole point of the audit. So:

- a lead of exactly this kind can still enter `approved_hold` through the
  recovery lane (`needs_contact` → verified email → promote), which is the only
  lane still adding sendable leads while discovery is paused;
- the measured bad rate in the shelf at audit time was **881 of ~7,604, about
  11.5%**. Nothing has changed that would make the next 11.5% different.

Deciding whether to build that gate is Casey's call, not an agent's: it is a
precision-versus-volume judgement on his own ICP, and the rubric needs a model
call per lead. Recorded here so the next session starts from the finding instead
of rediscovering it.

## The instrument that now sees this class

`bulkStatusWrites()` in `src/db.ts`, reported as
`parked.bulk_status_writes` in the debrief snapshot. Any second carrying 50+
`review_status` rows is a single-statement bulk rewrite — per-lead work spreads
across seconds — and it reports the size, the destination, how many were already
emailed, and the leading `[tag]` from `notes`. This audit reads as
`881 leads -> rejected, stamp [NOT ICP 2026-10-13]`.
