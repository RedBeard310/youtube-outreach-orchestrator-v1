The three questions

Q1Why did a second straight sending day send zero emails?

A function that writes explanations crashed, and it took the send with it. The plan itself was never in doubt. At 10:17:13Z Siege wrote a whole day to disk: 69 people against 69 free mailbox slots under an 870-message ramp, 9 live inboxes, email not paused, and every mailbox carrying a real campaign id. That last part matters, because it is the one thing that went wrong on Thursday, and on Friday it was clean.

At 10:20:49Z the run stopped with this:

  File "/home/casey/repos/automator/scripts/siege-plan.py", line 1298, in why_no_email
    fails = "; ".join(row.get("fails") or [])
TypeError: sequence item 0: expected str instance, dict found

why_no_email() exists to turn a blank space in the morning sample report into a sentence. Its own comment says why it was written: "Casey cannot tell a skill that correctly declined to write from a writer that fell over." It joins a list of failure strings into readable text, and one of those failures arrived as a structured object instead of a string. The code that exists to explain failures was taken down by an unexpected failure.

On its own that should have cost a report, not a day. What turned it into a lost day is the shape of the unit. siege-plan.service is a single systemd oneshot holding five commands that run in order: sync the warmup data, write and check 20 sample emails, record the standing approval, push the rest, write the report. systemd abandons every remaining command the moment one of them exits non-zero. The crash was in command two, so commands three, four and five never happened, and command four is the one that sends. The files on disk confirm it: Friday's folder holds plan.json and nothing else, where every other day this week holds samples.json and approval.json beside it.

So a cosmetic string operation, in a step that sends nothing and spends nothing, blocked a step that would have emailed 69 people.

Q2Is this the same thing that happened on Thursday?

No, and that is the worrying part. It would be easier if two identical days meant one bug. These are two unrelated faults that happened to land back to back in the same unit.

What they share is the real lesson: this one unit has at least two single points of failure that can silently cost a whole sending day, and roughly 131 approved people have now paid for them in two days.

Q3What did the rest of the pipeline do while the send was down?

Everything else ran clean, quietly, and small. No fatal error signatures, no halt, and $0 of Anthropic spend.

The recovery lane added 19 contact points and 4 email addresses across 4 people, against 80 points and 14 addresses over the prior week. That is a picked-over book rather than a fault, and the measurements say so plainly: the newest collect pass resolved a website for 149 of 150 leads, made only 4 Brave searches, had zero search refusals and zero rejections from the ownership gate, and its methods found 579 contact points of which 577 were already on file. The lane is on its thirteenth lap of a 2,387-lead book. It is re-reading what it has already read, which is what a thirteenth lap looks like.

The shelf is in good shape: 5,148 people ready to write, 99.8% of the pool enriched, and the stranded cohort from the 10-13 push is down from 54 to 4, so that problem has closed itself as the 10-28 debrief predicted.

The enrichment chain was the one other thing genuinely broken, and it is now fixed. It launched 3 batches and failed all 3, every one on the same single lead, every one dying on a quota error from Supadata, a transcript provider Casey cancelled in August. Decodo replaced it as the primary months ago, but the old code treated an exhausted Supadata plan as an emergency worth killing the lead over, so that lead would have been retried forever. Details in the fixes table below.

Discovery stayed at zero, which is Casey's instruction since 2026-09-08 and not a fault. The YouTube key pool reads 15 working of 66, also expected: the quota on 50 of those projects was cut, and it does not matter while discovery is off.

Recommended next — ranked

  1. Make the sample and report steps unable to block the push. This is the whole lesson of two lost days. Fix the immediate crash by coercing non-string entries before the join in why_no_email(), which is a one-line change. Then fix the structural problem: the push is the only step of those five that sends anything, and it currently sits behind two steps that do not. Either move it ahead of the sample and report steps, or run them as separate units, or let them fail without taking the unit down. Thursday's fault and Friday's fault were unrelated, which says the next one will be too, and all three cost the same thing.
  2. Then the slot ceiling, which is the real limit on everything. 2,090 people are waiting for a slot against a plan that could only fit 69. The 870-message ramp is irrelevant while 48 of 58 mailboxes are stopped, and the fleet did not move once in twenty-four hours. Three of the held mailboxes are healthy and waiting out the recovery clock, the nearest 11 hours away, so they return for free. The other 45 do not. Nothing in the send path is worth tuning until this number changes, because even a perfect sending day reaches 69 people.
  3. Monday will try again on its own, and the 69 are not lost. Siege rebuilds the plan from scratch each morning, so Friday's 69 are still sitting on the shelf and will be planned again. No manual recovery is needed, and nothing should be re-pushed by hand. The cost of these two days was the delay, not the people.
  4. The Apify allowance resets tomorrow. The lane finished October with nothing spendable, sitting on its $10 reserve floor, so it ran no batches this cycle. The $100 calendar-month allowance refills on 1 November, which at the measured rate is roughly 700 recovered leads for about $90, plus the OpenRouter cost of enriching them.
