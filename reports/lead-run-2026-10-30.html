2026-10-30 — a Thursday sent nothing, and an empty API reply is why

Siege's first pass of the morning found 12 mailboxes clear to send and 62 free slots. Its second pass, four and a half minutes later, found zero, refused to push, and exited with an error. 62 approved people with verified addresses and finished emails got nothing. The fleet was fine. SmartLead handed back an empty list of campaigns, and one unguarded line turned that into a lost day.

The shape of the day

Each bar is one hourly band-gate reading (25 of them, 07:05Z to 07:05Z), and its height is how many mailboxes were clear to send. The day has no send event to plot, because there was no send. The fleet held flat at 12 clear for nine hours, lost three at 16:05Z (two moved to watch, three to stop), and sat at 9 for the rest of the cycle. Nothing came back. Worth noticing on its own: the fleet shrank on a day it sent nothing at all, so sending volume is not the only thing burning mailboxes.

The three questions

Q1Why did a normal Thursday send zero emails?

One SmartLead API call came back empty and nothing checked it. Siege plans twice each morning. The sample pass at 10:16:50Z read the fleet and wrote it down: 12 mailboxes LIVE, 62 free slots under an 870-message ramp, and named each one in the log. The live pass at 10:21:20Z, the one that actually pushes, read 0 LIVE. It then logged "62 approved people are no longer plannable", printed "REFUSING to push: nothing the approval covers is still plannable", and exited 1.

The plan file it left behind says exactly what happened. All 58 mailboxes carry campaign_id: null and the reason no Siege campaign yet, and twelve of them carry that as their only reason. Those twelve are the twelve that were live four and a half minutes earlier. For comparison, the plans for 10-23, 10-26, 10-27 and 10-28 each have zero mailboxes missing a campaign. The campaigns exist. The read of them failed.

The line is list_campaigns() in automator/scripts/smartlead_api.py. It returns [] for any answer it does not recognise, with no retry and no error raised. Directly below it, list_accounts() is carefully guarded against the same thing: it retries, it refuses to return a partial list, and it carries a comment explaining what an empty one would cost. The campaign list never got that guard, so one bad answer blanks the whole fleet in silence.

Q2Was it just a sick fleet? 48 of 58 mailboxes are stopped.

No, and that number is the trap. The band gate really does have 48 mailboxes stopped, 1 on watch, 9 clear, and on any other morning that is where you would look. It is the wrong answer here. The push did not fail because 48 mailboxes were sick. It failed because the 12 healthy ones were invisible to it, and those twelve are nowhere in the stopped count.

The difference matters because the two have opposite fixes. A sick fleet needs new domains, a gentler ramp, and two weeks of warmup. This needs a retry on one HTTP call. If the stopped count had been read as the cause, the day would have been filed as more of the slow deliverability decline and the real defect would still be live this morning.

The fleet is genuinely weak, and that is a separate, real problem: 9 clear of 58, down from 12, with 1,995 people ready and waiting behind roughly 135 messages a day. Three of the held mailboxes are healthy and waiting out the 48-hour recovery clock, so a little capacity returns for free. None of that caused this.

Q3Did the stranded-cohort repair from 10-28 hold?

Yes, and it is finished. The 54 people marked as emailed who had never been emailed are now 4. Fifty flew on 10-29 and the rest of the stamp drift stopped. The shelf reads 5,148 ready to write, 1,668 stamped, 1,664 really emailed, and the gap is small enough that it can no longer hide a cohort.

The recovery lane had an ordinary day underneath all this: 12 contact points, 1 new address, 8 people touched, which is almost exactly one day's share of the 87 points and 16 addresses it found over the prior week. It is on lap 13 of a 2,391-lead book, so most of what it reads it has read before. Site resolution is healthy (16.7% with no site, zero Brave refusals), and the largest fault in the pass is cross-domain redirect, which stopped 61 of 113 method attempts across five methods. That is one condition wearing five names, and it is the biggest single thing standing between the lane and more addresses.

Recommended next — ranked

  1. Guard list_campaigns(), today, before the next push. It is the shortest high-value fix in the pipeline right now: give it the same treatment list_accounts() already has twenty lines below it. Retry an empty or unrecognised answer a few times, and raise rather than return [] if it stays empty. siege-plan.py should also refuse to write a plan in which every mailbox lost its campaign, because that is not a state the fleet can actually be in. This lives in automator, which is the send path and outside the five repos this agent may edit, so it is reported and not shipped. The next push runs in a few hours and will fail the same way if SmartLead answers badly again.
  2. Re-plan the 62 people who were approved and dropped. They were approved, had verified addresses, finished subjects and complete bundles, and the refusal was not about them. Nothing re-queues them automatically: Siege builds each morning's plan fresh, so they go back in the general pool and wait their turn by tier. With 1,995 people waiting and roughly 135 messages a day of capacity, "wait their turn" is a couple of weeks.
  3. Ask why the fleet lost three mailboxes on a day it sent nothing. The standing explanation for shrinkage has been that a big push burns inboxes. This cycle pushed zero emails and still went 12 clear to 9, with two more sliding to watch. Warmup traffic alone is now enough to stop a mailbox, which means new domains buy less than the arithmetic suggests. Worth one look at the spam readings behind those three before buying more.
  4. The Apify allowance resets tomorrow. $100 comes back on 10-31, which is roughly 700 recovered leads at $0.115 each, with about $100 of OpenRouter enrichment behind them against a $60.47 balance. It is the single biggest input the pipeline gets this month, and it front-loads, so the first days of November matter more than the rest.
  5. Decide on cross-domain redirect in the collect lane. It stopped 61 of 113 method attempts, more than half the pass, under five different method names. Some of those redirects are ordinary (a site moving to a new domain, or a page served from a host like Squarespace), and some are the ownership gate correctly refusing to file a stranger's details. Telling them apart is a real piece of work, and loosening an identity gate on a hunch is the one change that quietly poisons the lead data. Worth a decision, not a guess.
