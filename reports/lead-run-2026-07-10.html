The three questions

Q1Why did today suck so much?

One dominant cause: term-starvation. The finder only mines active search terms by priority. By mid-day the entire active pool had decayed to negative priority — every mineable term was already exhausted — while the good supply (274 never-run terms + all fresh discovery probes) sat paused and invisible to the finder. Result: 17 consecutive passes at 0 fresh leads. That dead stretch is most of the gap to a normal day.

Aggravators: (2) supply started thinner — yesterday's +480 drained the fresh proven terms; (3) frontier niches verify at ~38% vs 47%, so more leads went to needs_contact (+180) than approved_hold (+124); (4) a mid-day network blip (ENOTFOUND) hard-crashed the run because the driver wasn't resilient to DNS errors; (5) 10 restarts added pre-flight overhead.

Q2Why did it burn way more YouTube API than usual?

Same root cause, seen from the cost side. A saturated term isn't cheap to discover it's saturated — the finder still pays the full quota for it (a search call + channel-detail fetches for ~50 candidates) and only then finds every candidate is a dupe or hard-filtered. A representative pass: seen 400 · filtered 318 · dup 81 · new 1.

Across the day that's 86,993 channels fetched → 2,662 new (3.1%) — ~97% of the API spend bought nothing, ~700 fetches per parked lead. Amplifiers stacked on top: --concurrent 2 doubled the burn rate, 43 discovery cycles each ran extra validation searches, and 10 restarts re-ran the pre-flight each time. The safety-net RapidAPI tier hit 99.9% of its daily request cap unattended.

Q3Why did it find way fewer leads than usual?

Because the fresh-lead engine was starved of minable terms — not because the channel universe ran out. Two proofs it was starvation, not true exhaustion:

① The instant it was unblocked, yield returned. Manually reactivating the 274 never-run paused terms produced 26 fresh pitchable on the very next pass (then 8, 18, 5, 12) — straight back to healthy levels. ② The finder kept seeing 322–400 channels every pass — they were just already-known for the exhausted terms it was forced to mine. Fresh terms existed but were administratively locked out of the active pool (probes auto-pause; winners only promote at run-end; the 274 sat paused).

Recommended next — ranked

  1. Anti-starvation guard in the finder (highest leverage, cheap). When the top active priority drops to ≤0, auto-reactivate the best never-run paused terms (or promote paused probe winners) before selecting a pass. This alone would have prevented today's 17 dead passes.
  2. Cheap saturation detection. Spot overlap_warning from the search result set before paying for 50 channel-detail fetches — bail a term early once its candidates are mostly known IDs. Cuts the API-burn-for-nothing directly.
  3. Promote probe winners mid-run, not just at campaign end — run evaluate-probes every few fades so converting veins re-enter the active pool the same session.
  4. Per-session YouTube-quota governor in the campaign — stop launching concurrent passes once RapidAPI search crosses ~80%, so a long run can't drain the day's quota unattended (today hit 99.9%).
  5. The needs_contact recovery engine (biggest volume lever, deferred). 2,353 found-and-scored creators with no verified email. Recovering ~30% (~700) dwarfs a day of fresh finding and sidesteps the whole term-supply ceiling. Awaiting greenlight.
