The three questions

Q1Wednesday reached 313 people. Did the turn hold, and what set today's size?

It held, and the size was slots. Nothing changed on the supply side and almost nothing changed in the fleet. One extra mailbox went live, capacity rose by 15 messages, and the number of slots the plan could offer fell from 360 to 208. Emails tracked slots, and new people tracked emails.

The repeat-offer queue shrank again, which settles yesterday's finding for a third time. Siege hands slots out by tier, top tier first. Yesterday the top two tiers wanted 29 of 360 slots. Today they wanted 11 of 208, and 197 went to the bottom tier where the never-emailed people live. Only 8 of the day's 199 emails were repeat offers, down from 38. So the re-offers are a small daily cost that does not grow with capacity, and they are not competing with the shelf for anything.

The reason there were 208 slots and not 360 is Wednesday's own bill arriving. Every offer is a three-email sequence on days 0, 4 and 10, so a big first-touch day mortgages the following week. Follow-ups owed went from 47 to 100 in one day, and the day-4 bill for Wednesday's 352 emails lands on Monday 2026-11-09, which is also the day the 36 warmup mailboxes clear. Monday is the biggest capacity gain and the biggest follow-up bill of the run, at the same time.

The shelf agrees with itself to the person. ready_to_write went 5,299 to 5,127, down 172, which is the 173 first touches that came off approved_hold less the single person parked during the cycle. People with a real send row went 2,058 to 2,231, up exactly 173.

Q2Seven mailboxes came off suspension. Did the fleet get better?

Checked by joining the two plan files: not one of the 90 mailboxes suspended on Wednesday was live on Thursday. Six of the seven that stopped naming suspension now name the unreadable-status row instead, and the seventh is now a health stop at a score of 46.8. Nothing joined the suspended row either. Q3One person was parked. How long does the shelf last?

About three to four weeks, and nothing is refilling it. approved_hold went 7,371 to 7,372. Both recovery lanes have run out of people to work on, which is different from being broken and cannot be fixed with money.

  • The paid lane has now refused for want of candidates on 47 consecutive ticks, since 2026-11-02, with $31.20 of November's allowance unspent and $21.20 of it spendable. Its own projection reads a measured 0 leads with candidate_supply as the binding constraint.
  • The free lane found 2 people an address from 13 contact points, against 23 addresses for the prior week. Its newest judged pass walked 150 leads, found 96 contact methods, and all 96 were already on file.
  • Enrichment ran 1 batch for 1 lead, because 99.8% of the pool is already bundled. There is nothing left to enrich.

So the shelf is a fixed 5,127 people ready to write, draining at between 173 and 275 a day depending on the fleet. If Monday's warmup cohort lands it drains faster.

One thing in the free lane looks alarming and is not. Website resolution went from 2 leads of 150 with no site on Wednesday to 140 of 150 today, with zero Brave refusals. Brave answered every search and returned 125 candidate sites; the ownership gate threw out 115 of them because they could not be tied to that creator. That gate is deliberate, the cause field names it, and no amount of search credit changes it. It is a different slice of the same lap 14 walk over a picked-over book.
