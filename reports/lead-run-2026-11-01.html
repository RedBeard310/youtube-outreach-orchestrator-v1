The shape of the day

nothing parked, 17 hours four paid batches: 72, 62, 61, 55 people

One bar per hour of Saturday in Pacific time, and its height is how many people were parked in that hour. The day did nothing at all until 17:22, then parked 250 people in seven hours. That is the calendar, not luck: the Apify allowance is a UTC calendar month, so November opened at 17:00 Pacific and the lane started spending immediately. Read the four bars left to right and they shrink: 72, 62, 61, 55. Same 100 channels scraped each time, fewer published addresses in each batch.

The three questions

Q1Where did 250 people come from on a day that found no channels and sent no email?

From money, not from finding. The paid Apify lane ran four batches at 00:22Z, 02:20Z, 04:21Z and 06:22Z, scraping 100 already-known channels each time for the business email YouTube publishes on the channel page. It found 339 addresses in 400 channels, ZeroBounce verified them, and flipped 250 into approved_hold at $0.112 each. Two separate instruments agree on the number: the lead table gained exactly 250 and the lane's own ledger claims exactly 250.

For scale, that one night beat the whole of October, when this lane recovered 244 people for $28. The allowance front-loads by design, so this is the lane working as intended rather than a surprise.

The free half of the recovery lane, which crawls creators' own websites, found 2 people in the same 24 hours. That is not a fault and the measurements say so plainly: the newest 150-lead pass resolved a website for 148 of 150 leads, made 4 web searches with zero refusals, and its methods turned up 541 contact points of which 531 were already on file. It is on its thirteenth lap of a 2,119-lead book, re-reading what it has already read. The paid lane produced 125 times as many people on the same night, which is worth knowing before anyone tunes the free one.

Q2Can the pipeline afford what it just bought?

Not past Tuesday, on today's balance. A recovered address is not yet a person anyone can write to. The email is written from the enrichment bundle on disk, so every parked person needs the enrichment chain to research their channel first, and that step is the one that costs OpenRouter credit. The chain did its part the same night: 6 batches, 193 leads enriched, 6 failed, which is why the shelf of people ready to write grew to 5,341 and the pool is still 99% bundled.

So about 19 cents buys a verified, researched, writable person, which is cheap. The only thing wrong is that one of the two budgets is funded for a month and the other for two and a half days, and the second one is $7 short of the first one's output before a single new batch runs.

Two things make that less alarming than it reads. First, running out is a stop and not a loss: the chain checks the balance before every batch and holds at the gate, so leads stay in the pool and nothing pays for YouTube and transcripts only to die at the writing stage. That was fixed on 2026-09-15 after it happened. Second, the $50.16 is an overestimate, because it assumes the lane keeps recovering 0.625 people per channel and the four batches went 0.72, 0.62, 0.61, 0.55 in one night. If that keeps sliding, cost per person crosses the lane's own $0.20 ceiling in roughly six more batches and the lane stops itself for the month. Four batches is a short series, so treat the slope as a thing to watch rather than a forecast.

Q3What else moved, and what is waiting for Monday?

No send, correctly. Saturday is not a sending day, and the snapshot says exactly that rather than inventing a fault. The real test is Monday: it is the first sending day after two consecutive zero-email weekdays, 10-29 and 10-30, and both repairs live in automator, which this loop may not edit. Neither has shipped. Friday's 69 people are still on the shelf and Siege rebuilds its plan from scratch each morning, so nothing needs re-pushing by hand.

The fleet improved slightly, and this time it held. Mailboxes clear to send went 10 to 12 of 58, with three handed the clear band at 05:05Z and all three still clear at the final reading, zero round trips. That is the first clean recovery since the band gate took over: on 10-27 six came back and five were re-stopped within hours. Only 1 of the 45 held mailboxes is healthy enough to lift itself, an hour from its clock expiring, so capacity does not return on its own from here.

The stranded cohort is down to 4 people marked sent who were never emailed, from 54 on 10-28. That problem has closed itself.

Quiet elsewhere: $0 of Anthropic spend, no fatal error signatures, no halt flag, 6 of 199 enrichment leads failed individually and the chain quarantined each repeat offender instead of looping on it. Discovery stayed at zero, which is Casey's instruction since 2026-09-08. The YouTube key pool reads 15 working of 66, also expected and irrelevant while discovery is off.

FIXED Nothing watched the money between midnights. The balance that pays for enrichment was read once a day, by the debrief, at 00:20. That is fine for a number that moves 5% a day and useless for one that moved tenfold in a single cycle: the 10-31 debrief read 38 days of runway and was right, and one day later the same arithmetic reads 2.7. The next daily reading would have been the last one before the credit ran out, and the only other signal is the hourly check that reports the chain after it has already stalled. The hourly check-in now reads the live balance and writes an openrouter_runway_low line. Three tiers, each needing only what it can measure: under three days of runway, under the $5 the chain's own gate needs, or empty. It names the balance, the burn, and whether the unspent Apify allowance has already outgrown the credit. Costs one free HTTPS call. On 10-31 it held itself at a transcript gate from 04:52Z to 08:02Z, three hours of the only lane still producing, logging "supadata limit still active" each time.
