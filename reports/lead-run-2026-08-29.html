The three questions

Q1The video-graph lane produced 45 good leads on Thursday and 8 today. What happened to it?

It was switched off by its own spending cap at 10:31Z yesterday, and it has not run since. The cap is $50, and unlike every other limit in the pipeline it counts lifetime dollars, not dollars per run or per day. The lane crossed it, printed one line, and exited with the success code. Its relaunch loop reads that code as "the book is finished" and stops for good.

After that, something restarted the service once an hour for 21 hours. Each restart re-read the cap, printed the same line, and quit in about two seconds. 1,424 seeds are still unwalked in its book.

Nothing caught it, and the reason is worth stating plainly: a lane that starts and quits immediately still rewrites its state file. So every freshness check read green. The state file was zero hours old. The daily health record said productive: true, because the lane had worked earlier in the same 24-hour window. The one honest field, a count of consecutive runs that walked nothing, read 23 and sat in a file nobody looks at until the next morning.

The cap is the wrong shape for the job. It measures the lane's age, not its efficiency. At $50 for 1,877 good leads that lane costs 2.7¢ per lead, which is the cheapest in the pipeline. The sibling feed lane caps per lap instead, so its budget refreshes every time it starts a new pass and it can never lock itself out this way.

Q2Yesterday's debrief said the feed lane runs at 0.0078 good leads per seed. Is that still true?

No, on two counts, and one of them was a measurement bug.

First, the honest one. That 0.0078 was a reading taken mid-lap. Lap 6 has since finished, and the sweep's own counter closes it at 0.014 per seed, 167 good leads from 12,180 seeds at 3.4¢ each. A lap's yield isn't flat across the lap, so a half-finished lap is not the lap's rate. It nearly doubled in its second half.

Second, today's number in the raw data was simply wrong. It said the lane walked 13,199 seeds. It walked 6,019. The seed counter falls back to summing session logs whenever it can't difference two daily snapshots, and a lap rollover is exactly the case where the snapshot difference goes negative. That fallback over-counts a long daemon session, which is a bug we already fixed once in August for a different lane and did not fix here.

Corrected, the feed lane ran at 0.0193 per seed today, which is better than video-graph's 0.0161 and close to peer network's 0.0231. Yesterday's finding that the feed lane is 2.4 times less efficient per seed than video-graph was right for yesterday and does not hold today. The three graph lanes now cost within a penny of each other per lead.

This is fixed. The rollover case is computed exactly now, from the two numbers already sitting in yesterday's snapshot, and the source of every seed count is named so a fallback can never pass itself off as a measurement again.

Q3Why did the last five hours of the cycle produce almost nothing?

The feed lane spent them finishing a lap three seeds at a time. Lap 6 ended just before midnight with a dozen seeds left. Every hour after that, the refill job added the two or three channels our own scoring had newly qualified, restarted the walker to go and get them, and the walker walked them and stopped.

Measured: 232, then 17, then 4, then 3 seeds across four hourly restarts between 00:11Z and 04:14Z. Roughly 23 seeds in four hours, against a normal 250 an hour. It only escaped at 05:11Z, when the trickle happened to be empty, because the rule that starts a new lap only fires on the words "nothing to add".

Yesterday's debrief saw a one-hour version of this and called it minor. At five hours it is worth about 1,250 seeds and 24 good leads, and it recurs at every lap boundary, which is every two days or so.

Fixed. A trickle now counts as a finished lap: anything under 50 fresh seeds starts the next lap instead of restarting the walker for a handful. Nothing is lost, because starting a lap re-opens the whole book including the trickle. The cooldown and the cost-per-lead check that decide whether a new lap is worth running are untouched.
