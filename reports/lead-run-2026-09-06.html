The three questions

Q1Where did the 151 parked leads actually come from?

Not from finding new channels. From working the creators we already had. Of the 2,737 channels discovered today, 103 scored well enough to pitch, and 22 of those have a verified email so far. The other 129 parks came from the two recovery lanes emptying the needs_contact pile: the Apify email scraper flipped 70 and the Bloodhound lane's own verify pass flipped 21, with the rest arriving from leads found on earlier days that finished verification today.

The pool of creators we found but never contacted is 4,243, down 109 even after taking 20 new ones in. That is the second fall on record and much the bigger of the two. Recovery is now clearly outproducing discovery, and it costs no YouTube quota at all.

Not verified yet: 61 of today's 103 good leads are still waiting on the verify lane, so today's 21% verified rate is a snapshot of a queue, not a conversion rate. On the leads that have been ruled on, 22 of 42 got a usable email, which is the usual ~52%.

Q2Why did the recovery lane's brand-new timer make it worse?

The contact-collection pass runs for about 46 minutes, so it is launched as a background job and the launcher exits straight away. That was safe while the only thing launching it was the always-running campaign service. It stopped being safe yesterday, when the lane was given its own timer.

A timer job is a short-lived unit, and when a short-lived unit finishes, Linux kills everything it left running. Being launched in the background does not save the job, because the killing is done by group and the job is still in the group. Measured on the two scheduled passes: 6 of 150 leads reached the log on the first, 13 of 150 on the second, and both children died about 30 seconds in. The lane's bookmark had already moved past all 300, so 281 leads were quietly marked as walked without anybody looking at them.

Both halves are fixed and checked on live runs. The unit now keeps its background job alive when it exits, proven with a controlled before-and-after test (0 survivors before, 1 after). The lane also stopped trusting its own dispatch: it remembers where each batch started, reads the collect log for the pass's completion line, and puts the bookmark back if the line never arrived. The bookmark was rewound by hand over today's damage, and a full 150-lead pass is running as this is written, resolving websites for 6 of every 8 leads.

Q3The Apify email scraper is the best producer we have. How long can it run?

About three more days on the current month's money. It went on a two-hourly timer overnight and has run four batches of 100 channels at $7.02 each, so $28 of the $100 monthly allowance is gone since midnight and $53 is left after the $10 reserve. At this cadence it stops on its own in the middle of Tuesday and stays stopped until the billing month resets.

And the returns are falling batch by batch, which changes the sums. The four batches found an email on 89, then 75, then 67, then 54 channels out of 100, and turned into 11, 35, 24 and 22 parked leads. Cost per recovered lead ran $0.638 · $0.201 · $0.292 · $0.319. The pilot number in the standing orders, 92% of channels returning an email and 59% becoming a usable lead, came from the first 52 channels of the best-ordered queue. The honest planning number after 400 channels is roughly 25 parked leads per 100 channels at about $0.30 each, and it is still drifting down.

$0.30 a lead is three times the $0.10 line, and still the cheapest recovery we have for creators whose address is hidden behind YouTube's "View email address" button. That is a spend judgement, not an engineering one.

Recommended next — ranked

  1. Decide what the Apify scraper is allowed to spend this month. It stops by itself around Tuesday midday, quietly and by design, and nothing will page you when it does. At about 25 parked leads per $7 batch it is buying leads at $0.30 each and getting slowly worse. Raising the monthly cap is the single biggest volume lever available right now; leaving it is a defensible answer too, but it should be a decision rather than a surprise.
  2. Stop paying keyword search for nothing. 252 finder passes produced 11 good leads, and each search costs 100 quota units while the graph sweeps run on 1-unit calls. The sweeps ran out of key pool at 06:31 and the keyword lane is where most of that pool went. Capping the lane's passes per session, or skipping it entirely while its fresh-term reserve is empty, hands the sweeps back most of a day's quota. This is a lane-priority change, so it is your call, not the autopilot's.
  3. Checkpoint the video-graph sweep mid-chunk. Its own stop line says the chunk is not saved and the next run re-walks it. The lane walked 1,900 seeds today against 387 yesterday and has 217 left, so it is about to need a new book anyway; the re-walk is pure waste on every quota stop.
  4. Top up OpenRouter. Balance $158.56 against $21.67 a day of account spend is 7.3 days. Most of that is the enrichment backfill, which keeps no spend log of its own, so the account meter is the only honest number here.
  5. Give the recovery lane a second collect pass a day. It is due every 6 hours but only two of today's four scheduled passes even ran, both of them killed. With the kill fixed, the honest measurement of what four full passes a day produce starts tomorrow, and that is the number to size the next widening on.
