The three questions

Q1Reach went from 9 people to 313 in a day. What actually changed?

Twenty mailboxes started sending, and every one of them had been sitting on the same single block. All 20 carried the reason no Siege campaign yet in the day before's plan, 19 of them with nothing else against them. Casey shipped the automation that fixes that at 00:04Z on 11-04 (automator 197e81c, "qualified inboxes join on their own"), it attached 18 campaigns inside the next fifteen minutes and 2 more during the push, and this is the first plan written after that.

And the second half of the answer is why the new people got the slots instead of the repeat offers. Siege hands slots out by tier, top tier first, and the top tiers have a finite number of re-offers to make on any given day. Yesterday there were 67 slots and the top two tiers took 67 of them. Today there were 360, the top two tiers wanted 29, and the remaining 331 fell to the bottom tier, which is where the never-emailed people on the shelf live.

So the queue was never a policy that crowded new people out. It was a fixed cost that happened to be larger than the whole day's capacity. Give the day three hundred extra slots and the re-offers do not grow to fill them. That settles the open question from the last three debriefs about whether repeat offers are worth their slots: at this fleet size the question does not arise, because they are not competing for anything.

The shelf agrees with itself, which is worth more than either number alone. ready_to_write went 5,574 to 5,299, exactly 275 fewer, and 275 is the count of first touches that came off approved_hold. People with a send row went 1,783 to 2,058, also exactly 275.

Q2Yesterday this report said the warmup list was the number one problem. Was it?

No, and the mistake was in the instrument. Yesterday's top recommendation was to find out why 30 mailboxes had dropped out of the InboxKit warmup list, on the grounds that each one is refused while its age is unknown, so "it is 24 slots the day lost to a missing record." Checked properly this morning against the very plan file that report was reading: not one of those 30 mailboxes was held by the warmup list alone. Every one of them was also suspended in SmartLead, or still on a warmup timer, or both. Repairing the warmup sync would have freed nobody.

The rows the report reads carry a count of how many mailboxes name each reason. A mailbox that is already blocked picks up every other reason that applies to it, so a count can climb for a row that costs nothing. This exact trap was written down on 10-29, when the fix was to read how many mailboxes carried the missing-campaign reason alone rather than how many carried it. When the per-reason rows replaced that pair of fields on 11-03, the rule did not come with them.

Q3Nobody was parked. Is the supply side broken?

No. It has run out of people to work on, which is a different thing and cannot be bought. approved_hold finished the cycle exactly where it started, at 7,371, for the first time since the recovery lane shipped.

The paid lane has been dry for 35 consecutive ticks, since 2026-11-02, with $31.20 of November's $100 allowance unspent and only $21.20 of that spendable. It answered "no qualified lead without an email that this lane has not already tried" every time, scraped nothing and cost nothing. It would afford 301 more channels. There are no channels. More allowance buys nothing.
The free lane is picked over, not faulty. Lap 14 of an 1,803-lead book. The newest pass walked 150 leads, found 612 contact points and 606 of them were already on file, which is what a complete re-walk looks like. One lead gained an address, against 24 for the prior week. Two leads in 150 had no website, Brave refused nothing, and the cause reads resolution_ok.
Enrichment is done. Zero batches and zero leads, because 99.8% of the pool is already bundled and there is nothing left to enrich. That is the finish line, not a stall.

So the inventory is now fixed at 5,299 researched people ready to write to, and it is draining for the first time. At yesterday's 275 a day that is about four weeks. At the 36 warmup mailboxes arriving on 11-09 it is faster than that.

Which makes the supply decision real rather than theoretical. It was recommendation four on 11-02 and nothing has needed it since, because reach was stuck at single digits and the shelf was not moving. The shelf is moving now. Lifting the 2026-09-08 discovery pause is Casey's call and nobody else's.
