The shape of the day

This was a working day, which is worth saying first because the last two were not. The send program started, got through all six of its steps, wrote a plan for 288 people and pushed. No crash, no missing settings file, no approval wait. It reported success and the hourly health check raised nothing: zero fatal errors all cycle, no halt, $0 of Anthropic spend.

So the day has to be judged on what the emails did rather than on whether they went, and on that it looks like the days before it. 244 people were written to and one of them had never been contacted before. The other 243 were repeat offers to people already in the book, which is deliberate since 2026-10-07 and is not a fault. It is also the eleventh single-digit day in a row.

The three questions

Q1Why did 40 of the 288 planned people get no email?

Twelve bounced: SmartLead already had a failed delivery on file for the address and refused to take the lead. Three were people who had unsubscribed. One had no clean job title, so the writer produced nothing. One was held by the approval gate for the next morning, which is not a loss. That accounted for 17 of them, and 21 were filed as lost_silently, a field whose entire meaning is that the log never mentioned those people.

The log mentioned twelve of them. Ten carried one line each saying the writer had tried every offer it could and had none left: 

recX7aWDreNdF9T5m: love-your-channel-short wrote nothing and there is no
  other offer for them today (every offer that can run has already been
  made to them)

That is the single largest loss cause of the day. It is bigger than the twelve bounces that led the published table, and it was in no field anywhere. The other two were leads whose YouTube channel no longer exists, printing a verdict a reader of this file could not see. Both gaps are now closed, and the day really reads: 12 bounces, 10 people we have nothing left to say to, 3 unsubscribes, 2 dead channels, 1 with no job title, 1 manifest miss, and 9 genuinely unexplained.

Q2Is the loss growing, or is it the same people every day?

The same people. 32 of the 40 had already been lost on an earlier day, 8 were new, and the worst of them has now been lost on ten consecutive sending days. The split is complete this time, covering all 40, so that is the whole picture rather than a capped sample.

Part of this is already healing. A fix shipped two days ago makes the push write a bounce down on the lead row, which takes that address out of circulation, and it is working: all three bounced leads checked in the database now read email_bounced = true and invalid, stamped yesterday. The twelve-person bounce group should drain from here.

The two dead-channel leads will not drain. Their channels are gone from YouTube, the research step correctly refuses to spend anything on them, and nothing marks them so the plan will keep naming them every day forever. That gate is in the send program itself, which this pipeline does not own.

Q3Did anything new get into the pipeline?

Yes, in the one lane that is allowed to produce. Discovery of new channels stays off by Casey's order from 2026-09-08, so 0 new channels is the intended reading and not a fault. The contact-recovery lane, which goes looking for addresses for people we already have, had its best day in weeks: 8 new email addresses on 8 people, against 4 addresses in the whole prior week. Every one came from the domain-registration method, which has been quiet for most of a month.

Nobody was parked onto the shelf, though, so those 8 have not become writable emails yet. The shelf itself holds 4,852 finished emails nobody has asked for, and 1,330 people are waiting for a sending slot. Bundling sits at 99.8%, so the enrichment chain correctly reports itself idle for want of new work rather than stuck.

The fleet

35 mailboxes clear to send, down from 39. Four left the clear band and none came back. The gate ran every hour with no partial readings, so that is a real move and not an instrument artefact, and the day's peak equals yesterday's reading, which means there was no rise-and-fall hidden inside it either.

12 of the held mailboxes are healthy and will lift themselves without anyone touching a setting: 8 are waiting out a recovery clock with the nearest one 4 hours from clearing, and 4 are held only because other addresses on their domain are sick. That is capacity returning for free.

At plan time only 38 of the 124 mailboxes were live. The most common reason, named by 69 of the 86 that sat out, is a SmartLead suspension, and 39 of those carry it as their only reason, which is the figure that means real lost slots. The plan still wrote 288 slots against a 570-message capacity, because 282 of that capacity is reserved for follow-ups already owed to the 541 people mid-sequence.

Fixes shipped this cycle

The reader stopped matching labels, because labels change

Both of the checks that explain a loss before the push were pinned to the exact wording one log happened to use on 2026-11-06. One of them went blind when a single word moved: the summary line for a lead is its final status in capitals, and a lead with a deleted YouTube channel stopped reporting failed once it got a status of its own called channel_gone. The same lead the old comment names by name was silently unreadable.

So the status is read off the line now and judged against a short list of the four statuses that mean the lead is fine. A status nobody has seen before gets a row naming itself instead of disappearing, which is safe because this only ever speaks for people already known to be lost. The next relabelling cannot blind it.

The second fix reads the writer running out of offers for one person, taking care to ignore the near-identical line it prints when it still has an offer left to try.

Verified against the real log and plan: unexplained losses 21 to 9, explained-before-the-push 1 to 13. Four new test assertions, full suite passing, type check clean. Committed in youtube-outreach-orchestrator-v1 as 2ea45b1 (the two-minute sync timer claimed the commit before the agent could, which is normal).
