The three questions

Q1Did yesterday's self-restart fix actually hold?

Yes, and this cycle is the proof. Yesterday the pipeline sat switched off for 22 hours after the thing that stopped it had already repaired itself, because stopping was automatic and starting was not. The fix made the restart automatic too, for causes it can positively check.

It fired at 07:24:03Z, right at the start of this cycle. It read the stop note, counted 66 YouTube keys in the shared file, deleted the note and restarted the campaign loop, which launched its first session one second later. From that point the pipeline ran continuously: 17 campaign sessions, 565 finder runs, all five lanes live, zero hours stopped.

Nothing crashed and nothing cost you anything in Claude credit. Zero fatal error signatures, zero quota stops, and $0.00 of Anthropic spend, because the hourly health check found nothing that needed a repair agent. Three finder runs hit a hard wall and the loop shrugged them off, which is what it is built to do.

The health checks did keep talking, they just did it for free. Across the cycle they logged 25 notes about the video-graph lane walking nothing, 20 about the finder running out of search terms, and 8 automatic attempts to go fetch more terms. All observations, no paid agent, which is the design working.

Q2Where is the supply actually coming from, and how much is left?

Almost two thirds of today came from one lane, and that lane is re-reading a list it has already read seven times. The recommended-videos lane found 81 of the 127. It works by taking a channel we already like and following YouTube's "watch next" rail off its videos, so its raw material is our own past output.

Yesterday morning it finished its seventh full pass over that list of 12,307 seeds and immediately opened an eighth. The passes are getting thinner every time. Pass one returned 0.45 good leads per seed. Pass seven returned 0.007, which is about one and a half percent of where it started. It walked 10,717 seeds today for those 81 leads and has 2,538 left, roughly six hours, at which point it will start a ninth pass over the same ground.

It keeps going because it is still cheap, and that is a deliberate rule rather than an oversight. Pass seven cost 5.0¢ per good lead. The rule that decides whether to start another pass is a price ceiling of 25¢, not a yield floor, and it is set that way on purpose: an earlier pass looked dead on yield while being the cheapest source in the whole pipeline. So the lane will keep re-lapping for a while yet. Cheap is not the same as growing, though. It is finding fewer new creators every pass, and it cannot find any creator that is not already adjacent to something we own.

The other four lanes are, between them, contributing very little. Keyword search found 44, and it did that on three active search terms, having exhausted every fresh, never-run and cooled-off term it has. Its hit rate was actually the best of any lane at 5.4%, so the terms are not bad, there just are not any. The peer-network lane has now walked every seed it has and refills at about 16 seeds every two hours. Podcast crossover ran once and found nothing. Comment sweep is off because you paused it.

Cost per good lead, from the finder's own log: keyword search 2.4¢, peer network 2.4¢, recommended videos 4.1¢. All three are cheap. The constraint is not money, it is that only one of them has any material left to work through.

Q3Are these actually the kind of businesses you sell to?

A meaningful share of them are not, and this report has never said so. Of the 127 creators scored good enough to contact today, 22 are tarot readers, astrologers, psychics or channellers. That is 17% of the day.

They are not a fluke of today either. Going back two weeks, the daily share runs between 8% and 21%, and across those two weeks it is 352 of 2,790 good leads, or roughly one in eight. It has never appeared in a debrief because no report has ever counted it.

Half of them are not even sitting in an obvious bucket. Twelve landed in the catch-all category, which is where you would look for them. But eight were filed under Coaching & Consulting, which is half of that category for the day, and two more under Transformation & Performance Coaching. On a category report they look like exactly the kind of lead you want.

Why the scorer lets them through is defensible. A tarot reader with 20,000 subscribers really does sell a service to individuals, really does book calls, and really is trying to turn views into clients. The scorer is asking "is this a service business that could hire a YouTube strategist," and by that test they pass. The test you actually sell against is high-ticket business-to-business: lawyers, financial advisors, agencies, clinics, consultants. That is a narrower question and nothing in the pipeline asks it.

They have been accumulating. 351 of the 3,860 creators in approved_hold and 334 of the 4,980 in needs_contact match, so about 685 of the 8,840 you have found and never contacted. The share in the standing pools (7.8%) is lower than the share arriving daily (12.6%), which means it is getting worse, not settling.

One caveat on these numbers, stated plainly: they come from matching words like tarot, astrology, oracle, psychic and numerology against each channel's name and description. That will catch a few false positives and will miss anything that avoids those words. Treat 17% as close, not exact. Nothing has been excluded or changed, because your standing instruction is to filter liberally and to ask before adding a new exclusion category rather than assume one. This is the asking.
