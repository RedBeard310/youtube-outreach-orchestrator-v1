The three questions

Q1Did yesterday's fix work?

Completely, and the shape of the evidence is what makes it convincing rather than just encouraging.

The prediction was deliberately falsifiable: widening the collect book puts leads that already have a website stored at the front of each lap, the collector reads that site back instead of re-buying it, so yield should climb while Brave stays dead. Brave stayed dead, refusing on all four passes. The no-website rate walked down across the day as the cursor reached the reused-website tier: 91% → 74% → 88% → 13%.

The last pass collected 771 contact points from 145 of 150 leads. The best pass of the fully-funded era was 885 points off 148 leads, so the lane is back at roughly its old ceiling while buying nothing.

The repetition measure confirms it from the other side: 516 lead-slots on 516 distinct leads, a ratio of 1.00, against 253 over 903 yesterday. Every slot went to a lead the lane had not seen.

Q2Then why did the lane only park 6?

Because a collected contact point is not an email, and a verified email is not yet a parked lead. Both gaps are large, and the second one is new.

958 points came off 183 leads, but only 94 of them are email addresses. The rest are social profiles (365), websites (299), phone numbers (81) and domain records. Useful for a human, useless to a lane whose only exit is a verified email. 66 of the 183 leads yielded an email at all, and 40 of those verified good, a 61% verify rate that is perfectly healthy.

Then 34 of those 40 stopped dead. They are still sitting in needs_contact with a good, verified email attached. Checked against the database: not one of the 34 has an old signal score of 6 or more, and all 6 that did park do. That is the v2 hold gate working exactly as written. Casey's rule is that a lead enters approved_hold at old score 6+, or v2 7+ once the score has been re-run with the verified email. Nothing in the lane runs that re-score, so a lead that arrives under the old bar recovers its email and then stops one cheap step short of the finish.

The 18 pilot leads that parked today came through that same door, and they prove the route works: they also score under 6 on the old measure, and they parked because somebody ran the v2 re-score on them by hand.

The blocking step is free, and every one of the 34 would clear the gate. Checked in the database rather than assumed: all 34 already carry a v2 score, so the paid classification stage has already run on them. 16 sit at v2 8, 10 at v2 7, 8 at v2 6, and every one has its contact component at 0. Re-running rescore-v2.py --stage assemble is pure SQL with no model call, it adds the one contact point each lead has now earned, and that takes them to 9, 8 and 7 against a gate that wants 7. 34 of 34.

Q3Where did 342 new needs_contact leads come from with discovery off?

From re-scoring leads we already own, not from finding anyone. This is the 09-13 standing order landing, and it is worth stating plainly because a rising pool with discovery paused looks, at a glance, exactly like discovery having restarted itself.

5,395 channels with 10k+ subscribers had never been given a Signal Score v2, because the re-scoring script only looked at leads already at old score 6+. Casey's widened gate went live in both repos this morning: a lead may be worked for a contact at old score 6+ or v2 6+. That moved 342 already-owned leads into the lane's reach.

Checked rather than assumed: every arrival was first discovered between May and September, most of them in July and August. No channel was discovered, no sweep ran, no timer was touched.

It also resets a clock from yesterday. The lane was described as having about five days of book left with nothing replenishing it. It now holds 3,311 leads, and the refill source has barely started, since only the finance and coaching slices have been scored.

A condition that will refuse every lead in a batch identically should be read before the first one costs anything.
