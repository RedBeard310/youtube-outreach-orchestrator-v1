The three questions

Q1What happened to the mailbox fleet?

Nothing happened to the mailboxes. Something happened to the instrument.

The health gate runs every hour. It asks the warmup service for every live warmup subscription, matches those to sending accounts, and gives each one a band: clear to send, on watch, or stopped. At 14:05 UTC on Sunday that list came back with 28 subscriptions instead of 94, and it has come back with 28 on every one of the nineteen hourly runs since.

A mailbox with no warmup record gets no band at all. So the bands this morning describe 28 mailboxes, and yesterday's described 94, and the report subtracted one from the other: clear 43 to 19, stopped 46 to 6. Read as a day's move, that says 40 mailboxes came off suspension overnight. Read correctly, it says 66 mailboxes fell out of the instrument while staying exactly as suspended as they already were.

The drop is domain-shaped, which is the part worth acting on. Nineteen sending domains lost all three of their mailboxes at the same moment, with a handful of singles alongside. The gate asks for live subscriptions only, so a cancelled or lapsed one simply stops being returned and nothing raises an error. That pattern reads like a subscription or billing event at the warmup provider rather than a flaky read, but this file cannot see the provider's account and will not guess at it.

Q2Does that cost sending capacity on Monday?

Some of it, and one part is stuck in a way nothing in the pipeline can undo on its own.

The gate keeps a ledger of mailboxes it suspended in its own name, so it knows which ones are its to release. It holds 72 of those. Only 5 are still in the roster it can see. The other 67 are suspended in SmartLead by this gate, and the gate will never generate a resume for them, because the code that decides on resumes walks the warmup readings and a mailbox with no reading is not in that walk. They sit suspended with no instrument watching them and no clock running out.

That repair belongs in the send program, which owns suspend and resume. This repo does not touch that lever, on purpose.

The second effect shows up in this morning's send plan, written around 11:15 UTC, which is after this cycle closed. A mailbox missing from the warmup list already has a reason of its own in that plan: its warmup age is unknown. Expect that row to jump. Read its only count, not its count: a mailbox blocked by something else as well picks the reason up and proves nothing. That rule cost the 11-04 debrief its top recommendation and it applies here exactly.

Q3Why did 578 lead-slots produce one contact point?

A picked-over book, plus an ownership rule throwing out almost every website the search found. Neither is bought with money.

The book half is plain. The lane has 1,771 leads to walk and it is on lap 20. Across the cycle the methods turned up 1,227 things and 1,226 of them were already on file. One was new.

The other half swung hard from yesterday. In the newest pass, 120 of 128 leads resolved no website at all, and the cause is not a dead search plan: the search ran 128 times, refused twice, and handed back 110 candidate sites. The ownership rule rejected 102 of those 110, and 16 more were social profiles rather than sites. Nine of the lane's collection methods need a website first, so those 120 leads lost their page half before any method ran.

Yesterday the same reading was three leads of 150. A swing that size between two days is the cursor reaching a different slice of the book, not a change in the rule. The rule itself is deliberate and has been since the 2026-08-18 audit: nothing gets filed that cannot be tied to this creator. More search credit buys nothing here. The redirect fault that led the last two debriefs went to zero in the two newest passes for the same reason, because a lead with no resolved site has no link to follow.

Fixes shipped this cycle

A fleet that halved and stayed halved now says so on the figures a reader opens first

On 2026-11-10 a short reading of the fleet was caught and the move counts were guarded against it. That day the roster came back an hour later, so the guard was built for a blip: it marks a run that read fewer mailboxes than the run before it. A roster that drops and stays down is short exactly once. The step-down ages out of the window within a day, and from then on a fleet half the size of yesterday's reads as a steady one.

That is this cycle, and the guard was the wrong shape for it. The two change figures were never covered at all, so the snapshot published clear down 24 and stopped down 40 beside a flag saying the comparison was sound.

The report now records how many mailboxes each of the two readings covered, the difference between them, and whether they describe the same fleet. When they do not, both the day-over-day flag and the move-count flag read false, so the field a reader opens first cannot say yes to a comparison across two different rosters. It also now counts the mailboxes the gate suspended in its own name that have left its roster, which is the 67 above: capacity nobody can give back.

Verified: ran against the live gate state and it reproduces 28 against 94, a difference of 66, both flags false, and 67 stranded suspensions. Five new test cases cover the day of the drop and the morning after, when nothing in the window is short any more and only the roster sizes carry the fault. Full suite passes, type check clean.
