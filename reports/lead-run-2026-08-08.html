The three questions

Q1Why did the finder suddenly do twice as well?

Yesterday's fix fired for the first time, nine times. On 08-07 the anti-starvation floor was proven to be an empty net: it only revived search terms that had never been run, and that reserve drains permanently. The repair shipped at 07:28Z as a second tier, which revives the best proven terms instead, meaning terms that already converted at a measurable rate and have since cooled for 21 days or more.

It ran in all nine sessions this cycle. Each time the log reads the same way: active pool exhausted, no never-run terms remain, reactivated N proven terms. The results follow directly. Yesterday: 7 hard stops on an empty term pool, 6 starvation warnings, 110 pitchable. Today: 0 hard stops, 0 starvation warnings, 246 pitchable. Quality moved too, not just volume. The share of found channels worth pitching went from 19.3% to 31.9%, the highest of the 08-01 to 08-08 block, because revived proven terms are terms already measured against real conversions rather than fresh guesses.

Q2So why did only 70 leads get parked?

Because finding a good creator and finding their email address are two different problems, and the second one is still the wall. Of the 246 pitchable creators found today, 72 landed in approved_hold and 161 went to needs_contact, which is the lane for creators who are worth contacting but whose email nobody could verify. That is a 31% verify rate, right in the usual band, so nothing regressed. Doubling the intake simply doubled both piles.

One thing did change for the better. On 08-06 most of the parking came from rescuing old rows, not from new supply. Today the pool gain (+70) matches this cycle's own promotions (72) almost exactly, so the parking is genuinely today's work. The counterweight: needs_contact grew by 162 and now stands at 5,040, well clear of approved_hold's 4,173. The two piles together are 9,213 creators found, scored, and never contacted.

Q3Where is the day's time actually going?

Roughly a third of it went into re-classifying search terms the machine had already rejected. Every session opens by topping up the term reservoir, which scrapes YouTube autocomplete for candidate phrases and asks a cheap model whether each one would surface the kind of creator we want. Nine of those passes ran this cycle. Together they classified 38,869 candidates and kept 79, a hit rate of 0.20%. Each pass took 46 to 59 minutes of back-to-back model calls.

The reason it repeats is a gap in bookkeeping, not a bad classifier. A term that gets kept is written into the terms table, so it never comes back as new. A term that gets rejected is written nowhere at all. Autocomplete offers it again next run, the code sees a string it doesn't have, and pays to judge it again. Measured overlap between consecutive days: 70% to 76% of a harvest's candidates had already been judged on the previous run. Meanwhile every one of the nine sessions ended on its time budget, so this is not spare capacity being wasted. It is being taken directly out of finding.

youtube-lead-finder-v1fd9773aPersistent reject cache for the keyword prefilter. Rejected terms are remembered in logs/prefilter-reject-cache.json and skipped on later runs. Only rejections are cached, never keeps, so a stale entry can drop a term but can never let an unjudged one through, matching the classifier's existing fail-closed rule. The cache is stamped with a fingerprint of the prefilter skill text plus the model ids, so editing the skill or repointing models.json throws the whole cache away and re-judges everything. A batch that failed on transport is not cached, so one bad network minute can't become permanent.
