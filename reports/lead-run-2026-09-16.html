The three questions

Q1Did Casey's $300 actually fix enrichment?

Yes, completely, and it is cheaper than we thought. The batch that started at 14:34Z has produced 410 finished leads with 0 failures in seventeen hours. Compare that with the day before, when nine batches produced nothing at all.

The OpenRouter account meter read $41.10 for the day against those 410 leads, so the real price is about ten cents a lead. The estimate we have been quoting is 16 to 17 cents. That matters for the number in front of Casey: the remaining pool of 278 leads costs roughly $28, not $45, and the whole 603-lead backlog was going to come in near $60 rather than the $97 written in the standing orders. The balance is $258.71, which at the current rate is about six days, and most of that rate is this backlog draining rather than a permanent burn.

What it buys, in plain terms. 6,192 leads are now researched, bundled and ready for someone to write an email. That shelf is the entire point of pausing discovery, and it is waiting on Casey to release it, not on any machine.

Q2Yesterday's credit gate shipped. Why did a 500-lead batch still die?

Because the gate was right about when to start waiting and wrong about when to stop. It held from 08:07Z on a definite reading of DRY -0.19, and those five hours cost nothing at all, exactly as designed. Then at 13:07Z the probe itself failed to complete. The rule says an unfinished probe proves nothing, so it returned OK, and the chain launched 500 leads into an account that was still empty. 499 of them failed. Each one had already paid for its YouTube harvest and its Decodo transcripts before dying at the last step. The credits did not really land until about 14:34Z.

The asymmetry is the whole lesson. Before you start waiting you have no evidence, so you run. Once you are waiting you are holding OpenRouter's own word that the balance is below the floor, and a probe you could not finish is not evidence against that word. Fixed: only a confirmed balance reading releases the gate now. An unreadable answer keeps waiting, which costs nothing, because the leads never leave the pool.

Q3Why is the recovery lane parking so little?

Part of it is honest arithmetic. The lane ran three collect passes of 150 and six verify passes, and parked 40 leads. With discovery off, that is the only source of new leads, and 40 is normal for a lane working through a book of 3,711 with a capped search plan.

But the verify half was not working at all, and had not been for weeks. Every verify pass this cycle was handed the same four leads. All six of them. Running the selector by hand against the database returned those four leads and nothing else, so the entire verify queue was four permanently stuck rows.

The cause is the same bug class this selector has now been bitten by three times. The marks that say "somebody already ruled on this" are written on a row, but ZeroBounce rules on an address. A lead often holds the same address twice under two labels, because two different collection methods found it: the About-tab button saves it as a YouTube email and the website scrape saves it as a business email. ZeroBounce gets asked once, the verdict lands on the row the tool was handed, and the twin row stays blank forever. So the lead comes back every pass and buys another credit to re-learn that info@shanesmithlaw.com is undeliverable, which it has been re-learning since 24 August.

Fixed and verified live. The selector now asks whether this address was ruled on for this lead by any row. Against the real database: 966 unruled email rows across the whole table, exactly 16 newly excluded, every one a duplicate of an address already ruled on, and a lead holding a second genuinely unchecked address still selects. No backfill needed.

The key-pool question answered itself, by accident

This is the ninth consecutive morning reading 15 working / 50 out of quota / 1 blocked of 66. Four previous debriefs called for a probe six hours after the reset to tell "slow refill" apart from "quota cut," and nobody ran it.

The last nine days ran the experiment anyway, from the other direction. On 09-11 through 09-13 nothing of ours spent a single YouTube unit overnight. This cycle we spent heavily: 410 enrichment leads plus roughly 1,500 doomed lead attempts, all of which pay for a YouTube harvest. The morning reading did not move by one key. It cannot be our spending, because our spending went from nothing to a lot and the number stayed identical. And it cannot be a slow refill either, because 50 keys cannot be drained in the twenty minutes between the reset and the probe.

So the quota on those 50 projects was cut, and buying more keys from those accounts buys nothing. This only bites when discovery resumes, because keyword search costs 100 units a call while enrichment runs on 1-unit calls. Worth settling before Casey lifts the pause, not after. audit-key-projects.sh in the finder names the projects and costs no quota.

Deliberately not built today, so it can be calibrated against a lane that has just been unjammed.
