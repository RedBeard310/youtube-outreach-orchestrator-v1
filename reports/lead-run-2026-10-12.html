Q2Four passes walked 600 leads and parked two people. Is the lane working?

Yes, and the book is what is finished. The methods found 2,479 contact points on 596 of the 600 leads, and 2,467 of them were already in the table. That is a 99.5% re-walk: nine of thirteen methods ran at full strength on nearly every lead and turned up the same addresses they turned up last lap. Twelve points were genuinely new, spread over ten people. Three of those were email addresses on three people, and two of the three verified and parked.

Website resolution was not the constraint, and this time the arithmetic is unarguable. Brave ran 15 searches across 600 leads, because 145 to 148 of every 150 already had a site stored. Zero key refusals, cause: resolution_ok, no_site: 1 of 150 on the newest pass. The cursor is on lap 9 of a 2,514-lead book, stranded held at 3 for the 29th straight day, and needs_contact is 3,512.

Cost of the collecting: about 7 cents of Brave. The lane is close to free and it is close to empty, and those two facts are the same fact.

Q3Yesterday's fix worked. So why did the error report get harder to read, not easier?

Because the one shape big enough to read was the thing it retired. 09-finra was 53 of pass 1's 442 errors and then vanished: it does not appear in the top shapes of passes 2, 3 or 4 at all, which is yesterday's commit going live mid-cycle and working. What was left behind is a tally that cannot name anything. Pass 4 logged 458 errors across 432 shapes, the largest holding 4 of them, under 1%, with 426 shapes in the unnamed tail. The cause is structural: an error shape keeps the hostname on purpose, because the dead-method breaker needs one shape to mean "fails the same way on every lead". The hostname is per lead, so 150 identical failures become 150 shapes.

Counting the same errors by cause instead takes one pass to answer it. On a six-lead probe, nine shapes collapsed into three causes, and two of the three were real faults rather than weather. The first: the shared fetch guard allowed application/xml and refused text/xml, which is how most sitemaps are served, so 28-sitemap, the only method that finds the contact and team pages a homepage never links to, was throwing those sitemaps away unread. The second is the bigger one, and it needed a second instrument to size: 18-rdap has written 336 contact points in its life, all of them on 18 and 19 August, and nothing in the 53 days since. It sends no User-Agent. Three domains probed the same minute returned 403 with no header and 200 with one.

Nothing already built could have caught that. The pass footer counts points in total, so a method going dark hides behind 34-channel-page's 15,036. The shape tally keys on the hostname. The breaker needs one shape, so it was blind to it too. And the no-site rate was healthy throughout, because resolution really was fine. The gap was that no instrument asked the simplest question available: when did each method last produce anything?
