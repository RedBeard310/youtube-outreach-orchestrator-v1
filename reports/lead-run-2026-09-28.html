The only way to find a website for those is a paid web search, and both Brave Search keys have been at their monthly spending cap since Friday. So the share of leads the lane could not place a website for went 3%, 3%, 48%, 94% across the day's four passes, and the last pass collected 18 contact points from 4 leads out of 150. Nothing is broken. The lane is reading the leads that were always going to need money. At $5 per 1,000 searches, attempting that whole tier once costs under $3. Zero emails went out because it was Sunday and the sender runs Monday to Friday. Two parked, no faults, no halts, no Anthropic spend for the fifteenth day.

The shape of the day

The recovery lane walks a book of 3,317 leads that have a good score and no working email address. The book is sorted so the cheap leads come first. A lead whose YouTube page links a website, or whose website has already been found and stored, sits at the front, because reading it costs nothing. A lead with no link anywhere sits at the back, because the only way to find its website is to pay for a web search. Those back-of-book leads are called tier 1 in the code, and there are 576 of them.

Four collect passes ran, at 07:00, 14:01, 21:01 and 04:00 UTC. The first two were still in the front of the book and resolved a website for 97% of their leads. The 21:01 pass crossed the boundary, and the 04:00 pass was fully inside tier 1. Here is the same day read two ways.

The first two passes were bad for a different reason, and that reason has no remedy

Worth separating, because the fix for one is not the fix for the other. The 07:00 and 14:01 passes could see fine. They resolved websites for 97% of their leads and still collected one contact point between them across 300 leads. That is the mined-out book the last five debriefs have described: the lane is on lap 6, it has read these leads before, and a second reading of a lead that gave nothing the first time gives nothing again. No amount of search credit changes that. The only things that put fresh leads in front of the lane are new arrivals or lifting the discovery pause, and the pause is Casey's call.

The last two passes are the opposite case. Those leads have never been readable, because nobody has ever found a website for them, and the one tool that could is refusing. That one does have a price on it.

The three questions

1Did we find new leads?

No, and that is the standing order, not a fault. Discovery has been off since 2026-09-08 on Casey's instruction, so 0 new channels is the intended state. Every sweep, the keyword harvest and the campaign loop are stopped and disabled, and the flag file that holds them off is untouched.

2Did we recover leads we already had?

Barely. 40 contact points from 10 leads, against 1,300 in the prior seven days. Five of the forty were email addresses. The verify half tested two addresses in two separate passes and flipped both, which is the whole +2: 6,863 to 6,865 parked. Six of its eight scheduled passes were handed nothing to test. That is not a verify problem. Verify can only check what collect finds, and collect spent the day either re-reading emptied leads or unable to search.

3Did we email anybody?

No, and Sunday was always going to read zero. The Siege sender runs Monday to Friday because SmartLead only delivers Monday to Thursday, and its volume ramp counts sending days rather than calendar dates, so nothing slipped by waiting. Monday is day 4 of the ramp at a cap of 100. Then, forty minutes after this cycle closed, two emails did go out. At 00:20 Pacific on Monday the session-start send fired, found two leads in the approved lane and pushed both to SmartLead in 17.5 seconds. That lane was declared structurally empty on 09-25. It is not empty, it refills at a trickle, and the thing that fires it is still the unexplained session-start trigger nobody has found.

What broke, what's fixed

Nothing faulted

No fatal error signatures, no halt flag, no quota stop, no failed batch. Enrichment ran two single-lead batches and idled after each, correctly: its pool is three leads and two of those are permanently excluded. The shelf stands at 6,575 leads ready to write, with 6,857 of 6,865 carrying a research bundle (99.9%). Anthropic spend was $0.00 for the fifteenth day running.

The alarm counted a total search outage as one lost lookup

Yesterday's fix taught the alarm to count Brave refusals inside the pass it judges, so it could stop dismissing a real outage. It counts the number of times the "every key refused" line appears in the log. That line is printed at most once per pass, by design. The search helper sets a flag the first time every key refuses and never prints again, so one process prints one line however many lookups die after it. A collect pass is one process.

So the count can only ever be 0 or 1, and the alarm was reading it as a volume. On today's worst pass it would have reported that the pass "did lose 1 lookup ... which is the smaller half of this pass" about a pass where 141 of 150 leads got no website at all. Same shape as the three fixes before it: a sentence that names a remedy, or dismisses one, with no measurement behind it.

Shipped (7a06ce5). The refusal is now a yes-or-no, which is what it always was, and the cost is measured in leads. Once the line has printed, every later lookup in that pass got nothing, so the code records how many leads ran behind it, how many of those resolved no website (the upper bound on what the dead plan cost) and how many resolved anyway from a stored site or the channel page (proof the free routes are still carrying part of the pass). The same commit retires the site-collapse alarm's private copy of the parse, which had its own tail window that read past its own pass boundary, so a refusal logged by the next pass counted as evidence about this one. All three lane alarms now read one pass through one function with one regex, the same regex the lane itself rewinds on.

Verified: tsc clean, 100 tests pass (4 new, including a refusal-in-the-previous-pass case that must cost this pass nothing while still reporting the collapse). Run live against the real collect log: the alarm now reads "every key refused from its first lead onward, so the 150 leads behind that point ran with no web search at all. 141 of them resolved no website, and 9 resolved anyway off a stored site or the channel page."

The money

Anthropic: $0.00. Fifteenth consecutive zero. OpenRouter: $1.50 a day against $2.03 yesterday, with $191.38 left, which is about 128 days of runway. Spend fell because nothing is enriching, which is the same reason it fell and rose across the last fortnight. Runway here tracks whether the pipeline has work, not whether it is healthy.

Brave Search is the one active money problem and it now has a price. Both keys sit at their monthly spending cap and answer 402. The 576 tier-1 leads each need roughly one search to find a website, and Brave costs $5 per 1,000 searches, so attempting the whole tier once is under $3. The lane is partway through them already. This is the first time the remaining work has been priced rather than described, and it is small enough that the decision is about willingness rather than cost.

Apify rolls on 30 September, two days out. Its last run recovered 62 leads at $0.099 each before parking itself on its $10 reserve, where it has sat for three weeks. It is the only lane with a proven cost per recovered lead.

YouTube keys: 15 of 66 working, the same as every morning for a fortnight. This is settled and not worth re-diagnosing: the quota on 50 of the projects was cut, so the working pool is 15 and more keys on those accounts buy nothing. Nothing in the pipeline is currently blocked on it, because the sweeps that spend search quota are paused.

Recommended next, ranked

  1. Raise the Brave Search cap, or add a key. This is the only item with a number on it. 576 leads in the book have no website anybody has ever found, the lane is inside them now, and one pass at them costs under $3 of search credit. Every day it stays capped, the lane spends 600 lead slots on leads it structurally cannot read. Casey's spend call, and the smallest one on this list.
  2. Decide the discovery pause. Unchanged from the last five debriefs and still the biggest lever. The front of the recovery book is mined out, which is what the 97%-resolution passes collecting one contact point are telling us. Only new arrivals create work for the lane, for enrichment and for the send path.
  3. Watch Apify on 30 September. Two days out. Last run: 62 recovered leads at $0.099 each in 52 minutes. It will size its own batch to whatever is spendable above the $10 reserve.
  4. Find what fires npm run send when an agent opens this repo. It loaded two real emails at 00:20 Pacific on Monday. The lane it draws from was thought to be permanently empty and is not. Nothing has gone wrong yet, and nothing is watching it either.
  5. Unblock more Siege offers before the ramp reaches 250 a day. Ten of sixteen cannot run, each for a small named reason it prints at the top of every run. Offer variety, not lead supply, is what lets one board absorb more volume without emailing anyone twice.
The lesson. The lane's yield fell for two completely different reasons on the same day, eight hours apart, and the daily number is the sum of both. One half was re-reading leads it had already emptied, which no money fixes. The other half was reading leads it has never been able to see, which about three dollars fixes. Averaging them produces a number that recommends nothing.
