The three questions

Q1Why does the push lose the same people every day?

Because SmartLead knew the address was dead, told us so, and nothing on our side wrote it down. This is the one question this cycle could actually answer and close.

Twelve of Wednesday’s 38 losses were refused with bounce_count=1, which is SmartLead saying it already holds a hard bounce for that mailbox. It refuses the add, and it will refuse it again tomorrow, because nothing about a dead mailbox changes.

The pipeline already has machinery for exactly this. automator/scripts/dnc-sync.py runs hourly, reads lead_candidates.email_bounced, and files a bounced entry on the do-not-contact registry, which is permanent by Operation Siege’s own rules. The flag simply never got set. The only thing that writes it is sync-smartlead-replies.ts, which reads bounces off leads SmartLead ACCEPTED into a campaign, and a lead refused at the door never gets a campaign membership for it to read. So the lead kept email_verification_result = 'valid', stayed off the registry, and Siege planned it again the next sending day.

Measured over the 44 days of retained log to this morning: 188 bounce refusals across 116 distinct leads, with three of them re-attempted 13 times each. All 24 of Wednesday’s recurring losses are still sitting at email_bounced = false, do_not_contact = false and verification valid. Nothing was ever going to retire them.

Every attempt costs a planned slot, a written personalised email, and one more person in the morning people_lost figure. That is how a 24-person cohort got reported as a fresh loss on eleven consecutive mornings. Fixed this cycle, see below.

Q2What happened to the 16 people the log never mentioned?

Five of them are two whole batches that died on SmartLead’s rate limit. The other eleven sit in a gap between a batch being written and being pushed, and this report cannot close that from the log.

The day’s loss splits into 18 refused by SmartLead, 3 drafts we declined to offer, 1 that never got a draft, and 16 people the push log does not mention at all. emailed_unlogged is 0, so the send ledger agrees they were never written to, and deferred_unapproved is 0, so none of them is waiting for tomorrow’s approval.

Two batches raised listCampaignMailboxes 429: Account rate limit exceeded and escalated with pushed: 0, holding 1 lead and 4 leads. Those five printed no SENT, no FAIL and no SKIP, which is exactly how a dead batch looks in this counter. This is the 2026-10-05 shape back past its own retries: 17 of these refusals fired in the push window, and twice the guard spent all four attempts with 60-second waits between them and still got a 429. The backoff really does sleep, so the account was saturated for three minutes straight. SmartLead meters the whole account at 200 requests a minute and the reply poller walks 71 campaigns every two minutes alongside the push.

The remaining eleven are a different shape and worth naming even unresolved. 24 of the day’s 149 batch directories have no push-batch block in the log at all. One checked by hand, siege-2026-11-18-25787206-short-save-time-3b2be2, holds five finished drafts and a report.json written at 12:15Z, and push-batch was never run on it. That is the 2026-10-20 lesson and it is why this is stated as a lead rather than a finding. The batch loop lives in automator, which is the send path and not this repo’s to edit.

Fixes shipped this cycle

SmartLead knew the address was dead and we asked it again every day

0d6987cc2, youtube-email-outreach-v1

Q1 is the whole story. A bounce is the only refusal reason SmartLead gives that is permanent, and it was the only one the push could not act on, because every reason reached the caller as prose inside an error message. Enough to explain a lost email, not enough to retire the address behind it.

assertLeadAdded now throws a LeadNotAddedError carrying the counters SmartLead set, and the push acts on one of them. On a bounce it writes email_bounced, email_bounced_at and bounce_reason onto the lead row, and clears email_verification_result to invalid. It retires nothing by itself. The existing permanent machinery does that: dnc-sync.py files the bounced registry entry on its next hourly run. Clearing the verification result is what stops the next plan the same day instead of an hour later, because Siege takes the main address only when that column reads valid.

Deliberately only bounce_count. Retiring an address is not reversible by this pipeline, so the one counter that acts on it is the one that cannot mean anything else. The repeat-offer skip is Casey’s call from 2026-10-07, duplicate and already-added are this campaign’s own state, the lead limit is the account, and an unsubscribe is a decision SmartLead’s own block list already carries into the registry. Six of the eight new assertions pin which reasons must NOT trigger it, including the bounce_count: 0 that SmartLead sends on every single reply.

outreach_status is left alone on purpose. Seventeen of the recurring cohort sit at sent_to_smartlead, which is a true thing that happened before the address died, and overwriting it would move them out of the shelf’s already-sent count and claim they were never emailed. A rotated address from contact_points is retired at the point instead, using the verify pass’s own wording for a permanent rejection. The write is best-effort and caught, because the push has already failed and rolled its send row back by the time it runs, so a database hiccup must change nothing about the batch.

It self-heals the existing 116 without a bulk data write. Each of them records its bounce the next time it is refused, which costs one more wasted email each and then stops, rather than eleven more days of them.

Verified: typecheck clean, 13 of 13 tests in the outcome suite, and the full suite at 311 of 311 across 25 files. The 44-day refusal count and the state of all 24 recurring leads were read out of the live log and the live database before the fix was written.
