The three questions

Q1Yesterday's recovery-lane fix predicted about 0.9 contact points per lead. What did it actually do?

0.93 per lead, which is the prediction to two decimal places. Three collect passes dispatched 40 leads each. 44 of the 120 produced something, and together they wrote 112 contact points. The four days before the fix wrote 46, 2, 0 and 1.

The queue is genuinely moving now. Its cursor sat on the 40 oldest leads for four days; it has since walked forward to leads first discovered on 2026-07-12, and it is still inside its first lap of the pool. Nothing is being re-run.

Points are not yet emails, and that is the thing to watch. Collection and verification are separate steps: seven verify passes picked up 32 recovered leads over the cycle. The needs_contact pool grew from 4,724 to 4,796 while 88 new leads arrived in it, so roughly 16 left it. That is the first outflow the numbers show, and it is small. Tomorrow's cycle is the one that says whether points convert into parked leads at any useful rate.

Q2Why did good leads fall 38% on a day with no errors and no outage?

The primary lane restarted a lap, and each lap is worth about a third of the one before it. The recommended-videos feed walked its 11,938-seed book for the fifth time and finished at 15:12. Lap 6 began at 16:10 and has walked 6,898 of those same seeds since. By the sweep's own counter it has found 54 good creators doing it.

That is 0.0078 per seed. Lap 5 ran at 0.021. Lap 4 ran at 0.033. Lap 1, back in July, ran at 0.45. Each re-walk of the same list finds the neighbours of neighbours we already took, so the sixth pass is turning up 1.7% of what the first did.

The cost of that is not spread evenly. The feed lane consumed 6,974 of the 9,510 seeds walked today, which is 73%, and 45% of the day's model spend, and returned 40% of the good leads. The video-graph lane, which walks fresh seeds its own refill made rather than re-walking an old book, did 0.0187 per seed on 2,402 seeds. It is 2.4 times more efficient per seed than the lane the standing orders call primary.

Q3Yesterday said the keyword lane should be given new phrases or retired. Which is it?

Neither yet, because on cost it is the best lane we have. Its per-pass yield did fall, from 0.083 to 0.056 fresh good leads, and all 15 sessions still opened STOCK-UP with 22 starvation readings, so the term drought is real and unchanged.

But 591 passes produced 33 of the day's 134 good leads, which is 25% of the output for 18% of the model bill. That works out at 2.6¢ per good lead against the feed lane's 3.9¢ and the video-graph lane's 3.4¢. Retiring it would delete the cheapest quarter of the day.

The reason it looks bad on a per-pass measure and good on a per-dollar one is that a finder pass is cheap and a graph seed is not. The honest read is that the keyword lane is not the problem, and it should stay until something replaces the volume.

Recommended next — ranked

  1. Decide whether the feed lane should keep re-walking the same book. This is the one decision that changes tomorrow's numbers, and it is yours because the standing orders make this lane primary. Lap 6 is at 0.0078 good leads per seed, a third of lap 5 and 1.7% of lap 1, and it is spending 73% of our seeds and 45% of our model bill to get there. The cheapest option is a rule: when a finished lap yields under some floor, walk only the seeds added since the last lap instead of starting a full new one. That keeps the lane running, which the standing orders require, and stops it re-asking 11,938 creators a question they already answered.
  2. Watch whether recovered contact points become parked leads. Collection is fixed and proven at 0.93 points per lead. What is unproven is the step after it: 32 leads went to email verification and the needs_contact pool only shed about 16. If that ratio holds it is a slow lane, and the bottleneck has moved from the queue to verification. One more cycle settles it, and no new seeds are needed either way.
  3. Find a seed source that is not our own output. Carried seven days. All three graph lanes are now fed only by the leads the pipeline itself finds, so the supply shrinks whenever yield does, which is the loop we are in. Dropping the feed lane's view floor from 20k to 5k is still the fastest way to buy about a week of road while a real answer gets built.
  4. Keep the keyword lane. Yesterday's open question resolves against retiring it. It made 25% of the day's good leads for 18% of the bill, at 2.6¢ each, cheaper than either graph lane. It is starved, not broken, and the starvation is a term-supply problem the backoffs already handle gracefully.
  5. Decide the outlet for the parked pools. 3,659 in approved_hold and 4,796 in needs_contact. That is 8,455 creators found and never contacted, up 137 today.
