The three questions

Q1160 emails Thursday, 82 Friday. What halved it?

The fleet, and only the fleet. The daily ceiling never moved: 870 both mornings. What moved is how many mailboxes were allowed to start a new person. Siege printed 42 live at Thursday's push and 18 at Friday's, and the allowance those mailboxes carry fell from 630 messages a day to 270. SmartLead's own backlog was flat across the two days, 143 unsent first emails and about 822 people mid-sequence. So nothing about the queue changed. Three fifths of the mailboxes simply stopped being allowed to send.

And all 82 slots were used. 145 Whale-tier people were ready against 82 slots, the push took every one, and lost nothing. Under the fleet it had, the day did the best it could do.

One small loss did not reach the failure count. At 10:32Z SmartLead answered 429 when Siege tried to start a campaign holding 4 people. Those 4 are loaded and will not be sent until somebody starts that campaign. They counted as sent everywhere.

The snapshot's own day-over-day fields read -3 inboxes and -45 messages, and they are not wrong so much as not comparable. delta_comparable: false, because the older plan on disk was written at 23:00Z Thursday by a dry run, 11.4 hours after Thursday's push. That is the shape the 10-16 stamps were built to catch, and they caught it. The 42-to-18 figure above is taken from Siege's own log line at each push instead, which is the only snapshot a re-plan cannot overwrite.

Q2Did any of those 82 emails reach somebody who had never heard from us?

One. 78 of the 79 people were repeat offers to somebody already written to. Over the seven days before this cycle the same measure read 1,158 new people out of 1,685, so the first-touch share fell from 68.7% to 1.3%.

The giveaway had been sitting in plain sight for three mornings. The shelf printed ready_to_write: 5138 and already_sent: 1663 on 10-15, 10-16 and 10-17: the same four numbers, to the person, across 242 emails. Sending an email does not move a lead out of approved_hold, so a repeat offer leaves every shelf figure untouched, and a lossless push and a day that reached nobody print identically.

It is a queue, not a fault. Siege hands slots out strongest-first, and "strongest" means tier, then score, then subscribers. Nothing in that order asks whether a person has heard from us. The Whale tier ran out of never-emailed people on the 776-person push of 10-13 (767 of those were first touches), so since then its re-offer queue has been first in line every morning. Friday it took all 82 slots, while 141 Strong-7 and 808 Other-7+ people sat ready, with research bundles, and got nothing.

Repeat offers are deliberate. Casey reopened that stream on 2026-10-07, and a second offer to a creator who ignored the first is a real play. The point is that "82 sent, 0 failed" and "we reached one new person" are two different days and they printed the same number. Both instruments shipped today: sent_today.first_touch counts people against the prior seven days, and send_plan.waiting_for_a_slot carries the queue that explains it, read off the plan Siege already writes. The 79 counts people with a new-offer row in leads.outreach_sends; the 82 counts people stamped outreach_processed_at. The three-person gap is rows that landed outside the window and is not worth chasing.

Two consequences, and the second one is the expensive one. First, the ceiling: 18 mailboxes at 15 messages a day is 270, and after follow-ups that was 82. Second, and worse for the offers, Siege picks a mailbox by persona, and several offers may only come from a made-up name. With 3 made-up mailboxes live, those offers are capped at a handful a day however many people are on the shelf.

The one piece of good news is that this gate lets a mailbox come back. Its own rule is 48 hours reading 90 or better, not the old gate's two-week warmup restart, and two mailboxes are already partway there ("recovering: clean reading, 11h of 48h at 90+"). So this is reputation to repair, not hardware to replace.

Read off automator/state/inbox-health.json at its 07:05Z run, which bands all 58 mailboxes hourly, cross-checked against the persona labels in Siege's own plan. Its thresholds: stop below 80 or two spam hits in three days, watch below 90 or one hit, and a domain with two sick mailboxes pulls its siblings in, which is why these fail in whole threes.
