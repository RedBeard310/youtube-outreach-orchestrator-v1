The three questions

1A zero after the best day on record. Did something break?

No. siege-plan.timer is set to Monday through Friday at 08:15 Eastern, 45 minutes before SmartLead's sending window opens, and its last run was Friday at 12:15 UTC. It did not fire on Saturday and it will not fire today. That is deliberate: SmartLead delivers Monday to Thursday only, so writing emails on a Saturday would just queue them behind Monday's.

The ramp costs nothing either. Siege's volume ceiling climbs 50, 50, 100, 100, 150, 150, 200, 250, and the note in its own config says the count is kept in sending days rather than dates, so a skipped day resumes where it left off instead of jumping. Friday was day 3 and took 100. Monday is day 4 and takes 100. The plateau of 250 a day is still about four sending days out, which lines up with Casey's instruction that full volume was not wanted until the end of September.

The only thing this repo's own send command did was confirm it has nothing to do. It fired at 00:20, selected zero leads, and logged attempted=0 sent=0 failed=0. Its lane has been fully sent since Friday morning and the 6,573-lead shelf sits under approved_hold, which its selector does not read on purpose.

2Brave ran out of money. How much did that cost, and what did the lane do about it?

Both keys reached their monthly spending caps during the cycle. Two of the three collect passes logged All 2 Brave Search API key(s) refused: 402 Usage limit exceeded, the line the search helper prints when no key will answer at all. Nine of the collector's ten methods need the creator's own website, and the website comes from that search.

What it did not cost is the interesting part. The lane read its own refusal line and rewound its cursor twice, for previous_pass_search_dead, so the 300 leads in those two passes keep their turn instead of being stamped walked and waiting a full lap of 3,300 for another look. That machinery was built after 2026-09-04, when exactly this failure turned a widened batch into 71 parked leads instead of 627. It worked without anybody looking at it.

What it did cost is the small share of leads that needed a fresh lookup. Site resolution still read 97% across both passes, because most of those leads are re-walks whose site was already stored, and the free channel-page route resolves plenty more with no search at all. So the honest reading is two causes at once: a picked-over book, which no money fixes, and a dead search plan, which money does fix for the minority of leads needing a new lookup. Brave is also the pipeline's only web search since Tavily was dropped on 09-13, so the same cap throttles email finding, not just this lane.

3With sending off and Brave capped, what actually produced anything?

Three things, all small. The recovery lane parked 3 leads, its first parks in two days, from 27 contact points across 10 leads. The enrichment chain ran 3 batches of one lead each, three different leads, all exit 0, and then went back to idling. That is the shape of a finished job: the chain is no longer draining a queue, it is catching each lead the recovery lane flips, one at a time, within hours.

The shelf grew by exactly those three, from 6,570 to 6,573 researched leads ready to write, with 6,855 of 6,863 parked leads carrying a bundle. At Friday's 107 that is about sixty sending days of material, and at the 250-a-day plateau it is about twenty-six.

The Apify email-recovery lane produced nothing and correctly said so twelve times. It holds $10.0053 with $10 reserved, so it has $0.0053 to spend, which buys zero channels against a 25-channel floor. It has been resting on that reserve since 09-06. It is not broken and it is not going to restart on its own.

The alarm told Casey a real Brave outage was not evidence of a Brave outage

Shipped: orchestrator 41a23f5. Typecheck clean, 96 tests pass, 4 of them new, verified against the live collect log.

On 09-22 this alarm was fixed for blaming the search plan whenever the string "Brave Search API key" appeared anywhere in the log tail. That fix was right about the symptom and wrong about the mechanism: it assumed the line prints on every pass because one key sits permanently at its $5 cap, so it hard-coded the sentence "raising the cap would not have changed it" into the alarm text.

The line prints only when every key refuses a single lookup. Counted per pass, the last eight passes logged it 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 1 and 0 times. So when both keys capped out yesterday, the alarm fired at 01:11, recorded brave_refusal_logged: true, blamed the re-walk alone and told Casey his money would buy nothing. One hour earlier the lane itself had rewound the cursor off that same line. The two halves of one measurement disagreed, and the half that talks to Casey was the wrong one.

collectPassAttribution() now counts refusals inside the pass being judged, using the same pattern the rewind detector uses, so they cannot disagree again. The record gains pass_brave_refusals, and attributed_to can say book_rewalk+search_dead, because both were true. When a pass logs no refusal the alarm still says plainly that search credit is not the constraint. The rule this keeps re-learning: an alarm that names a remedy costing money has to read the pass in front of it, not the file it sits in, and that cuts both ways.

The thing worth remembering from this cycle. Two systems hit a wall on the same day and only one of them was a problem. Sending stopped because Saturday is not a sending day, the ramp counts sending days so nothing slipped, and Monday resumes exactly where Friday left off. Search stopped because the money ran out, and the recovery lane handled that correctly on its own, holding its cursor so 300 leads keep their turn. What needed fixing was neither of those. It was the sentence the alarm prints to Casey, which had been hard-coded a week ago to say a Brave refusal is never evidence, and was still saying it an hour after the lane had acted on one.
