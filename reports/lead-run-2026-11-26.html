The shape of the day

Everything that was supposed to run, ran. Siege wrote its plan at 11:26 UTC, the push unit reported success, and eight emails went into SmartLead. Zero fatal errors, no halt flag, $0 of Anthropic spend, eight cents a day on OpenRouter against a $298 balance, and the reply reader ran 22 times with no failures. This repo's own send command fired once and found nothing ready, so all eight emails came from the send program.

The day was small because the fleet is small. Only 4 of 124 mailboxes were live when Siege looked, worth 60 messages of capacity against an 870-message allowance, so the plan carried 25 people. The approval that morning covered 36, so eleven approved people never got a slot at all. 1,797 people are now waiting for one, up from 1,724.

Then 17 of the 25 got no email. Every single one of the 17 had been lost on an earlier push, none were new, and one has now been dropped on ten consecutive snapshot days. Eight people were emailed and all eight had heard from us before, which makes this the fifth sending day in a row that reached nobody new. Across the last eight days the pipeline has reached five people who had never heard from us, while 4,855 finished emails sit on the shelf for people nobody has ever written to.

The one reassuring number is the fleet itself. The clear band held flat at five mailboxes, on a roster that read 28 on both mornings, so the comparison is sound rather than two different fleets subtracted. The plan's drop from 16 live mailboxes to 4 is not a second collapse. It is yesterday's collapse arriving in the plan, which is covered in Q1.

Everything else behaved or was deliberately quiet. One lead was parked, taking the shelf to 7,393. The free recovery lane walked 600 lead slots across four passes and its own half produced 9 contact points and no email addresses, against 91 points and 10 addresses over the prior week, which is what a book on its twentieth lap pays. Enrichment is idle because there is nothing arriving to enrich, which is correct. Discovery stayed off per the standing order of 2026-09-08, so zero new channels, and none of that is a fault.

The three questions

Q1Did the fleet collapse a second time, or is this yesterday's collapse arriving late?

It is yesterday's collapse, reported again by a different instrument. The plan's live-mailbox count fell from 16 to 4 and its capacity from 240 messages to 60, which looks like the fleet halving twice in two days. It did not. The health gate moved those mailboxes out of the clear band at 08:05 UTC on 11-25, which yesterday's debrief reported as 16 clear going to 5. Siege wrote this plan at 11:26 UTC, three hours later, so this is the first plan written after that gate run. The gate itself is flat today: 5 clear on both mornings, with the roster covering 28 mailboxes on both readings, so the comparison is checked rather than assumed.

Inside the cycle the gate moved one mailbox back to clear, one to watch and five to stop, and the one that came back went down again in the same cycle. The clear band peaked at 6 at 12:05 UTC and finished at 5. Seven held mailboxes are healthy enough to lift themselves, the nearest within an hour, so some capacity returns for free. The part that will not return on its own is 67 mailboxes the gate suspended in its own name that have since left its roster, third morning running: nothing is watching them, so nothing can resume them, and that repair lives in the send-path repo.

Of the 120 mailboxes not live, the biggest named reason is a missing warmup record, on 96 of them. Only 22 carry it alone, and the 22 are the slots the day actually lost. Read the 22, never the 96. The second recoverable block is 12 mailboxes suspended in SmartLead with nothing else wrong, up 5 on yesterday.

Q2Why does the loss count keep falling while the day keeps getting worse?

Because the count is a numerator and the plan it comes out of shrank by three quarters. Across the last four sending cycles the published loss read 40, 23, 18, 17, falling every single morning, and the part of it that was the same people again read 32, 21, 18, 17. Read as counts, that is a leak closing. As a share of what the push was really driving, the loss on those four days read 14.0%, 27.1%, 20.0% and 68.0%, and the repeat-refusal part of it 11.2%, 24.7%, 20.0% and 68.0%.

Nothing about the standing cohort changed. They are planned every morning, written every morning, and refused every morning, for reasons that do not change overnight: two have channels that no longer exist on YouTube, several carry a bounce already on SmartLead's file, and seven have run out of offers. What changed is that the fleet can now only offer 25 slots, so the same leak takes two thirds of the day instead of a tenth of it. The note that introduced these fields on 11-07 priced the then-cohort of nine at "about 9% of a 101-slot day" by hand, and no field has carried that arithmetic since.

One honesty note: today's split covered all 17 losses, but one earlier day inside the 14-day window named fewer people than it lost, so "nobody new was lost" is a lower bound, not a certainty.

Q3What took the 17, and which parts of it are fixable here?

Five were refused by SmartLead, ten never reached the push at all, and two left no trace in the log. By cause: 7 people ran out of offers, 2 have channels YouTube no longer has, 1 got no draft because the writer could not find a clean job title, 2 were refused because SmartLead already has them as unsubscribed, and 1 was absent from its batch's manifest. Six push skips cost nobody, because two were rewritten and sent in the same run and two were never in the plan.

The seven offer-exhausted were all turned down by the same offer, love-your-channel-short, for the same precondition: no safe enemy in the research bank. Yesterday's fix published the exact wording per variant, so this morning needs no hand-reading of bundle files: 9 of the 10 leads hold a bank with zero enemies in it and 1 holds enemies the skill's safety filter ruled out. Those are opposite repairs. An empty bank needs another research pass or those people should stop being planned; a filtered bank is a question about how strict the filter should be, and that is Casey's call.

None of the 17 is fixable in this repo. The dead channels, the unsubscribes and the offer rotation all live in the send path, and the empty research banks are a research decision. What this repo can do is stop the arithmetic hiding, which is what shipped.

Fixes shipped this cycle

The loss is published as a share of the plan, not just a count

youtube-outreach-orchestrator-v1 · 841772a

people_lost and lost_recurring are numerators, and the plan they come out of changes size every morning. For four sending days running they have fallen while the day underneath them got worse, and this cycle the gap became absurd: 17 lost against 18, on a plan a quarter the size, with every one of the 17 a repeat. This is the same shape as the 10-16 and 11-23 lessons, a number read without its denominator, sitting in the field a reader opens to see whether the loss is growing.

push_outcome now carries people_lost_pct and lost_recurring_pct. Both are measured over people_emailed + people_lost, which is the plan minus anybody the approval gate held for tomorrow, so a person deferred on purpose cannot size a loss, and the denominator is checkable from the two fields printed beside it. lost_recurring_pct inherits lost_split_complete, so a truncated split makes it a lower bound rather than a confident figure. Both read null, never 0, when the loss is unknown or the push drove nobody, because 0% of nothing is the one reading that would make a collapsed plan look clean.

Verified against the live send log and Siege's own plan: 25 planned, 8 emailed, 17 lost and a 10-day streak all reproduce exactly, and the two new shares read 68.0 and 68.0. Six new assertions, including the case that matters, which is the same cohort reading 36% of a 25-person plan and 9.2% of a 98-person one. Suite 446 all pass, typecheck clean. No send, research or filter behaviour was touched.
