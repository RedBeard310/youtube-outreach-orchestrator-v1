The three questions

Q1Where did +350 come from?

From having six ways to find creators instead of one. The pool went 4,267 → 4,617, the biggest single-day gain since 08-01 and the fourth-biggest on record. Intake rose 48% to 5,332 creators, and the split by method is the whole story:

The verify rate improved too, for the first time in weeks. Of today's creators who reached an email outcome, 363 parked and 492 went to needs_contact, which is a 42.5% verify rate against the low-to-mid thirties this has held all month. Treat that as directional rather than settled: the day's leads were counted about an hour after the cycle closed, so some are still being worked.

The loop itself was quiet and healthy. 16 sessions, 132 finder passes, 0 hard stops, 0 quota stops, 0 crashes, 0 scoring failures, every session ending on its own time budget rather than on a problem.

Q2So why does the finder think it had a great day?

Because it grades itself by looking at the shared database, and it is no longer the only thing writing there. A finder pass records what it produced by asking the lead database which rows appeared since the pass began. That was accurate when the keyword engine was the only writer. Since 08-08 three background services have been writing continuously into the same place, and the newest of them, peer-network comments, only started yesterday and wrote 1,280 rows today.

The gap is not subtle. Across 132 passes the counter claimed 762 fresh pitchable for itself. The keyword engine's entire output for the whole 24 hours was 255. So at least two-thirds of what those passes claimed was work something else did, and the real figure is lower still, because passes only cover part of the day.

The number moved in the opposite direction to reality. Reported per-pass yield went from 2.40 yesterday to 5.77 today, which reads like the finder suddenly got twice as good. Measured properly, the keyword engine's pitchable-per-pass fell, from 3.75 to 1.93. Yesterday's report told the same story in reverse and nobody could see it: the counter also misses everything found between passes, and until peer-sweep arrived that undercount was bigger than the over-credit, so the two errors hid each other.

What it actually costs. That number is the trigger for the fade check, the rule that says "this batch of search terms is worked out, go invent new ones." A pass that reads 12 or more is treated as still productive and skips the pivot. Eight passes cleared 12 today, and on the keyword engine's real rate none of them plausibly did it on their own work. Eight missed pivots is small. The direction is not: the background services are days old and growing, so left alone this quietly switches off the mechanism that keeps the term supply alive. Fixed today.

Q3Did yesterday's harvest backoff deliver?

Yes, at about half the size predicted. The search-term harvest stopped force-firing every session: it ran 6 times against 14, skipped 106 times on the new low-yield backoff and 26 more on the ordinary cadence gate. Session startup fell from 21.5 minutes to 13.7, and the cycle spent 218 minutes on startup instead of 301, with 16 sessions running rather than 14.

The forecast was roughly four harvests, startup near a few minutes, and passes clearing 140. Actual: six harvests, 13.7 minutes, 132 passes. The shortfall is simply that the harvest was never the only thing in the opening stretch. The reservoir check, the verify kickoff, promotion and probe evaluation all sit there too, so removing the harvest cannot take startup to zero.

And the source really is dry. The six harvests that did run wrote 4 usable terms between them (0, 1, 2, 0, 1, and one unmeasured). The backoff is stopping waste, not restoring supply. Term supply held anyway: 138 discovery runs, 131 promotions, 0 quota stops, which is the invented-vein path carrying the pool on its own.

The rule that reads a lead's origin tag moved into one shared file, src/discovery-method.ts, so the daily report and the finder can never drift apart.
