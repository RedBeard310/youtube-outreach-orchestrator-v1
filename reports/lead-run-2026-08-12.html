The three questions

Q1Why did only one of five search methods find anything?

Because yesterday's fix was applied in one place and the same bug lived in three more. Every one of the day's 212 finds came from keyword search. The other methods contributed nothing.

Yesterday the campaign was hard-stopping because a retired service kept reporting "100.1% of quota used". The fix taught the campaign that a negative "requests remaining" means the service is switched off, not that quota is low, so the guard turns itself off instead of stopping the run. That worked. The campaign had zero quota stops today, down from six.

What nobody checked is that the four background sweeps each carried their own private copy of that same quota check, and none of the copies got the fix. So they read the same stale file, saw the same phantom 100.1%, and paused. Every 15 minutes. All day:

[sweep] PAUSE: quota at 100.1% >= 70% cap. Leaving headroom for the campaign.

There was no campaign to leave headroom for, and no quota being consumed. The file they were reading was written at 06:41Z the previous morning and has not been touched since, because nothing writes it once the retired service is gone. They were pausing on a snapshot of a world that no longer exists.

Q2Why did everything stop at 20:09Z?

Somebody paused it on purpose, and left a note saying so. This is not a fault and it needs no repair:

migration freeze: Airtable -> Postgres code swap in progress (2026-08-11T20:09:19Z)
Written by the migration work, not by an autopilot breach.
Remove this file to resume: rm logs/autopilot-halt.flag

The migration plan itself calls for exactly this, so the cutover doesn't land on a repo mid-run. The commits back it up: the email repo pointed its lead store at Postgres, and the deep-research repo collapsed its 52 per-client bases into one Postgres schema, both in that window. I left the freeze in place. Removing it is your call, not an unattended agent's.

One thing the freeze exposed. The halt flag is supposed to be the pipeline's one stop-everything switch, and the four sweeps have never honoured it. They kept running straight through, independently, and would have gone on writing leads into a database being swapped underneath them. Today that was hidden, because the false quota reading happened to be holding them still for the wrong reason. Fixing the quota bug on its own would have turned a silent problem into a live one, so both went in together.

Q3Underneath all that, is the finding engine healthy?

The part that ran was the best-quality finding we have had in a while. There just wasn't much of it.

21.7% of everything found was worth contacting, up from 11.0% yesterday, and 18 emails verified against 3 yesterday. Some of that is flattering: the low-precision method (peer-network comments, which has been running at 2.6%) contributed nothing today, so the average rose because the weak lane went quiet, not because the strong lane improved. Per pass, keyword search returned 0.96 pitchable, identical to yesterday.

Term supply is still the wall. 13 of 14 sessions stopped on "term supply exhausted", and the finder's own line is blunt about it: active pool exhausted, NO never-run and NO cooled proven terms remain. The probe table now holds 12,307 terms, 7,418 of them already tried, and 0 were promoted as winners this cycle. Nine sessions ran under 12 minutes against a 90-minute budget, because they ran out of things to search for and quit.

Legal Services was 27 of the 46 worth contacting, 59% of the day. That is the narrowest niche mix on record. It is what the last un-mined vein looks like when everything else is dry, and it will not hold for many more days.

Recommended next — ranked

  1. Decide when to lift the migration freeze, and expect a burst when you do. Both sweep backlogs have been frozen for a day and the recommended-videos feed has 126 seeds left in its current lap while peer-network has 5,147. When the flag comes off, all four daemons resume at once against a 39-key pool. That is the intended behaviour, but it is a much heavier first hour than the last two days have looked like.
  2. Term supply is still the binding constraint, and it is now a single-niche problem. Legal Services was 59% of everything worth contacting. There are no never-run terms and no rested proven terms left, 7,418 probes have been tried, and 0 were promoted this cycle. New seed material or a wider definition of who counts as a good lead is the only way past this, and it is the thing that caps every day until it changes.
  3. Decide the outlet for approved_hold. 4,660 creators prepped and waiting, none emailed. This has led the list for over a week and is still the only item that needs a decision from you rather than code.
  4. Build the needs_contact recovery engine. 5,783 creators, and it grew faster than the parked pool did today.
  5. Consider removing the RapidAPI path outright. Its retirement has now caused two separate outages on two consecutive days, in four different copies of the same check. Every copy is inert now, but the mirror is still wired in and still writes the stale file everything reads. Deleting it removes the whole class of failure, though that is a bigger change than an unattended cycle should make on its own.
  6. Have the hourly check-in watch sweep productivity, not just the campaign. Today's fix makes an idle daemon visible in the daily report, which is a day late. The same measurement in the hourly check would have caught this within an hour of it starting.
  7. Meter OpenRouter as a real cost. Still unmetered. The ledger reads $0 because it only counts Anthropic.
