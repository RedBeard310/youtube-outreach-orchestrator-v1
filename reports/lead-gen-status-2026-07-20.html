What we've been doing — the last 72 hours

Plain language: the two ways we find channels, what each one did, what broke, and what got fixed.

The two methods, in one paragraph each

Method 1 — Keyword search (the original). We keep a big list of phrases real people type into YouTube — "timeshare exit attorney", "pension planning uk", "buyers agent australia". The system searches each phrase, looks at who comes up, and scores them. The catch: a phrase burns out once you've seen everyone it can show you. So this method only keeps working if we constantly feed it new phrases. We get those by "harvesting" YouTube's and Google's autocomplete suggestions — the dropdown that appears as you type.

Method 2 — The channel graph (new, built 4 days ago). Take a creator we already know is good. Look at what YouTube recommends next to their videos — the "up next" panel on the right. Collect those channels, score them, repeat. It's the digital version of "who else is like this guy?" It needs no search phrases at all, which is exactly why it's still running while Method 1 is stuck.

The keyword engine: why it stopped

Two problems stacked on top of each other

1. Every phrase is used up. All 6,565 search phrases have been mined out — each one has been run until it stopped showing anyone new.

2. We can't get new phrases. The autocomplete harvest — how we top up the phrase list — is being blocked by Google at the network level. Every single request comes back "403 Forbidden". I tested it again just now, three days after first seeing it: still blocked.

The practical effect: the campaign wakes up every 30 minutes, finds it has no phrases to search, gives up, and sleeps. Over 72 hours that's 170 attempts, 91 of which failed outright, producing 27 good leads total. The graph produced 714 in the same window.

Why is Google blocking us? The server this runs on is a datacenter machine, and Google treats datacenter addresses as suspicious. Worth noting the two methods are affected very differently:

  • The graph method gets "slow down" (429) — a temporary speed limit that clears in about two minutes. We've hit it 416 times since the sweep started and it self-recovered every time by waiting and retrying. Harmless.
  • The keyword harvest gets "forbidden" (403) — a hard refusal that does not clear. It's been three days.

What we learned

  • Bigger creators are not better seeds. The relationship is a hump, not a slope — 1,000–5,000 subscribers is the peak. Too small and they're hobbyists; too big and YouTube recommends entertainment channels instead of peers.
  • We were limited by search phrases, not by available channels. There was never a shortage of creators to find — only a shortage of ways to ask for them. The graph method sidesteps that entirely, which is why it's still going.
  • Finding leads is cheap; the email is the hard part. A good lead costs about a penny to find. Roughly half of them have no verifiable email — that's now the real bottleneck, not discovery.
  • Real estate is the richest vein by a wide margin — about half of all good leads, because those creators cluster tightly and recommend each other.
  • Don't trust a small sample. Twice this week a number from a tiny sample pointed the wrong way. Both times the fix was to measure more before acting.

What needs a decision from you

Only one thing: the keyword engine stays dead until it can reach autocomplete again. That means residential IP addresses (roughly $1–5/month at this traffic volume, since the requests are tiny) or using a data provider's API instead. Everything else is running unattended and needs nothing.

Worth weighing honestly: the graph method is currently producing 26× more good leads than keyword search ever did in the same window, and it has 1,985 creators left to walk. Unblocking keyword search is worth doing, but it is not urgent.
