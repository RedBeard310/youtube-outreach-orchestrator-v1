The three questions

Q1Why did it find so few leads?

The term supply is genuinely exhausted. The finder only mines active search terms. Three layers of supply all bottomed out at once: (1) the proven-niche active pool is fully mined; (2) the 274-term never-run reserve that the 07-10 anti-starvation floor auto-reactivates from is completely consumed — the finder logged "active pool exhausted and NO never-run paused terms remain" on 175 / 175 sessions; (3) frontier discovery is saturated — Claude generates 40 candidate terms a pass but with 4,438 terms already in the table, every candidate dedupes to nothing: "0 unique, in-ICP candidates… nothing to write."

Net yield: 266 fresh pitchable across 375 passes = 0.71/pass (07-12 was ~3.0). This is the 07-09 "term-supply-limited, not channel-supply-limited" finding hardening into a hard ceiling. The lever is not more passes — it's a wider ICP or the needs_contact engine.

Q2Then why 168 hard-stops and a restart every ~2.5 min?

A dead-code back-off. When the finder aborts "No active terms" (exit 1) twice in a row, the campaign logs a hard_stop and stops the session — correct. campaign-loop.sh is supposed to then sleep 30 min on a hard wall (QUOTA_WAIT). It didn't. The classifier last_stop_reason() read the chronologically-last stop event, but the campaign always logs a done checkpoint (final verify+promote) after the hard_stop — so it always resolved to done and fell into the 20-second CLEAN_PAUSE.

Result: relaunch every ~2.5 min → 168 hard_stops across 172 sessions, all cycle, each re-running the reservoir pre-flight and a fresh Claude-sonnet discovery pass that produced nothing. The waste here was finder-side LLM tokens + wall-clock, not YouTube quota (the finder never even reached a search call). Fixed: classify by the stop cause, not the last line — the intended 30-min back-off now fires (≈12× fewer relaunches).

Q3Why did the health check report $0 and no anomaly?

Because the two things it watches both looked fine. The hourly checkin.ts fires a fix-agent only on (a) a fatal error signature or (b) approved_hold flat while the finder keeps producing. Neither tripped: "No active terms to process" is a clean abort, not a fatal signature (so fatal_signatures_today = []); and approved_hold was not flat — it crept up +115 as the verify lane drained the already-found backlog into parked, independent of new finding.

So the check-in saw a healthy, cost-free pipeline and never spent a dollar — which was the right call (a fix-agent can't invent net-new terms), but it means the instrumentation is blind to a 45% finder-failure rate. That's a real gap: the day's dominant pathology left no trace in the health signal. See rec #4.
