The three questions

Q1Why did everything stop at 00:49?

The OpenRouter account ran out of credits. Every thinking step in this pipeline runs through it: scoring a channel, working out who hosts it, filtering candidates, inventing new search terms. All of it started answering HTTP 402, which means "you have spent everything you paid for." Lifetime numbers off the account: $810 granted, $810.20 used. Not a bug, not a key problem, not a config problem. The tank is empty.

What happened next was mostly right. Two finder passes failed identically, which tripped the campaign's own hard-wall stop. The hourly check-in spotted the failure signature at 00:51Z, diagnosed it correctly, wrote a halt flag that names the cause and the fix, and stopped the loop. The graph sweep and the peer sweep both read that flag and put themselves to sleep. That is the system doing what it was built to do.

What was missing is any warning beforehand. The daily debrief meters Anthropic spend, which has read $0.00 every single day since the pipeline moved off Anthropic on 08-01. It is a fuel gauge on a tank nobody draws from. The account that actually pays for the work was watched by nothing at all, and it had been walking down at $6 to $8 a day in plain sight.

Q2Why did the biggest lane keep going for another six hours and produce nothing?

Because a dead scorer and a weird channel looked identical to it. The video-graph sweep already stops itself hard when the YouTube key pool runs out, and the comment in the code explains why: without it, the sweep "consumes its seeds and drains to a false complete with zero yield." An LLM failure got no such treatment. It counted the channel as failed and moved to the next one, silently, forever.

The evidence is unambiguous. In its session log, the last good lead landed in chunk 121. It ran to chunk 481. That is 360 chunks ≈ 7,200 seeds with zero good leads, while its own llm_calls counter sat frozen at 728 the entire way. Roughly 17 candidates cleared triage every chunk and every one of them failed to score.

The seeds are the real loss. A walked seed is checkpointed and never walked again. The lane went into the outage with about 14,500 seeds left and came out with 7,303, so it spent close to half its remaining road on nothing. It also had a start-up halt check, which never fired: this is the one long-lived process in the pipeline, and it had been up since 18:46 the previous evening, so its check was six hours stale by the time the flag was written.

Q3Was the day thin only because of the outage?

No, and this is the part worth carrying forward. All 163 good leads landed before 00:49. Compare like for like: the same 17.8 hours a day earlier produced 226. So the day was already 28% down before the account died. The outage cost the last quarter of the cycle. Something else cost the rest.

Two of the five seed books are finished and the third is nearly there. The recommended-videos feed has 1 seed left of 11,496. Peer-network has zero left of 11,361 and its refill is blocked behind the halt. Video-graph has 7,303, about half a day. Every one of these lanes is seeded by our own qualified leads, roughly 160 a day, while consuming more than 11,000 a day. A lane fed by its own output cannot outrun itself, and that ceiling arrives this week rather than eventually.

The keyword finder is no longer carrying anything either: 214 passes produced 16 good leads, about one per fourteen passes, against 136 from video-graph alone. And the health snapshot read the whole thing as an improvement, because seeds_advanced for video-graph came out 53.1% higher than the day before. It walked faster because it had stopped doing the expensive part.
