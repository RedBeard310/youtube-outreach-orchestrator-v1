Q2The snapshot said 11 people were lost. Were they?

Five were. The other six had already been emailed, and the push log simply never said so.

The field that answers "who was planned and got nothing" works by joining Siege's plan against Siege's log, and a log is narration, not a record. On 11-02 it lost a whole block: the push batch that ran at 11:37:55Z printed its working lines, no header, and no SENT lines at all. Two of the people it emailed appear nowhere in the file.

Six leads in total had a real row in the send ledger, with the sending mailbox, offer and batch id all matching the plan, and no SENT line anywhere. So the join called every one of them lost.

Two independent instruments now agree. Siege's own database rows for the day read 116 pushed, 3 failed, 2 dropped. And every email in the cycle that wasn't this repo's own send reads 116 against 116 emailed. The old arithmetic could produce neither number.

Q3The fleet doubled to 124 mailboxes. Why did 12 do the sending?

Yesterday's second recommendation was to check one field on Monday, because 58 brand-new mailboxes with no campaign attached and a failed SmartLead read look identical in the plan. It answered: 23 mailboxes were otherwise fit to send and sat out purely for want of a Siege campaign. That's the harmless version: about twenty minutes of setup, not a code fault.

But 23 doesn't explain 112 mailboxes sitting out, and nothing published the rest. The plan had it all along:

The 36 are a timer, four days from lifting themselves. They also settle a contradiction in this morning's numbers: the health gate reads 63 clear mailboxes and the plan reads 12 live. Those are two different gates measuring two different things. One bands on deliverability, the other suspends on age. So "63 clear" is not sending capacity, and yesterday's "roughly five times the capacity" read it as if it were.

Real capacity this cycle was 180 messages a day against an 870 ramp, with 104 follow-ups already owed. The ceiling was 123 slots, and the push used every one.
