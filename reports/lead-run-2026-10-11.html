The three questions

Q1Nothing sent. Is that a fault?

No. It was Saturday. siege-plan.timer runs Monday to Friday, so the snapshot reports sending_day: false and binding_constraint: not_a_sending_day, and no plan was written because no plan was due. The push log carries zeros rather than a gap, so this is a schedule reading its own calendar and not a run that died quietly.

This repo's own send path ran once and had nothing to fire: attempted=0 sent=0 failed=0. siege-plan.service's last run was Friday at 12:05Z and reported success, which is the run the 10-10 debrief covered. Nothing restarted, nothing escalated, no halt flag, and the 429 that stranded six people in two stopped campaigns on Friday did not recur because there was no push to recur in.

Q2The recovery lane found 1,042 contact points and parked two people. Which is it, a broken lane or a finished book?

A finished book, and this cycle proved it from both directions at once. Yesterday's debrief answered the first half: on a slice where no lead has a stored website, Brave answers, the strict ownership gate refuses the candidate because the page cannot be tied to that creator, and the methods have nothing to work with. That shape repeated here in pass 1: 125 searches, 105 real candidates, 97 refused by the gate, 8 accepted, and 90 points found on 125 leads.

The second half is new, and it is the stronger half. Passes 2, 3 and 4 walked slices where most leads already had a website on file, so the gate never came into it and nine more methods could run. They found 519, 523 and 592 points. Of those 1,634 points, 1,615 were already stored. The methods are not weak and resolution is not down: on the stretch of the book we can collect from, the collecting is already done. 19 new points off 450 leads is the whole remaining yield.

Both alarms went quiet on their own after pass 1 without anyone touching anything, which is the attribution fix behaving: the 11:11Z bloodhound_site_resolution_collapsed record reads attributed_to: "ownership_gate" and states plainly that spending nothing would change it. That is the first production outing of yesterday's noSiteCause(), and it named the cause correctly with no human in the loop. Lap 9 of a 2,515-lead book, stranded held at 3 for the 28th day, needs_contact down to 3,511.

Q3What was the biggest single thing going wrong in those passes?

A method that has never worked, running once per lead on a 12-second timeout. The new error instrument says the commonest error shape in the newest pass was 09-finra: FINRA N, 53 of its 442 errors, and the same shape topped the two passes before it at 44 and 28. Checked against the database, leads.contact_points holds zero rows from that method against 15,031 from 34-channel-page. Probed directly, its endpoint answers 404 Invalid resource: the API path was retired and nobody noticed, because an error buried in a 442-error total reads as weather.

It could not have worked anyway. The method searched a national register of financial advisers using hostFirstName, and of the 2,194 needs_contact leads that have one, 23 hold a full name. Searching FINRA for "Jane" returns hundreds of people, and filing one of them is exactly the stranger-filing bug the 2026-08-18 audit closed. So two independent faults, both silent, in a method that costs ~50 futile requests a pass.

Both are fixed below. The honest footnote: fixing it does not park anybody. The lane's yield is set by the book, not by this method. What it buys is ~50 fewer dead requests a pass and an error report where the top shape means something.
