The day had two moments in it and nothing in between. Ten emails at 00:20 Pacific, when an agent opened this repo and the session-start send fired, and 97 between 09:25 and 10:22 when Siege ran. Counted from outreach_processed_at in Postgres, which the email repo stamps on the push. Loading a lead into a SmartLead campaign is not the same as SmartLead delivering the email: its scheduler sends Monday to Thursday, 09:00 to 15:00 Eastern, so most of these will land over the coming week.

The three questions

1Where did 107 emails come from, and what is this repo still doing?

Siege sent 97 of them and the orchestrator sent 10. That split is now the normal shape, not an anomaly, and the interesting part is how Siege got its number. It works from its own board of 4,737 people in three tiers, holds 513 do-not-contact blocks, and ran against 37 sending mailboxes, of which 22 were live at 15 emails a day each. The mailbox ladder offered 185 slots. Siege took 100, because day 3 of its own ramp caps new emails at 100. It wrote 100, sent 97, skipped 1, failed 0.

The cost of those 97 emails was zero. Every offer that ran is a template with one or two per-lead fields, so the batch writers reported "No model calls, $0" on each one. That is the design working: the expensive research was paid for weeks ago and sits in the bundles on disk.

What this repo did was the 10 at midnight and the prep that filled the shelf those 97 were drawn from. That is a fair division of labour, but it means this repo's log now describes a tenth of the money path. The grounded metrics already count both figures side by side, which is why today's headline needed no hand-typed query for the first time.

2Why is OpenRouter still spending $2.50 a day with enrichment idle?

Because the auto-reply timer pays a model to re-read the same reply every two minutes. It ran 683 times inside this cycle, always in dry run, and 401 of those runs classified one address, tara@rehab-hq.com. Dry run means nothing is ever sent, so the reply never leaves the queue, so the next run classifies it again. The classifier is openrouter:deepseek/deepseek-v3.2, which is cheap per call and not free 401 times a day.

This matters because of what it replaced. Until 09-24 the account's spend was enrichment, and enrichment explained it. Enrichment processed zero leads this cycle and the finder logged zero calls, yet the account still spent $2.52. That is the same daily rate as yesterday with the actual work gone, which is the signal that something else on the account is the spender now.

The fix is small and it is not in a repo this agent may touch: hold the classification result against the reply's id, or back the timer off while it is in dry run. Either one takes the 683 runs down to a handful. Runway is 77 days at $2.54 a day on a $194.99 balance, so this is waste rather than a threat.

3Is the recovery lane still worth the hour a day it takes?

On this cycle's numbers, barely. Four collect passes, 600 lead readings, and they produced 26 contact points across 20 leads, 6 of which were email addresses. The verify half tested 5 addresses across 3 passes and was handed nothing on the other three. Nobody was parked. The book moved from 3,326 to 3,321, so 600 readings bought 5 leads for the second day running.

Set that against the same lane a week ago: 3,672 contact points and 405 email addresses in the seven days before this cycle, with a single day on 09-20 producing over a thousand. The cause is settled and the hourly check-in named it correctly four times today without escalating: the lane is on lap 6, re-reading leads it already emptied. Every pass this cycle was 100% re-walks, website resolution ran at 98 to 99%, and Brave refused nothing. It is a picked-over book, not a broken lane.

The alarm that has been saying so is about to stop. Its long baseline of "normal" has fallen from 27.7% to 7.3% across four firings as the degraded passes age into its eight-day memory, and today's fall read 45% against a 40% threshold. One more quiet day and the lane's decline stops announcing itself. That was predicted yesterday and it is now one day from happening.

What broke, what's fixed

Nothing faulted

Zero fatal error signatures. Zero halts. Zero quota stops. $0.00 of Anthropic spend against a $150 ceiling. The stranded counter, which is what would say a lead had fallen between the recovery lane's two halves, held at 3 for the thirteenth day. The YouTube key pool read 15 working of 66 for the thirteenth identical morning, which is the settled state and costs nothing while nothing is searching. Every timer fired on schedule. Discovery produced zero new channels, which is the instruction.

The report could not see the only lane still producing leads

Shipped: orchestrator 5eb5581. Typecheck clean, 86 tests pass, both new queries run live against Postgres.

The grounded metrics counted the recovery lane's output nowhere. The closest field, parked_today, is the change in the parked pool, and the Siege send path moves that pool too, so a zero there could mean the lane produced nothing or that it produced something the same day something else left. Today it read 0 for a lane whose yield had fallen 98% five days earlier, and nothing in the file said so.

There is now a recovery_lane block: contact points and email addresses this cycle, the same two figures for the seven days before it, the book depth, the stranded counter and the lap number. The measurement sits next to the two selectors it is derived from, so widening one cannot leave the number describing a pool that no longer exists. On this cycle it reads 26 points and 6 emails against 3,672 and 405 for the week before, which is the whole story in one line.

An empty send still read as a send nobody measured

Same commit.

Yesterday's fix taught the send log to say "zero failures" instead of "unknown" when a batch came back clean. It missed the case where there is no batch at all. Both session-start sends since the approved lane drained logged send_sent: null and printed sent=? failed=?, which is the shape a send that crashed before printing its tally writes. Nothing was attempted, so zero sent and zero failed are the facts, and an empty batch now says so. There is a test for it.

Enrichment finished, and the chain is idling because it has nothing to do

No action taken, none needed.

6,852 of the 6,860 parked leads carry a bundle on disk, which is 99.9%. The backfill chain launched no batches at all this cycle: its pool is three leads, two of them permanently excluded. It logged one thing all day, a transcript-provider limit at 12:40 that cleared nineteen seconds later on its own. The lasting reading is that the work Casey redirected everything into on 09-08 is done, and the shelf is the result: 6,570 researched leads with an email written or ready to write, which is about sixty days of sending at yesterday's rate.

Ten of Siege's sixteen offers cannot run, and that is now the ceiling on volume

Not broken, and not this repo's to fix. Recorded because it is the biggest lever left.

Siege checks every offer before it plans a day and prints exactly why each one is out. Six can run. The other ten are blocked on small, nameable things: no subject line in Notion, no batch writer script, zero of the two required follow-up bodies, an email type missing from the database vocabulary, a Notion status left on Paused, and in one case a writer that imports a path from Casey's old Mac. One is blocked on purpose, the 80% time offer, whose gate says the breakdown video does not exist yet.

Why it matters: the mailbox ladder offered 185 slots and the day's own ramp cap was 100. Offer variety is what lets the same 4,737-person board absorb more volume without emailing anybody twice, so each unblocked offer widens the road ahead of the cap.

Recommended next, ranked

  1. Stop paying to classify the same reply 400 times a day. The auto-reply timer ran 683 times this cycle in dry run and classified one address 401 of those times, because dry run never clears the queue. It is most of what OpenRouter spent on a day when nothing else was working. Hold the result against the reply id, or slow the timer while it is in dry run. The script is automator/scripts/smartlead-auto-reply.py, which is outside the five repos this agent may edit.
  2. Unblock more Siege offers. This is the volume lever now. Ten of sixteen are out for want of a subject line in Notion, a batch-writer script, two follow-up bodies, a database vocabulary row, or a Notion status left on Paused. Siege prints the exact reason for each one at the top of every run, so the list is already written. More live offers means the same 4,737-person board absorbs more sending without anyone being emailed twice.
  3. Decide what npm run send is for. It fired 10 emails at midnight and found nothing this morning, and it will keep finding nothing: its selector reads review_status = approved and that lane is fully sent. Siege reaches the 6,570-lead shelf by naming leads individually. Either accept that this repo's send command is retired in favour of Siege, or open its selector to approved_hold and accept that a deliberate hold gate becomes an automatic one. Casey's call. No agent should make it.
  4. Decide whether the hourly collect pass keeps its slot. Two days, 1,200 lead readings, 5 leads moved and nobody parked. It costs no money and it is not broken, so leaving it alone is defensible. But it is no longer a source of leads, and the honest reading is that the needs_contact pool of 4,236 needs a method it does not have rather than another lap of the ones it does.
  5. Expect the recovery lane's yield alarm to go quiet, and do not read that as recovery. Its memory of normal has fallen from 42% a week ago to 7.3% today as the degraded passes age into it. Today's firing cleared the threshold by five points. Tomorrow it probably will not fire at all, and the picked-over book will still be there.
  6. Watch Apify on 30 September. Four days out. It has logged the same resting line correctly twelve times a day and needs nothing done to it. Its last real run recovered 62 leads at about ten cents each.
  7. The 09-19 debrief is still unwritten, and its metrics file exists, so it can be written. It is the only gap left in the record.

The thing worth remembering from this cycle. The pipeline nearly doubled its record sending day, and almost none of the work happened inside the repo that calls itself the coordination loop. The division is working. What it changes is where to look: the numbers that describe the money path now live in the send ledger and the Siege log, and the orchestrator's job has quietly become keeping the shelf stocked and the counting honest.
