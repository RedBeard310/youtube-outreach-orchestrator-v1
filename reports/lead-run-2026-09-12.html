The three questions

Q1Why did only two leads park?

Because the recovery lane has almost nothing left to read. Its collect half picks leads out of needs_contact that have no contact point of any kind. That pool is now 253 leads. The lane runs four passes a day at 150 leads each, so it walks 600 lead-slots daily against a 253-lead book. It is reading the same leads over and over.

The logs say it plainly once you count them. Four collect passes covered 571 lead-slots but only 271 distinct leads. Passes three and four were handed a byte-identical list of 150. They returned "Collected 0 contact points from 0/150 leads" both times, which is what re-reading a lead you already read gets you.

The verify half is in the same state from the other direction: 6 passes, 30 lead-slots used out of 1,200 available, and the queue now stands at 4 leads. Neither half is broken. Both have finished their work.

Q2Where did the other 3,482 leads go?

They are still sitting in needs_contact, and nothing in the pipeline can reach them. This is the finding of the day and it is worth reading slowly.

The lane has two halves with two separate rules for picking leads. The collector skips any lead that already has a contact point, on the reasonable grounds that it has already been worked. The verifier only picks leads that have an email contact point, because an email is the only thing it can check.

A lead where the collector found a website, a phone number and an Instagram handle but no email satisfies neither rule. The collector will never look at it again. The verifier has nothing to rule on. 2,778 of the 3,735 leads are in exactly that position — 74% of the pool, permanently parked in the gap between two sensible rules.

This is the same shape as the Apify double-billing bug on 09-06, where a channel that published no email looked identical to a channel nobody had checked. Here it is inverted: a lead that was checked and yielded something useless looks identical to a lead that is finished.

2,281 of those stranded leads carry a website contact point that is already stored. Finding that website is the expensive step, the one Brave's cap is blocking. For those 2,281 it is already paid for and sitting in the database.

Q3So is raising the Brave cap still the top lever?

No, and the pipeline has been saying otherwise once an hour for a day. The two lane alarms both blame the search plan whenever a Brave refusal appears in the log, and that line is now printed at the top of every single pass, so the attribution had become automatic. They fired 24 times in this cycle telling Casey to raise the cap or add keys.

Brave really is refusing, at 402 Usage limit exceeded against the $5 monthly cap, and it does not reset until 1 October. But raising it now buys the 253 leads the collector can still see, not the 3,735 the pool report shows. An alarm that names the wrong fix is worse than no alarm when the fix it names costs money.

Yesterday's rewind fix did work, for the record. The lap_complete waiver fired at 21:02Z and released the lane off the 58-lead tail it had been pinned to, and batch sizes went straight back to a full 150. The lane then re-pinned itself at the top of the book instead, for a different reason, which is fixed below.

What happenedWhat shipped
The lane could not tell an empty book from a blocked one. Both site-resolution alarms blamed the Brave plan unconditionally, because the refusal line they key on is now printed on every pass. A new bloodhound_collect_book_drained observation fires when a day's passes cover the whole book more than once, and it suppresses the two older alarms when it does. Verified by re-running the check-in end to end: the new one fires with the right numbers, the old two go quiet.
A re-read that found nothing was re-read again, up to 48 times. Passes three and four were the identical 150 leads and both collected zero. The search-dead budget had 46 more of those queued, which is about twelve days. rewindWaiver now waives the second consecutive zero.
