The three questions

1How does a finished email disappear?

This morning's 07:20 send took 18 leads through writing and pushing. Four pushes went through in under two and a half seconds each. The rest hit fetch failed, which is what a connection timeout looks like from the inside. The email repo retries a transient failure once, after 60 seconds, and four more got through on that second attempt. Nine did not, because both of their attempts fell inside the same two-minute wobble. A tenth was correctly refused by the placeholder guard, which spotted [Agent Name] still sitting in the body.

Each of those ten was then written down as failed. That is the honest label, and it was also a trapdoor. The send queue selects leads at ready_data_scraped, enriched or email_drafted, and failed is in none of them. It is not in the tick's list of finished states either, so a manual npm run tick would have picked them up, and nobody has run a tick in months. Ten researched, written, verified-address leads went quiet, and no alarm anywhere watches for that.

The reading that makes this worse: after the failure the approved lane in Postgres read 0 ready, 0 drafted, 10 failed. A send queue of zero is also exactly what it reads when the lane has genuinely finished its work. The two states are the same number.

2What actually produced work this cycle?

Two things, and only one of them was a timer. The 47 emails are the whole output story. The pause that had held since 09-10 came off on 09-23, on a condition that is enforced in code rather than trusted: a mailbox may only send from warmup day 12 onward, measured per mailbox from the InboxKit snapshot. The 18 mailboxes bought on 09-19 reach day 12 on 30 September and cannot send before 1 October.

Enrichment did 124 leads, and 119 of them came from a run nobody scheduled. The VPS backfill chain ran three batches totalling five leads and logged no pending inflow, idling after each, which is correct because its queue is about one lead. Meanwhile a separate process started at 21:28 UTC on 09-23 has been working a 132-lead list from an interactive session's scratchpad file in /tmp, at roughly eleven leads an hour. It is doing exactly the work the discovery pause exists to fund, and it accounts for the money: OpenRouter moved $10.18 in the cycle, which at 119 leads is about 9 cents each and matches the 10 cents a lead measured on 09-16.

Worth knowing rather than worth fixing: no systemd unit owns that run. Its list of ids lives in a temporary folder belonging to a chat session. If it dies, nothing restarts it, and the chain's own log will keep saying "idling" while it does.

3Is the recovery lane still the bottleneck?

Yes, and it is unchanged from yesterday's reading. The lane walked its usual four collect passes and 600 lead slots. It found 73 contact points, up from 29 yesterday but against 1,063 on 09-20. Its hit rate per pass ran 7%, 9%, 5% and 3% against a 32-pass median of 42%. The book went from 3,344 leads to 3,331, so 600 readings bought 13 leads.

The cause is the one established on 09-22 and confirmed twice since: the lane is on lap 6 and is re-reading leads it already emptied. Every one of the four alarms it raised said so, attributing to book_rewalk and not to search credit, and none of them recommended spending money. Website resolution was fine all cycle, at 97% to 99% on three of the four passes. The alarm fix shipped 09-22 is now three days into proving itself.

The verify half did what it could with what it was handed: six passes, six addresses actually checked, five more rejected on ownership before spending a credit, four flips into approved_hold, plus one more from the hourly re-score. That is the whole +5. Two of the six passes were handed nothing at all.

What broke, what's fixed

A network blip on the last step deleted ten finished emails from the pipeline

Shipped: orchestrator ce5abf1. 87 tests pass, 11 of them new, typecheck clean, verified against the live database.

The send queue now also considers leads at failed, but only on the evidence in their own fields. A written subject and body means the work is paid for and the lead resumes at the push, which is exactly what email_drafted already meant. A bundle on disk with no draft means it resumes at compose, which is exactly what ready_data_scraped already meant. A failed lead with neither never got that far and stays out, because pulling it in would quietly turn a send into a find-and-enrich run, and that coupling was removed on purpose on 2026-07-17.

This is the same reading that effectiveStatus() in the email repo already makes when it decides where to restart a lead. The two repos disagreed: one knew how to resume a failed lead and the other would never hand it one. They agree now.

Verified live, not just in tests. Before the change the send queue returned 0. After it, npm run send:dry returns exactly the ten stranded ids and nothing else. The fix is retroactive by nature: it rescues leads already sitting in the trapdoor, not just future ones.

A send that loses more than half its batch looked identical to a clean one

Same commit.

The email repo prints a tally at the end of a run and then exits 0, because one lead failing is deliberately not fatal to the batch. So the orchestrator's log line for this morning read send_attempted=18 send_exit=0, and that is all it read. A run that sent 18 and a run that sent 8 wrote the same line.

The send driver now reads that tally and records send_sent and send_failed in the JSONL, prints them on the closing line, and says so loudly when more leads failed than went out. Nobody has to read 500 lines of child output to find out a batch went badly.

Nothing else faulted

Zero fatal error signatures. Zero halts. Zero quota stops. $0.00 of Anthropic spend against a $150 ceiling. The stranded counter, which is the number that would say a lead had fallen into the gap between the recovery lane's two halves, held at 3 for the eleventh day. The YouTube key pool read 15 working of 66 for the eleventh identical morning, which is the settled state and costs nothing while nothing is searching. Discovery produced zero new channels, which is the instruction.

Yesterday's fix is holding too. The walking in place alarm fired 13 times about one window on 09-23 and zero times this cycle.

The money, and a number that changed a lot

OpenRouter runway went from 191 days to 20. The balance fell from $210.16 to $199.98, which is $10.18 in a day against $1.10 the day before. Nothing is wrong: enrichment restarted, and enrichment is what OpenRouter is for. At the measured rate of about 10 cents a lead it is the cheapest part of this pipeline. It is worth saying plainly anyway, because the runway figure on the last three debriefs was a number nobody had to think about and now it is about three weeks.

For scale, writing the whole shelf would be a different bill. The 6,675 finished leads each still need an email composed, and composing is a separate model call from enriching. There is no measured per-email cost yet, because the cycle's spend is fully explained by the 119 enrichments. Getting that number is worth doing before any large send, and it needs one send with a per-task cost log rather than a guess.

The thing worth remembering from this cycle. The pipeline spent thirteen days where zero output was the correct answer, and everyone learned to read zero as normal. On the first day that stopped being true, ten finished emails fell through a gap and the queue still read zero. The label failed was accurate and the trapdoor was that no query included it. A status that describes what happened is not the same as a status something acts on, and the gap between those two is where work goes to die quietly.

Deliberately not done: no email sent by this agent, no discovery lane touched, no .env or secret read or written, no alarm threshold moved, no halt flag written, and the unscheduled enrichment run was left alone rather than killed or adopted. The loop is left running for the next cycle.
