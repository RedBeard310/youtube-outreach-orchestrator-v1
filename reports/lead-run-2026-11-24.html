An empty file, not an empty library. The wording on the board is the send program's summary after the last offer in a person's rotation declined to write, and the writer had already said why, two lines earlier in the same log.

Every one of the ten was turned down by the same offer, love-your-channel-short, for the same stated reason: no safe enemy in the bank. That offer opens by siding with the creator against something they push back on, and it reads that from the enemies bank in the creator's research bundle, a short file listing the people, ideas and practices the creator argues against, each with a quote. No enemy in the file, no opening line, no email.

Q2Why did 65 emails reach nobody new?

Because the plan is still spending every slot on people we have already contacted, and there were only 89 slots to spend.

Repeat offers are deliberate and have been since 2026-10-07, so this is not a fault on its own. The number that makes it worth reading is the pairing: 60 people emailed, 60 of them repeats, and 4,854 finished emails sitting unsent for people nobody has ever contacted. The send program fills slots by tier, and the top tier ran out of never-emailed people back on 10-13, so its re-offer queue takes the room. 1,346 people were ready and waiting for a slot: 219 in the top tier, 714 in the next, 413 below that.

The slot ceiling is the other half. Monday had 89 slots across 17 live mailboxes out of a fleet of 124, against a warmup allowance of 870. So the constraint is not the ramp and not the shelf. It is how few mailboxes can send and which people the queue puts in front of them. Both of those levers live in the send program, which this repo does not touch on purpose.

Fixes shipped this cycle

The biggest loss on the board now names its cause instead of its conclusion

The row "every offer that can run has already been made to them" shipped on 11-21 and led the loss table on all three sending days since. It reads as an offer library with nothing left to say, which sends a reader straight at writing more offers. That is the wrong repair. The phrase is the send program's own summary after the last offer in someone's rotation declined to write, so it states a conclusion and never a cause.

The cause was in the log the whole time, in a column format nothing here could read: 

| Doctor Carl Truesdale    skipped    no safe enemy in the bank (0 found, all ruled out)
rectI4slX3LejGPG7: love-your-channel-short wrote nothing and there is no other offer
  for them today (every offer that can run has already been made to them)

Both lines are the same person at the same moment. The loss row now carries the offer that ran dry and the precondition it failed on, and a new offer_exhaustion block holds the full verdict, which offer it belongs to, and how many leads that offer said it about, so a thin reading shows as thin instead of confident.

The pairing is per offer and not per person, deliberately. The writer keys its refusal by channel name, the summary line keys by lead id, and the summary lines print in one group at the end of the planning phase rather than beside the batch that caused them. No honest per-person join exists in that file, so the offer's dominant verdict inside the day is what gets claimed, and an offer with no verdict in the window keeps the bare old wording rather than borrowing somebody else's reason. The verdict has its per-lead count collapsed to N, because "(0 found, all ruled out)" would otherwise split one precondition into a row per lead.

Verified: ran against Monday's live log and plan and it reproduces the published figures exactly, 89 planned, 85 approved, 23 lost, 13 lost before the push, with the top row now naming love-your-channel-short and its empty bank. Seven new test cases cover an offer with no verdict, two spellings of one precondition, a person emailed by a later batch, the batch summary lines that hold the word "skipped" and are not verdicts, and an unreadable plan reading unknown rather than zero. Full suite passes at 438 cases, type check clean.
