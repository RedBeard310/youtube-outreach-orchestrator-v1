The three questions

Q1Why did 167 of 503 planned emails not go out?

The clock ran out on the whole unit, not on the push. siege-plan.service is a oneshot with five ExecStart steps, and TimeoutStartSec covers all five together. It was set to 3600. The run started at 10:25:34Z and systemd killed it at 11:25:34Z, to the second, with 216 of the plan loaded. Of 239 batch directories written for the day, only 110 ever reached their email builder, so 129 batches had no email to push.

A second push ran by hand from 15:28Z to 16:00Z and loaded 120 more, then finished normally. That left 167. Casey raised the ceiling to 21600 seconds, six hours, and committed it at 16:08Z (174220f). The live unit confirms it: TimeoutStartUSec=6h, no daemon-reload pending.

Six hours is enough, with room. The two runs together loaded 336 emails in 84 minutes of pushing, which is 4.0 emails a minute. A 503-email plan needs about two hours at that rate, plus the nineteen minutes of pre-push steps. Today's 10:15Z run is the first one under the new ceiling and is the test.

This is the same push_killed shape as 10-01 and 10-02, and it is not the slowness those cycles blamed. The pool-leak fix that took a batch from 31s to 1.9s (7fd02b6fd) is deployed and holding. The remaining cost really was the plan being bigger than the hour.

Q2The recovery lane logged "0 contact points from 0/150 leads". Is it dead?

No. It is a full re-walk of a picked-over book, and the methods worked fine. The proof is direct: dry-running five of the exact leads from that pass turned up 21 contact points, every single one of them already stored. The insert is ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING and reports only rows it really wrote, so a lead whose points are all on file scores zero and reads in the log exactly like a lead nothing was found on.

Everything else about the pass was healthy. Website resolution, which nine of thirteen collection methods depend on, came in at 3 no-site in 150 with zero Brave refusals. The lane is on lap 8 of a 2,520-lead book at 100% re-walk. Stranded held at 3 for the 25th day, so no selector gap has reopened.

That ambiguity was unreadable and is now fixed in both repos. The collect pass prints what the methods found alongside what was new, and names its error shapes instead of counting them, so a dead method chain and a picked-over book no longer print the same line. The grounded snapshot carries the same two numbers, so the next cycle does not have to re-run the lane by hand to answer this.

Q3The pause was meant to fund enrichment. Is there any enrichment left to fund?

None. The job is finished and the shelf it filled is now draining faster than anything refills it. Enrichment stands at 7,584 of 7,594 bundled, 99.9%. The chain launched two batches all cycle, one lead each, and spent the rest of the day writing no pending inflow — idling. One of the two failed on unparseable model output at stage 08; the chain moved straight on to the next lead, which succeeded.

Inflow and outflow are now three orders of magnitude apart. The free recovery lane added 3 addresses on 3 people and parked one lead. The paid Apify lane added nothing, because $10 of its $100 month is left and all $10 is the untouchable reserve, so $0 is spendable until 31 October. Against that, the push spends 336 to 503 people a day across five sending days.

So the arithmetic on the shelf is the real headline. 6,557 never-emailed people at 336 a day is 3.9 weeks. At the full 503-email plan, which is what the six-hour ceiling is meant to deliver, it is 2.6 weeks. Fixing the push makes the pipeline better at emptying the bank, and nothing is putting anything back in.
