Q2Friday planned 618 and the shelf holds 6,015 people. Why did Monday plan 76?

Follow-ups, and it was not a fault. Every offer is a three-email sequence on days 0, 4 and 10, and SmartLead only sends Monday to Thursday, so a weekend's worth of due follow-ups lands on Monday on top of Monday's own. 1,495 follow-ups came due against a total allowance across the live fleet of 855 a day, and 50 of the 57 live mailboxes had no room left at all for a new person.

The fleet was in the best shape it has been in all week. 57 of 58 inboxes live, against 54 on Thursday and 50 on Friday, and the highest daily allowance of the three days. The number that collapsed was the leftover after the follow-up bill was paid, and the snapshot could only show that leftover, which reads exactly like a suspension wave. That gap is now closed: the debrief carries the allowance, the follow-up bill and how many mailboxes are full.

So Friday's 618 was borrowing, not throughput. The honest ceiling is arithmetic off Siege's own numbers: 855 messages a day across four sending days is 3,420 messages a week, and each new person costs three of them, so the fleet supports about 1,140 new people a week, roughly 285 a sending day. The shelf's 6,015 is therefore about 5.3 weeks of real sending, which is the same answer the 277-a-day figure gave, reached a different way.

That derivation is mine, not a measured field: it assumes every started sequence runs all three emails and nobody replies out of it, so treat 1,140 as an upper bound. The measured fields are the allowance, the bill and the full-mailbox count.
