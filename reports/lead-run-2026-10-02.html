The shape of the day

One lever moved everything, and it is a billing cycle. A creator's email address usually sits behind a button on their YouTube About tab that only a person can click. One paid service clicks it for us, at about seven cents a channel, and bills monthly. October's allowance opened on the 1st, the lane spent it as fast as it could, and by 02:22Z on the 2nd it had nothing left to spend and went to sleep.

That is not a fault. The lane is built to front-load, and at thirteen cents a parked lead it is the cheapest route to a sendable lead this pipeline has. The thing worth saying out loud is the arithmetic: the whole of October's paid recovery happened on the 1st and the 2nd. For the remaining 29 days the only lane adding leads is the free one, and the free one found six addresses yesterday.

Downstream, every newly parked lead needs its channel researched before an email can be written, so the research chain ran eight batches and finished 582 leads, then hit the end of its queue at 06:35Z. The shelf is caught up: 7,577 of 7,587 parked leads have a research bundle on disk. Research is what spends OpenRouter credit, which is why the daily bill went from $19.52 to $50.91 and the runway from 8.5 days to 2.2.

The send went out unattended for the first time, because Casey gave Siege standing approval on 30 September. It loaded 135 of a planned 150. The 15 that did not go were not refused or skipped. Systemd killed the push mid-batch when the service hit its one-hour time limit.

No, and that is the standing order. Discovery has been off since 2026-09-08 on Casey's instruction, so 0 new channels is the intended state. Every sweep, the keyword harvest and the campaign loop stayed stopped, and the flag holding them off is untouched. Ninth debrief in a row saying this.

2 Did we recover leads we already had?

Yes, and by a distance. 742 contact points across 717 leads, 707 of them email addresses. The parked pool went 7,062 to 7,587 and the waiting pool fell to 3,517. This lane only got its own measurement on 26 September, and 742 is two and a half times the best reading since (292, yesterday).

Almost all of it was bought. 701 of the 742 points came from the paid lane. The free pass found 41 points, of which 6 were email addresses. For scale, the free pass found 160 points and 25 addresses across the whole previous week, so it has not collapsed, it is just small. It is on its seventh full walk of a 2,525-lead book, which means most of what it reads it has already emptied. No money fixes that.

Two things that are not wrong, so nobody re-diagnoses them: both Brave Search keys are still at their spending cap, and it barely cost anything, because only 2% of the leads the cursor read this cycle lacked a stored website. And the stranded count held at 3 for the nineteenth day, so no selector gap has reopened.

What broke, what's fixed

1. Systemd killed the send 15 emails short, and nothing said so

Diagnosed here. The fix is one line in a root-owned unit file, so it needs Casey.

Siege runs as one systemd service with five steps in a row: sync the mailbox warmup, plan the day, record the standing approval, push, then write a report. All five share a single sixty-minute budget (TimeoutStartSec=3600). The first three steps took about two minutes. The push got the rest and needed slightly more than it had.

Why it needs an hour for 150 emails: the push runs one small batch at a time, one to three emails each, and each batch pays about 35 seconds to start a fresh npx tsx process. 86 batches of startup is the hour. Yesterday's debrief measured exactly this and called it "not a fault, just slow". It became the fault the first day the plan was big enough to cross the line.

What that cost: 15 emails unsent and never retried, and the fifth step, the report, never ran at all. What reached a reader was "135 of 150", which reads like a few leads a writer could not finish. The service sat in a failed state for twenty hours and no part of the pipeline mentioned it.

Shipped (da7a159, orchestrator): the daily snapshot now reads the service's own verdict and reports push_unit_result: "timeout" with binding_constraint: "push_killed" instead of the softer partial_push. It only trusts a verdict from a run that started inside the cycle window, so Friday's outcome can never be reported against Sunday, and it reports nothing rather than guessing when the probe fails. Local systemd only, no network.

What Casey needs to do: raise that one number. TimeoutStartSec=3600 in /etc/systemd/system/siege-plan.service wants to be TimeoutStartSec=4h (or 0 to leave it uncapped), then systemctl daemon-reload. It is root-owned, so an agent cannot do it.

2. The free recovery lane was being credited with the paid lane's 701 emails

Shipped: fd88b5a, orchestrator.

Both lanes write their findings into the same table, leads.contact_points. The free pass files under its method name, the paid one files under apify:endspec/..., and the daily snapshot counted one total. So the headline read 742 contact points, 707 emails, which looks like the best day the free lane has ever had. The free lane found six.

That mattered the same day, not in principle. Apify's month ran out at 02:22Z. The next 29 days belong to the free pass alone. A reader who believed the lane had just produced 707 addresses would draw the exact opposite conclusion about what happens next.

This was caught by hand in yesterday's debrief, which is why it is now in code: the snapshot carries free_email_points_added and paid_email_points_added side by side, the totals are unchanged so nothing already reading them shifts meaning, and the note on the block tells a reader which pair is the lane. The test is the source prefix, so a new free method needs no change and a new paid writer has one list to add itself to.

Verified: tsc clean, gatherer run end to end on live data, the block returns 41 free and 701 paid against a hand-written SQL count of the same window.

3. A YouTube outage charged ten good leads an enrichment attempt

Shipped: fd88b5a, orchestrator.

A lead gets three tries at enrichment and is then dropped from the pool for good, which stops one genuinely broken channel looping forever. The chain already refunds a whole batch when the batch obviously died of infrastructure: more than 100 failures, or zero successes with two or more failures. Neither guard can see a batch that worked.

The 15:35 batch enriched 92 leads and failed 12. Ten of those 12 died on 503 backendError from YouTube's videos endpoint, which is Google being briefly unavailable and has nothing to do with the channel. The batch looked healthy, so each of those ten quietly paid a third of its lifetime. Three such minutes across a lead's life and a perfectly good lead is excluded from enrichment forever, with no log line saying why.

Nothing was lost this time. All ten came round again later in the cycle and enriched fine, because the pool was big enough to re-offer them. The leak is cumulative, not immediate, and it scales with volume: 23 attempts were charged this cycle and the inflow is now 500 leads a day when the paid lane runs.

The fix refunds per lead rather than per batch, by striking the transiently-failed ids from the batch's own ids file, which is exactly what the attempt counter reads. It is deliberately narrow: only known-transient signatures qualify (upstream 5xx, dropped connections, rate limits), so an unrecognised failure still pays and a genuinely broken lead still ages out. Each lead is capped at three refunds across its whole life, so nothing can become immortal by failing transiently forever.

Verified: replayed against the four real run logs from this cycle. The 15:35 batch refunds exactly the 10 and charges the other 2, which are a deleted video, a bad comment request, a missing channel and a database encoding error, every one of them about that lead. Re-running the same log refunds nothing further, so a replay cannot double-spend the cap. With the ledger pre-loaded at the cap the ids file is left untouched and the run says capped=10.

4. The allowance figure in the daily snapshot was always one batch out of date

Shipped: 0208785b8 (email repo) and the orchestrator commit beside it.

Found while writing this debrief, which is how it earns a section. The snapshot reported $15.69 left and $5.69 spendable, and priced 43 more recoverable leads on top of that. The lane had been resting on $10.00 left, $0.00 spendable since 02:22Z, seven hours earlier.

The cause is small and worth knowing. Every tick asks Apify for the live figure, but only a tick that launches a batch leaves that figure on disk, in the batch's run log, and it writes it before the batch spends. So the freshest number the snapshot could find was the one taken at the start of the last batch of the month, $5.69 short of the truth. Nothing was wrong with the lane. The reader was.

That number is the top budget decision in the pipeline today, so it should not lag a batch. The lane now writes its reading every tick, resting or not, and the snapshot prefers it when it is newer, falling back to the run log otherwise and saying which one it used. The snapshot for this cycle now reads $10 left, $0 spendable, 0 channels affordable, 0 projected leads, which is the honest answer and the reason recommendation 2 below is phrased the way it is.

Verified: tsc clean, selftest ALL PASS, loop script syntax checked, and the gatherer re-run against the lane's real 06:22Z resting reading returns the figures quoted above with budget_read_from: "loop_tick".

What worked, and is worth not re-examining

Yesterday's price-brake fix earned its keep on day one. The paid lane stops itself for the month after two batches in a row above twenty cents a recovered lead. The 00:22 batch flipped only 8 addresses itself because the recovery lane's own timer had already parked 30 of them mid-scrape. Under the old accounting that reads as $0.71 a lead and a strike. With yesterday's fix it reads 38 recovered at $0.150, inside the ceiling, and the lane is still running with zero strikes.

Yesterday's budget projection was close. It predicted the remaining allowance would buy about 982 channels and park about 500 leads. It bought 881 and parked 472.

The paid lane stopped itself cleanly. Three times since 02:22Z it has logged $10.0 left, $10 reserved, $0.0 spendable buys 0 channels, waiting for the cycle to reset. It is resting, not broken.

The lesson. This was a good day by every number on the page, and all three faults in it are the same shape: a figure that arrived without its cause. 742 contact points read as a free lane firing on all cylinders when it had found six addresses. "135 of 150 sent" read as a handful of leads a writer could not finish when it was really a process killed by a timer. A healthy batch of 104 read as healthy while ten good leads quietly spent a third of their enrichment budget on a Google outage. None of today's fixes send an extra email or save a dollar. They make the next version of each of these say what happened.
