Three times yesterday the check-in told Casey that website searches were failing and he should raise the Brave Search cap. Every lead in the last two passes had been read before. The lane collected 47 contact points against 1,063 the day before.

The seventh bar is the pass that finished the lap, at 06:01Z on 09-21. It came up short at 104 leads instead of 150, which is how the lane signals it has reached the end of its list. The three bars after it are this cycle, and they are the same list being read a second time.

The three questions

1Did the day add anything to the pile?

Almost nothing. +7 leads into approved_hold, taking the pool from 6,846 to 6,853. The recovery lane's own log claims 13 parks across three re-score passes, and the pool grew by 7, so six leads left it for other reasons in the same window. Both readings say the same thing: this was the weakest ordinary day of the pause.

Nothing else contributed. Apify is sitting on its $10 reserve with half a cent spendable and cannot run until the billing cycle rolls on 30 September. Discovery is paused, so no new channels arrived.

2Why did the lane stop producing?

Because it has read its whole list once and is now reading it again. The lane keeps a book of 3,347 leads it wants contact details for, and walks it in laps of about five days. Lap 5 closed at 06:01Z on 09-21. Lap 6 opened right behind it and went back to the top.

Every lead in the last two passes had been read before: 150 of 150, twice over. And the leads themselves are largely spent. Of the 3,347 in the book, 3,016 already hold some contact detail that is not an email, which means the lane has already worked them and taken everything it could get. Only 331 have never yielded anything at all.

The output matches. Across the cycle the lane collected 47 contact points against 1,063 the day before, and of those 47, fifteen were email addresses. A second reading of a worked lead is close to free and close to worthless.

3Did anything leave?

No, for the thirteenth day. This morning's send fired at 18 leads and sent none. Checked in the database rather than trusting an exit code: zero leads reached sent_to_smartlead in the whole cycle. The email pause set on 2026-09-10 refused the batch before a word was composed.

The shelf is now 6,586 leads researched and ready to write, 96% of everything parked, up from 6,570. It is the largest thing in the pipeline and it is waiting on a decision, not on work.

The alarm named a remedy that would have bought nothing

Three times this cycle the check-in raised bloodhound_collect_yield_degraded, and all three told Casey the same thing: "The lane logged a Brave Search refusal, so website resolution is the likely cause: raise the plan cap or add keys." Here is what those passes actually did.

The three passes that were blamed on failed website searches found websites for 119 of 150 leads, and only one of those 119 gave up anything. The websites were there. The leads had simply been emptied already.

Why it got this wrong: the only test was whether the words "Brave Search API key" appeared anywhere in the tail of the log. One Brave key has sat at its $5 monthly cap since early September, so that line now prints at the top of every single pass. A test that is always true is not a test.

This is the third outing of one bug class, and the repo has written the rule down twice already: an alarm that names a remedy costing money must be sure the remedy is the constraint. On 09-12 it fired 24 times blaming Brave for a drained book. The fix then added a suppressor, but it only triggers when the day's passes cover the whole book more than once. The book is 3,347 leads and a day covers 0.18 of it, so the suppressor could not fire and the misattribution walked back in through the gap.

What broke, and what is fixed

✓The alarm now reads the pass it is judging, not the file it sits in

Shipped in the orchestrator (9dc4eb6), src/recovery/bloodhound-lane.ts and scripts/autopilot/checkin.ts. A new reading, collectPassAttribution(), answers two questions about the pass being judged, both free and both off lines the log already writes:

Did website searches actually fail in this pass? If the pass resolved sites, search is not what broke, whatever a leftover Brave line says elsewhere in the file.
Has the lane read these leads before? The share of the batch that appears earlier in the log. This is measured against the whole log rather than a window, which makes it a lap-scale reading and not a repeat of the existing stuck-cursor check, which sat correctly silent at 1.36× while this read 100%.

The alarm now picks one of three explanations instead of always picking the expensive one, and it stops telling Casey to spend money when money would not help. The closing line changes with it: "the remedy is a spend call a fix agent cannot make" becomes "nothing is faulting, and no code change makes a read-out book yield again."

Verified: typecheck clean, 69/69 tests (6 new, including the regression this exists for and a case where a real search outage must still read as one), and the check-in run end to end against the live logs. It now reports: "Website resolution is NOT what broke here: this pass resolved a site for 119 of 150 leads (79%). A Brave refusal line is printed on every pass because one key sits permanently at its monthly cap, so it is not evidence about this pass... 150 of the 150 leads (100%) have been collected from before in this log, and only 1 of the 119 with a working site produced anything." It exited healthy and escalated nothing.

✓Yesterday's fix proved itself in production the next day

The 09-21 change stopped a slow decline from erasing its own baseline, by remembering what normal looked like over 32 passes rather than 8. This cycle it was load-bearing. The 02:11Z alarm read against the long baseline of 45% and said so in its own text: the short 8-pass baseline had already sagged to 37%. Under the old rule the day's final pass, which produced one contact point from 150 leads, would have been measured against a baseline the collapse itself had dragged down.

The same commit's per-pass keying also held. Three yield alarms for three distinct passes, no hourly repeats, against 7 and 9 copies of two alarms the day before.

!Correcting yesterday's forecast

Yesterday's debrief said to expect about 19 parks a day until Brave is funded. That was wrong in its reasoning even though the number was in the right neighbourhood. The lane fell to 7, and funding Brave would not have prevented it.

Brave still matters, but it is smaller than it looked. Of the 3,347 leads in the book, 620 would need a paid search to find a website; the other 2,727 already have one on file or linked from their channel. Money buys attempts at 620 leads, most of which have already been walked past once without a website being found.

!The session-start send fired again, at 18 leads

Still unexplained, and the number is climbing: 14 leads on 09-20, 15 on 09-21, 18 today. Something runs a live npm run send the moment an agent opens this repo, inside the debrief unit's own process tree, and no hook exists in any settings file that can be read.

It is harmless only because the pause holds. If the pause lifts before the trigger is found, the next debrief agent sends whatever is ready without being asked.

!Three debriefs are still missing

09-17, 09-18 and 09-19 remain unwritten, correctly named again by the missing_debriefs field. Their metrics are on disk, so they can be written after the fact. Today's agent authenticated normally, so the original fault was the expired login and not the script.
