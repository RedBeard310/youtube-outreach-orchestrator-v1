The three questions

Q1Did switching off discovery cost anything?

Almost nothing, and the numbers say why. Parking came in at +133 against yesterday's 142, a difference well inside the normal day-to-day wobble. Only 28 of the 133 were channels found during this cycle. The other 105 came out of the pile we already had, through enrichment and the recovery lane.

That is the whole case for the pause in one line. Finding channels and parking leads had already come apart from each other. The recovery lane has been the bigger producer since early September, and it does not need a single new channel to keep going. It has 3,789 creators waiting in needs_contact, all of them already found and scored.

Q2Why did every lead in the send fail?

Three faults, stacked. Two of them broke the send, and the third turned a bad send into lost work.

One: the repo stopped reading half its own config. env-storage keeps two files, a shared bank of keys plus a per-repo fragment holding that repo's own settings, and build-envs.sh glues them into each repo's generated .env. When that generated file was deleted from the email repo on 2026-09-01, the loader kept reading the shared bank and silently lost every fragment-only value. This is the second outage from it. The first was ENRICHMENT_REPO_PATH on 09-02, which stalled the backfill chain for days and got patched one variable at a time. This time it took all 16 SmartLead campaign IDs, so nine leads had nowhere to be pushed.

Two: a skill folder was renamed a month ago and nobody told the code. The composer for the nick_saraev variant reads its instructions off disk at ~/.claude/skills/nick-saraev-cold-email. The 2026-08-06 skills reshuffle renamed that folder to cold-email-nick-saraev. Two leads died on ENOENT before writing a word.

Three, and this is the expensive one: the campaign ID was looked up after the email was written. Nine leads paid for a full compose, roughly a minute of model time each, and then hit the missing ID at the push step. Worse, every failure was written back as outreach_status = failed, and failed is not in the send queue. Those 11 enriched, ready-to-mail leads fell out of npm run send entirely and into the tick's prep queue, where they would have been re-enriched from scratch.

Q3The key pool question from yesterday: did the pause answer it for free?

Yes, and it rules out the explanation that was ours. The last two debriefs both read 15 working of 66 twenty-odd minutes after the reset and could not tell three explanations apart: Google refills late, those projects had their quota cut, or our own sweeps wake at the reset and burn the pool within seconds.

Last night was an accidental controlled test. 59 keys were already quota-dead at 22:40Z, and then discovery stopped and nothing of ours touched a YouTube key for over eight hours. The probe at 07:20Z, twenty minutes past the reset, still found 50 exhausted and 15 working. Only about eight of the 59 came back.

So our early-morning spending was never the main story. Either the refill lands well after 07:00Z for those projects, or their quota really has been reduced. The free way to tell them apart is audit-key-projects.sh, which reads each key's project number without spending a unit. There is no rush: discovery is off, and the lanes that are still running use one-unit calls and free page reads, not the hundred-unit searches that drain a pool.
