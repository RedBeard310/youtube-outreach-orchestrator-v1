The three questions

Q1The shelf fell 800 on the day we sent 782 emails. Are those the same 800?

No, and they can't be. Sending leaves a person exactly where they were. The review_status stays approved_hold and only the outreach_status moves to sent_to_smartlead, which is why the shelf count and the never-emailed count drift apart a little more with every push. So a push of 782 and a drop of 800 lining up on the same day is a coincidence, and acting on it would have sent the next session looking for a bug in the send path.

What actually moved it was your fit audit. At 16:26Z one statement set 881 people to rejected and zeroed their score. Each row carries [NOT ICP 2026-10-13] at the front of its notes, naming which of five patterns it failed: selling a product rather than teaching, showing off rather than teaching, being a direct competitor, selling only low-priced things, or being a faceless channel living on view income. Two minutes later 684 live email sequences belonging to those people were stopped, every one successfully.

Nothing about that is broken, and the enforcement holds. Siege only ever picks from three statuses, approved_hold, approved and needs_contact, so rejected drops out of every future plan on its own. There is a working undo script. The problem was purely that no instrument could see an event of that shape, so the one number on offer pointed at the wrong lane.

The audit also overwrote updated_at on 436 people who had been emailed hours earlier, so their send timestamps are gone. Harmless here, since outreach_processed_at is the field anything reads, but worth knowing before trusting updated_at as a history.

Q2782 emails in one push. Did anything get hurt at that size?

Almost nothing, and the parts that did hold up are the ones we fixed earlier this month. SmartLead refused not one email. Its account-wide limit of 200 requests a minute was hit four times during the push, on listCampaignMailboxes, and the retry added on 10-05 absorbed every one. That is the fix doing exactly the job it was built for, under four times the load that exposed it.

One batch of five people died anyway, and it was our own gap. At 11:17:15Z the sign-off guard that runs before a batch's first lead asked SmartLead for the campaign's sequence, got the same rate-limit refusal, and had no retry, so the owner-or-youtuber batch stopped with pushed=0. The 10-05 fix wrapped the add-lead and mailbox calls and left the sequence file on a bare request. Fixed today in the email repo, with tests that drive the real functions so a future rewrite fails in the suite rather than on a push.

The other eight losses were ours on purpose. Our writer's quality gate refused seven drafts after their retries and the do-not-contact check caught one more person at the door. Two of the seven were refused for an em dash outside the sign-off, which is the same copy rule that has now cost drafts on three separate days. The others were a phrase lifted from a golden example, a short email, a consultant abstraction, and a missing required line. Every one of those is a fix to the writing skill, not to the push.
