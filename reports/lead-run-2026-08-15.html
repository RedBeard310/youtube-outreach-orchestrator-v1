The three questions

Q1Did yesterday's fix actually work?

Yes, and the number to check was the one named yesterday. Hard stops went from 38 of 38 sessions to 4 of 17. The keyword lane went from 133 channels to 1,252 and from 12 usable leads to 85. Twelve sessions ran their full 90-minute budget and stopped on the clock, which is what a working session looks like.

The cooldown ladder fired 303 times and revived 1,990 terms. Neither of the two strict rungs ever fired. Rung 3 fired 140 times and rung 4 fired 163 times, so every single revival all cycle came from the relaxed cooldown that was added yesterday. Without it the day looks like yesterday.

One prediction was wrong, and it matters. Yesterday's simulation said each revival would return about 24 terms. The real average was 6.6. So the keyword lane is not restocked, it is on a drip: the active pool sits at zero, a pass empties it, the next pass revives seven more. Measured this morning the pool held zero active terms, which is where it has been all cycle. The lane works, but it works the way a hand pump works.

Q3Why did comment sweep return 3,648 channels and only 19 leads?

Because its new seed picker chooses channels by comment volume, and on YouTube the channels with the most comments about YouTube are the ones teaching people to get rich on YouTube. Their commenters are aspiring creators. They don't own a business, so there's nobody to pitch.

The self-refilling seed engine shipped yesterday and this was its first full cycle. It added 30 seeds. Nearly all of the day's 3,648 channels came from those 30. The 39 hand-picked seeds are exhausted and produced 14 channels between them. Here is the whole lane in five rows:

This is not a cost problem. The whole pipeline ran on $7.45 of model spend, so 11 cents a lead is affordable. It's a selection problem: the picker was optimising for the biggest audience rather than the right one, and the fix is to change what it sorts on. That shipped today.

What broke, what's fixed

The keyword harvest is mined out and the backoff is the only thing hiding it

The harvest is the one process that puts genuinely new search terms into the book. It ran 11 times this cycle and pulled about 4,400 raw autocomplete phrases each time. Almost all of them were phrases it had already rejected on a previous run:

It walks the same 42 seed phrases every run, and that list hasn't grown since 2026-07-13. Autocomplete gives back nearly the same answers each time, so the input barely changes and neither does the output. The low-yield backoff caught this correctly and skipped 355 of 361 harvest attempts, which is the guard doing its job. It also means the term book has no restock path at all right now, and the only thing standing between the keyword lane and yesterday's outcome is the seven-terms-at-a-time revival drip.

The backoff message names the remedy itself: new seeds. That's rec #3 below.

Three quarters of the term book is now guesswork

The book holds 14,751 paused terms. 10,772 of them are probes, phrases invented by a cheap model or scraped from autocomplete, and they convert at 0.23%. The 3,979 real book terms convert at 2.25%, ten times better. The campaign wrote about 40 fresh probes after nearly every one of its 348 passes, because the fade check fires whenever a pass returns fewer than 12 fresh leads and no pass all cycle returned more than 3.

That's the second thing fixed today. Revived proven terms were being written at priority 30 while those fresh probes went in at 70, so a term with a measured conversion rate was ranked below a phrase a model had invented seconds earlier.

One thing worth knowing that is not a fault

OpenRouter cost $7.45 across 11,208 calls, up from $2.21. The rise is intake, not waste: the pipeline found 7,832 channels instead of 4,407 and every one gets scored. That works out to 2.1 cents per lead worth contacting and 6.3 cents per lead parked. Anthropic spend was $0.

One commit in the finder carrying two changes. Typecheck clean, all 193 unit tests pass, and both regex sets were checked in node against the 30 real seed channels pulled from the live API. No .env file was touched.

RepoCommitChange
youtube-lead-finder-v171d235cComment-sweep seed discovery ranks by audience before comment volume; revived proven terms outrank invented probes

The seed ranking. Candidate seeds now sort into three tiers, business audience first, general creator education second, YouTube hustle last, and only then by comment volume. Checked against the 30 seeds the engine actually picked, the tiers line up with realized yield exactly: business 1.05%, creator education 0.52%, hustle 0.14%. Hustle is tested before business, because half the hustle channels describe themselves with business words ("YouTube Automation Expert", "Coach Yotz") and testing business first put two of the worst seeds at the very top. Nothing is rejected. Seeds over the per-run cap stay eligible for a later run, so a low-tier seed is deferred rather than thrown away. Aprilynne Alter is the reason for that restraint: she came out of "start a faceless youtube channel", the most hustle-flavoured query in the bank, and returned the best yield of the entire cycle. A hard filter would have lost her. The tier is written into each seed's source string and the run log, so next cycle can measure whether the ranking earned anything.

The term priority. Raised from 30 to 72, above both probe tiers and below hand-curated terms at 75, and made tunable with PROVEN_REVIVE_PRIORITY. This mattered less when the never-run reserve still had stock. It has been empty since about 08-07, so the revival is now the only thing refilling the pool, and all 1,990 revived terms this cycle landed underneath the fresh probes. If a revived term turns out to be mined out after all, the fast-pause rule retires it again after one run, so being wrong costs a single search.
