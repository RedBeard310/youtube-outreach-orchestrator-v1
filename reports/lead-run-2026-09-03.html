The three questions

Q1How did the pool gain 627 on a day it only found 90 creators?

The recovery lane did it, and for the first time the logs prove it lead by lead. Yesterday the pool numbers said the backlog was draining but the per-pass logs could only credit a handful of it. Today the two line up exactly.

needs_contact is the holding pen for creators good enough to contact who have no working email address. The recovery lane walks that pen looking for contact details, then checks whatever it finds. The cause is a change you made yesterday. The collecting half of the lane had its batch widened from 40 leads to 150. Yesterday the checking half was starving on an empty queue, running batches of one to ten. Three collecting passes of 150 ran this cycle, the queue filled, and the checking half went to full 200-lead batches. Roughly two thirds of every batch converts.

What this means for where the leads come from. Recovery produced sixteen times what fresh finding did. The backlog still holds 4,350 creators and the lane converts about 65% of what it manages to check, so on those numbers there is a lot left in it. The limit is how fast the collecting half can work, which is why widening its batch mattered so much.

Q2Why is the OpenRouter balance suddenly two days from empty?

Because yesterday's enrichment fix worked, and enrichment is the biggest spender in the whole pipeline. This is a real bill, not a billing error, and it is the one thing here that needs you rather than code.

Enrichment is the step that reads a creator's channel and builds the research file an email gets written from. Yesterday it was broken for four hours and did 96 leads. Today it did 556, nearly six times as many. Spend went from $16.73 a day to $82.05, and the balance dropped from $305 to $223.

The arithmetic closes cleanly. The finder writes its own spending log and that log accounts for $4.04 of the day. Enrichment writes no log at all. The unexplained remainder is $78, and 556 enrichments at the measured cost of about 14 cents each is $78. So the jump is entirely enrichment finally running at full speed.

Why this matters more than the number. If the balance reaches zero, everything stops at once, not just enrichment. The same account pays for scoring every channel the finder looks at, for both discovery lanes, and for the recovery lane. This already happened on 2026-08-25 and the whole pipeline went dark. The only guard we have is one that notices after it runs dry and restarts things once you top up.

One honest limit. The 14 cents per enrichment is the measured figure from the cost note in the brain, not something today's logs report, because nothing meters enrichment. The $82 itself is the provider's own account meter and is not in doubt.

Q3Why did discovery write nothing for the last five hours?

The YouTube key pool ran out of its daily allowance, and one lane responded by pretending to work rather than stopping. Each YouTube key gets a fixed allowance that refills at midnight Pacific and at no other moment. The pool drained at about 02:00Z, five hours before that refill.

Running out is not itself a fault. What the two lanes did next differed, and the difference is the finding.

The video-graph lane did the right thing and stopped. It exited cleanly with an honest reason, kept its place, and waited. Its only mistake was in the loop around it: it relaunched every fifteen minutes, so it started, found no live key, and quit 27 times across seven hours.

The recommended-videos lane did not stop, and that is the expensive one. When its keys died it quietly switched to the RapidAPI mirror, which was retired in August and whose account now sits about 150% over its plan. So it kept running for four hours and forty-one minutes writing nothing. Its log looked perfectly healthy the whole time: chunks advancing, a hundred or more new channels spotted each chunk. That reads as a working lane. It was not. Spotting a channel only needs a public web page, but assessing one needs a working key, so it found candidates all night and could not look at a single one. Its counters for leads scored, model calls made and money spent sat frozen at the same numbers for the whole four hours and forty-one minutes.

This is the second time in two days that a step reported success while doing nothing, and it is the same shape as yesterday's enrichment outage. Both are fixed below.

Recommended next — ranked

  1. Top up OpenRouter today or tomorrow. $223 left at $82 a day. If it empties, every lane stops at once: scoring, both discovery sweeps, enrichment and the recovery lane all bill the same account, and the pipeline went fully dark for exactly this reason on 2026-08-25. This has moved from "not urgent" yesterday to the only thing on this list with a deadline.
  2. Decide whether enrichment should have a spending ceiling. It is the majority of the LLM bill, it is metered nowhere, and one bug fix took it from $17 to $82 a day overnight with nothing to catch it. Every other lane has a cost cap. This is a judgement call about whether you want a ceiling here at all, given that a ceiling on enrichment would also stop leads becoming writable.
  3. Add YouTube keys. The pool ran dry five hours before its daily refill, so quota is now the binding limit on finding, ahead of seeds or terms. Both fixes above make that failure clean and visible instead of silent, but neither creates a single extra unit of quota. Adding keys is one command on the Mac.
  4. Widen the recovery lane's collecting batch again. Widening it from 40 to 150 is what produced today's record, and the checking half is still the faster of the two: it cleared full 200-lead batches all day and converted about 65% of each. The backlog holds 4,350 more. This is the cheapest lever on the board and it is now producing sixteen times what finding new creators does.
  5. The discovery lanes have under two days of road left between them. Recommended-videos has 7,828 seeds and video-graph has 3,246, and both walk material we generated ourselves. This is the same supply problem carried for thirteen days. It is worth noting that the video-graph lane came back today at a 4.3% hit rate, the best of any lane, which suggests raising its cap was the right call.
