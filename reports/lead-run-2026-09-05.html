The four questions

Q1Why did the pool gain 71 again, when yesterday's diagnosis said the cause was a spending cap?

Because the spending cap was only the visible half. The recovery lane resolves a creator's own website before nine of its ten collection methods can do anything. It has two ways to do that: a paid Brave search, and a free read of the creator's YouTube About page. Yesterday found the paid one dead. Today's finding is that the free one has never worked.

The method is 34-channel-page.ts. Its own header calls it "the one that unblocks the lane," and it was written precisely so the paid search would not be a single point of failure. It fetches the About page through fetchRaw, which caps its read at 1,000,000 bytes.

Measured this morning on four consecutive leads that the lane had recorded as having no website:

The About-tab links sit after the entire video grid and the player bundle, in the last four percent of the document. Every page is bigger than the cap. So the method fetched successfully, parsed successfully, found nothing, and reported no fault, because "this creator pinned no links" is a legitimate outcome that produces the identical site=(none) line.

This is the same shape as the 402 that hid inside if (!res.ok) return [] a day earlier, and the same shape as the enrichment batches on 09-02. Four cycles running, the defect has been a step wearing the success path's clothes.

Fixed and verified live. fetchRaw now takes a caller-supplied byte cap, defaulting to the old 1 MB so no other method changes, and reports whether it truncated. Method 34 asks for 4 MB, better than twice the largest page seen. Re-running the real method against those same four leads: davidburkus.com nickcarrier.com solvepmproblems.com now resolve, along with nine social profiles between them. MUMFORD LAW correctly reports "page rendered with no outbound links," which is the genuine case and is now distinguishable from the broken one. Each resolved site un-skips nine downstream methods for that lead.

What this changes about yesterday's recommendation. Raising the Brave cap is still worth doing, but it is no longer the only route. The free path costs no money, no API key and no YouTube quota, which matters because quota is the binding constraint on everything else.

Q2Yesterday's fix was supposed to stop the loop walling every 30 minutes. Why did it wall 21 more times?

Because it waited for a signal that was never going to arrive. Yesterday's change told the campaign loop to sleep until the midnight-Pacific refill if a sweep lane had already fallen asleep on the same drained key pool, reading the rest marker the sweeps write. The reasoning was that all copies of "out of quota" should agree.

No marker file has ever existed on disk. The loop fell straight through to the old flat 30-minute retry, and hard-walled at 19:05Z, then every 34 minutes through to 06:53Z, 21 times.

The dependency is structurally wrong, not merely unlucky. A finder pass spends 100 quota units on every search. The sweeps spend 1 unit per channel lookup and get their edges by scraping watch pages, which costs no quota at all. So the campaign lane hits the daily wall many hours before a sweep does, and on a day like today the sweeps never hit it. They walked 10,244 seeds and wrote channels in 23 of 24 hours while the campaign loop could not buy a single search. Waiting for a sweep to fall asleep meant waiting for a lane that was still working.

Fixed. The loop now asks its own session log. When the pool cannot serve a search, the finder rotates every key and aborts with a fixed line, and that line is a clean discriminator: present in all 21 walled sessions this cycle, absent from all 7 productive ones. Verified against every session log on disk. It then sleeps to the midnight-Pacific refill using the same clock arithmetic the sweeps use, which survives the November daylight-saving change and clears itself, since adding keys mid-day costs at most one extra sleep.

The gate matters as much as the sleep. The other thing that hard-walls this loop is term-supply exhaustion, and there were 26 starvation notices this cycle. Terms do not refill at midnight, so parking a term wall until morning would have been a fresh bug. A session with no exhaustion line keeps the old behaviour.

Q3How did discovery run all night if the key pool was drained by 19:00Z?

Because "the pool is drained" is not one condition. It is drained for searches. A search.list call costs 100 quota units and a channels.list call costs 1, and the recommended-videos lane finds its next channels by scraping public watch pages, which costs nothing.

So after 19:05Z the campaign lane could not run a single term, while the feed lane kept writing 44 to 109 channels an hour on the small change left in the pool. It produced 1,518 of the day's 2,162 channels and 58 of the 79 pitchable leads.

This is why the day looks healthier than yesterday on every discovery metric despite an identical parked number. Yesterday's 17 dark hours were not a worse quota situation. They were the feed lane having run out of seeds; today it rolled into a fresh lap and had material to walk.

Stated plainly because it changes what "add more YouTube keys" buys: more keys mostly buy keyword search, which is the lane that needs 100 units a query. The cheap lanes are limited by seeds, not by quota.

The lanes refill each other's books, a lane qualifies a good creator and that creator becomes a seed for the next lane, so a circuit with only one producing lane runs down.

The peer lane is the clearest signal: it walked 76 channels and produced nothing at all. It is not broken, it has simply read everything it owns.
