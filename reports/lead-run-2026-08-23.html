The three questions

Q1Did the day actually go up?

Yes, on every number that matters. 4,788 channels is the highest since 08-20, 276 good leads beats yesterday by 45, and 99 parked beats it by 12. Nothing crashed, no lane stalled, no hour of the day produced fewer than 150 channels.

Two things did not improve. The share of good leads with a verified email slipped from 37% to 35%, which is normal drift inside its usual 35–45% band and not a signal on its own. And the cost of a good lead ticked up from 2.6¢ to 2.7¢, because spend rose 25% while good leads rose 19%. Both are small. The day was straightforwardly good.

Q2Why did the top lane walk so much more?

Because each seed is handing it less to do. The sweep walks a chunk of 20 seeds, collects the channels those seeds link to, and pays two LLM round trips for each channel it has not seen before. Fewer new channels per chunk means a shorter chunk.

The sweep works from a frozen snapshot of its book and ignores the seed file until it is reset, so that commit changes the next refill, not this cycle's walk.

Q3Was the walk rate measured correctly?

No. It has been double-counting long sessions, and both of yesterday's findings rested on it.

The generator finds every session log that overlaps the 24-hour window and adds up how far each one got. That is exact for a lane whose sessions start and finish inside one day. The video-graph sweep is not that lane: it runs one continuous session for 22 to 24 hours. Today's window caught the tail of a session that began at 09:30 the previous morning, and counted that session's entire 5,204-seed history as work done in the last 38 minutes.

A cycle's seed walk is now read off the seed book, not off whole session logs

scripts/autopilot/debrief-data.ts, commit 21b3f84. This is the Q3 fix.

The exact number was already on disk and going unused. Each lane's state file carries how much of its book is walked, this generator has been writing that into the daily snapshot since 08-22, and the difference between two consecutive snapshots is precisely the work done between them. So the generator now differences the book and falls back to the old log sum only when the book cannot answer: no snapshot from yesterday (a fresh deploy, a skipped cycle), or a negative difference, which is how a re-lap or a refill that drops merged rows shows up. A new seeds_advanced_source field names which method produced each number, so the two can never be silently confused.

The throughput alarm also learned to hold its fire across the change. A baseline written before tonight is a log sum by definition, so comparing today's corrected number against it would manufacture a drop. The alarm reports the percentage, which is honest arithmetic, but will not escalate until both sides were measured the same way. That heals itself tomorrow.

It does mean the model frequently omits the score field and the fallback carries the lane. Not worth touching while the output is this good.
