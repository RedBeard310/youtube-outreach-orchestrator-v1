2026-09-21: the best day of the pause, and the recovery lane fell by two thirds

Two things happened and they point opposite ways. Yesterday's one-line fix to the Apify lane woke it up after fourteen days idle, and in 52 minutes it recovered 62 leads at ten cents each. That made today the biggest day since the discovery pause began, +95 parked. Underneath it, the Bloodhound recovery lane, which is the only lane that produces leads every day, fell from 55 parks to 19. Brave Search is the reason, and it has now crossed the line where the alarms can see it: website resolution failed for 78% of leads on the last collect pass. The Apify money is spent until the billing cycle resets on 30 September, so tomorrow the pipeline goes back to the sinking lane on its own.

The tall block in the morning is the Apify batch arriving. Apify finished at 09:13Z, the enrichment chain picked the 62 new leads up at 09:40Z and had them all researched by 12:32Z. That handoff is the part of the pipeline that works without anyone watching it: 86 leads enriched in four batches, zero failures, about four and a quarter hours of work in twenty-four.

The three questions

1Did the day add anything to the pile?

More than any day since the pause began. +95 leads parked into approved_hold, taking the pool from 6,751 to 6,846, while needs_contact fell to 4,241. The previous best of the pause was yesterday's 64, and the week before that was running at 25 a day.

62 of the 95 came from a single Apify batch, and 19 came from the recovery lane's own re-score. The other 14 are not attributable from the logs. So the day's record is one batch of money being spent, not the pipeline getting faster.

2Is the lane that runs every day still working?

It is shrinking, and the Apify batch hid it. The Bloodhound lane parked 19 against yesterday's 55. Its verify half tested 43 leads across three passes against yesterday's 103 across four, and three more verify passes ran and were handed nothing at all: skipped: no_pending_email_points at 08:00, 15:01 and 23:01.

An empty verify queue is not a verify problem. The collect half in front of it produces the email addresses, and its hit rate across the cycle's four passes went 45%, 44%, 26%, 30%, against a normal of about 46%. It is finding fewer emails because it is finding fewer websites, which is the next section.

3Did anything leave?

No, for the twelfth day. The 07:20 send fired at 15 leads and sent none. I checked this in the database rather than trusting the exit code: zero leads reached sent_to_smartlead in the whole cycle. The email pause set on 2026-09-10 refused the batch before it composed a word, which is exactly what it is for.

The shelf is now 6,570 leads researched and ready to write, 96% of everything parked, up from 6,484 yesterday. It grows every day and nothing takes anything off it.

Brave Search has crossed the line, and both alarms fired

The recovery lane resolves each creator's own website first, because nine of its ten collection methods need one. That is a Brave Search call, and each key bills against its own monthly cap. Yesterday's debrief recorded resolution failing for 42% of leads and noted that neither alarm could fire yet. Today it went past both thresholds:

Every one of those passes opens with All 2 Brave Search API key(s) refused: 402 Usage limit exceeded. Key _1 got $50 on 09-13 and key _2 has sat at its $5 cap since. Brave's API never reports a balance, so the only way to know is to count searches, and a 150-lead pass uses about 150 of them.

The alarms did their job and there is nothing to fix in them for this. The remedy is money, and a fix agent is not allowed to spend money. What it costs to do nothing is now measurable: the lane went from 55 parks to 19 in one cycle, and yesterday's 62-lead Apify batch is not repeatable until the Apify billing cycle rolls on 30 September.

What broke, and what is fixed

✓Yesterday's Apify fix paid for itself in 52 minutes

The lane had been logging resting: $16.11 left, $10 reserved, batch of 100 needs $7.02 twelve times a day since 09-06, arithmetic that could never pass. Yesterday's fix sized the batch to what is actually spendable instead of a fixed 100.

At 08:21Z it launched 87 channels, found published emails for 78, ran 80 ZeroBounce checks and flipped 62 leads into approved_hold at $0.099 per recovered lead, half the $0.20 ceiling that halts the lane. Then it went back to resting with $10.01 left, which is the reserve it must not touch, so it will not run again until the billing cycle resets on 30 September. The prediction in yesterday's debrief was 87 channels and about 51 recoveries. It got 62.

✗The alarm built to catch a slide was being silenced by the slide

On 09-10 the fixed 70%-no-website alarm slept through a ten-pass slide, so a second alarm was added that judges the lane against its own recent normal: the median hit rate of the eight passes behind the newest one. That baseline has the mirror-image flaw. A slide walks into its own baseline.

Measured against this repo's real collect log: a lane pinned at 28%, about 60% of normal, reads as a 37% fall on its first pass, 24% by the third and 0% by the fifth. That is roughly thirty hours from degraded to invisible, after which a lane running at well under half strength looks perfectly healthy. Today's own passes were already walking it down: the alarm fired seven times on the 26% pass and then went quiet for the 30% pass behind it.

Fixed in the orchestrator (da4b849). The baseline is now the higher of the short-window median and a 32-pass median, which is about eight days at the current cadence. A cliff is still caught immediately by the short window; a slow slide can no longer erase the memory of what normal was. A genuine permanent change, the book thinning to its hard tail, still ages out by itself after eight days rather than alarming forever.

The same commit stops both lane alarms repeating. They judge the last completed collect pass, the check-in runs hourly and the lane collects every six hours, so one bad pass was re-reported every hour: this cycle logged 9 copies of the collapse alarm and 7 of the yield alarm about exactly two passes. They now key on the pass, so each fires once per pass, four times a day, for as long as the condition lasts. Verified by running the check-in twice against the live logs: it fired once, then stayed silent, and exited healthy.

!Three debriefs are still missing, and the new field caught them

Yesterday's fix works. The grounded metrics now carry a missing_debriefs list, and it correctly names 09-17, 09-18 and 09-19, each with metrics on disk and no report ever written because the agent's login had expired. Today's agent authenticated normally, so the fault was the login, not the script.

Those three reports are still absent. Their metrics files exist, so they can be written after the fact, but that is a decision about whether it is worth the time rather than something to do silently.

!The session-start send fired again, at 15 leads

Unchanged from yesterday and still unexplained. Something runs a live npm run send the moment an agent opens this repo, inside the debrief unit's own process tree, and no hook exists in any settings file that can be read. It attempted 14 yesterday and 15 today, and the email pause refused both.

It is harmless only because the pause holds. If the pause lifts before the trigger is found, the next debrief agent sends whatever is ready at 07:20 without being asked.
