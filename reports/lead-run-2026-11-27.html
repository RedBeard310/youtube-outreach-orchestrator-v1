The fleet got better, which is new. The three questions

Q1The fleet recovered. Why did reach not move at all?

Because capacity was never what was stopping reach. This cycle is as close to a controlled test as this pipeline gets. The only thing that changed was the number of slots: 25 people planned yesterday, 61 today, a plan 144% bigger, driven by 2 more live mailboxes and 30 more messages of allowance. Emails went from 8 to 44. People reached for the first time ever went from 0 to 0.

Every one of the 36 extra slots went to somebody who had already been emailed. That is what the tier queue does: Siege fills slots by tier, the whale tier ran out of never-emailed people on the 10-13 push, and its re-offer queue takes every slot before a ready lower-tier person is reached. There are 374 whale-tier people waiting, 728 strong and 755 other, and 4,855 finished emails on the shelf for people who have never been contacted.

So the recommendation this report has led with for five mornings, restore the fleet, has now been partly granted and bought nothing in reach. The ordering of that queue is the lever, and it is a setting in the send path. Fleet repair is still worth doing, because it is worth doing for follow-ups and for volume. It just cannot be the answer to "we have reached one new person in eight days."

Q2The loss share fell from 68% to 28%. Did anything actually improve?

No. The cohort is identical and the denominator tripled. Seventeen people lost yesterday, seventeen today, all of them repeats both mornings, with the longest streak now ten snapshot days. Yesterday that was 68.0% of a 25-person plan; today it is 27.9% of a 61-person one. The share is the honest unit, which is exactly why it shipped two days ago, and it reads the arithmetic in both directions: a shrinking plan makes a flat leak look like a flood, and a growing plan makes it look like a repair.

One thing is genuinely better than yesterday, and it is the confidence rather than the number. Yesterday lost_new: 0 was a lower bound, because an earlier day in the window had named fewer people than it lost. Today the split covered all 17 and no day in the window is capped, so "nobody new was lost" is exact.

The causes do not change overnight because they cannot. Two of these people have channels YouTube no longer has. Three were refused because SmartLead already holds them as unsubscribed. Seven have run out of offers. One got no draft at all. None of that improves by being planned again tomorrow, and a lead whose channel is deleted will be refused on every future push forever.

Q3What took the 17, and what is fixable where?

Five were refused by SmartLead, ten never reached the push at all, two left no trace in the log, and one person the log forgot entirely was emailed anyway (the send ledger caught that, so it was not counted as a loss). Four push skips cost nobody: all four were voice-objection-proof drafts that were rewritten and sent in the same run.

By cause: 7 people ran out of offers, 3 are unsubscribed at SmartLead, 2 have channels YouTube no longer has, 1 got no draft because the writer could not find a clean job title for a plain business owner. The seven offer-exhausted were all turned down by love-your-channel-short for one precondition, no safe enemy in the research bank, and the per-variant split published yesterday reads it without opening a single bundle file: 7 of the 9 leads hold a bank with zero enemies in it, and 2 hold enemies the skill's safety filter ruled out. Those are opposite repairs. An empty bank is a research pass or a decision to stop planning that person; a filtered bank is a question about how strict the filter should be, and that one is Casey's.

None of the 17 is fixable in this repo. Dead channels, unsubscribes and the offer rotation live in the send path; empty research banks are a research decision.

Fix shipped this cycle

A chain that is silent because it is idle no longer reads like one that stopped

youtube-outreach-orchestrator-v1 · 7f0499a

With discovery paused, enrichment is the only thing that turns a recovered address into a lead anybody can write to, so "is the chain running" is the question that matters most in this snapshot. This morning it could not be answered. The chain published gate_cleared_transcripts with 12.4 hours of silence behind it, which reads as a chain that announced it was resuming batches and then stopped.

It had not stopped. The transcripts gate opened at 19:37:08 UTC onto a pool holding no inflow, and the chain says its idle line once and is then quiet on purpose until work arrives. That latch is only reset where a batch really runs, so a gate opening onto an empty pool left a promise standing as the newest line in the log. Two repairs, because the field was wrong in two places. The chain now resets that latch whenever any of its three gates clears, so it re-states idling_no_inflow instead of leaving the promise. And the snapshot now carries chain_unit_state, chain_unit_result and chain_unit_started, read straight from systemd, the same local-only probe the send path has used since 10-30.

The distinction is the point. An idling chain and a chain that died while idling publish the same silence and the same four zeros, and this is the second time in ten days this block has had to learn that a lane quiet by design cannot be measured by how long it has been quiet. A silent chain whose unit is active is idling; a silent chain whose unit is anything else is an outage; and a chain_unit_started that moves is a restart nobody ordered. All three read null when systemd cannot be read, which is unknown and never a dead chain.

Verified: shell syntax clean, typecheck clean, the 446-assertion snapshot suite all pass, and systemd returns active / success / 2026-11-14T06:13:41Z for the live unit. No enrichment, send or research behaviour was touched; the chain logs one extra line per gate clearance and nothing else.
