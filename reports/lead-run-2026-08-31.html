Two cycles, 48 hours, ending 06:00Z today. The left half is yesterday running normally and then falling off a cliff when the key file was emptied. The right half is this cycle: 24 consecutive empty hours. There is no shape to read here and no decline to analyse. The machine was switched off for all but the first hour and a half of it.

The three questions

Q1Why did an outage that fixed itself still cost a whole day?

Because stopping and starting were not the same kind of decision. Stopping is automatic. Starting was a person deleting a file.

Here is the sequence. Yesterday at 04:58Z your Mac's two-minute copy overwrote ~/env-storage/.env with an empty file, so every process loaded zero YouTube keys. The campaign failed twice in a row, and at 08:10Z the hourly health check spent a repair agent on it. That agent did the right thing: it worked out that the problem was an emptied secrets file, that it is not allowed to write secrets files, that the backup copies on disk were from July and would restore a smaller, older set of keys, and that guessing on an authentication path was the one thing it had been told not to do. So it wrote a note explaining all of that to logs/autopilot-halt.flag and stopped. At 08:40Z the campaign loop read that note and exited.

The keys came back by themselves. A later copy from your Mac carried a full file, and the bank holds 66 keys right now, numbered _1 through _66 with no gaps. The note in the file even said this would happen, and that everything would resume on its own once it did.

It did not resume, because nothing re-checks. Twenty hourly health checks in a row read that note and stood down, which is exactly what they are built to do. All five discovery lanes did the same thing: they relaunched on their timers, read the note, and quit in about two seconds. 253 relaunches across the four lanes that log it, and not one seed walked.

This gap was half-closed already, which is the part worth knowing. On 08-25 the OpenRouter account ran dry, everything stopped correctly, and nothing noticed the top-up. The fix then was a self-clearing check, but it was written to recognise that one cause and no other. An emptied key bank was not on the list, so the machinery to recover existed and simply did not apply. Fixed today by making the list a list. Details in the table below.

Q2Was anything at all getting done?

Yes, and more than usual. The enrichment backfill produced 224 finished leads against 104 the day before. That lane takes creators already sitting in approved_hold and builds the research bundle an email gets written from. It does not need YouTube keys, and it does not read the stop note, so it ran flat out for 24 hours with nothing competing for the machine.

So the day was not empty of work. It was empty of finding. Every creator enriched today was one we already had.

The parking number tells the same story. +5 into approved_hold, taking the pool to 3,793, and all five landed at 08:09Z in the last session before the stop. The needs_contact pool moved the other way, 4,921 down to 4,916, which is those same five leads being promoted out of it once an address checked out. Nothing arrived to replace them, because nothing was found.

Worth saying plainly: this lane running during a stop is correct, not a leak. It is the one part of the pipeline that has a backlog deep enough to work through without any new supply. It also turns out to be where the money goes, which is Q3.

Q3How is a stopped pipeline spending $42 a day?

Because the spending number in these reports has never been the account's spending number. It sums one repo's own log, the lead finder's. Every other repo draws on the same OpenRouter account and keeps no log at all.

Today made that impossible to miss. The finder recorded one call costing $0.0079. The account actually spent $42.79. The difference is the enrichment backfill from Q2: 224 bundles, fifteen model calls each, none of it counted.

The consequence was a runway figure of 42,965 days. Balance divided by a spending number that was missing 99.98% of the spending. The real figure, using the account's own meter, is 8 days on a $339.19 balance.

This is the same blind spot that emptied the account without warning on 08-25. The fix then was to watch the balance. That is not enough on its own, because a balance only becomes alarming once you know how fast it is falling, and the falling rate was the part being measured wrong. Fixed today by reading the provider's own meter instead of trusting each repo to report itself.

Reconstructed from the archived daily snapshots, the account has been running at $13 to $37 a day all week while these reports showed $4 to $7. Some of that gap is the provider settling costs a little after the fact, but most of it is enrichment that was never in the figure.
