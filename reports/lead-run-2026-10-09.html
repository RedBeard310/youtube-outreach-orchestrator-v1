2026-10-09: the first whole plan that ever got out, and the shelf turns out to last twice as long as we said

The push ran for 86 minutes without being killed and loaded 561 of a 567-email plan, the biggest and the most complete day on record. Casey's six-hour ceiling is the reason: 128 of those emails were loaded after 11:18:14Z, the second the old one-hour ceiling would have ended the run. Only six planned emails never went, and five of them were drafts our own quality gate refused. The other finding corrects yesterday's headline: 52% of the plan was a second or third offer to somebody already emailed, so the never-emailed shelf drains at about 272 people a day, not 561, and it holds about five to six weeks of email rather than under four.

The shape of the day

One bar per minute, 10:18Z to 11:45Z, counted from automator/logs/siege.log. The run started at 10:18:14Z, loaded its first email at 10:19Z, and finished on its own at 11:44:42Z. The rate is flat from the first minute to the last: it was loading 12 emails a minute at 11:44, the same as it was at 10:30. Nothing degraded, nothing backed up, and the five quiet minutes in the tail are batches whose drafts were being written rather than pushed.

The three questions

Q1Did the six-hour ceiling fix the push?

Yes, and the margin is comfortable. The run took 86 minutes 28 seconds wall to wall, so it needed less than a quarter of the new six-hour budget. siege-plan.service reports Result=success and the live unit reads TimeoutStartUSec=6h. Every one of the five steps in that oneshot finished, including the daily report, which wrote itself at 11:44:43Z. The three cycles that ended in a mid-batch kill (10-01, 10-02, 10-08) are closed.

What the fix was worth, exactly: 433 emails were loaded before 11:18:14Z and 128 after it. That timestamp is where the old 3600-second budget would have sent SIGTERM, so those 128 emails are the fix, measured rather than projected. The day also beat its own previous best by a wide margin: 561 loaded against 337 on 10-08 and 140 on 10-07.

Throughput held at 6.5 emails a minute across the whole run, up from 4.0 on 10-08. The pool-leak fix from 10-03 (7fd02b6fd) is still carrying that: 233 batches in 86 minutes is 22 seconds a batch, including writing the drafts. At this rate the six-hour ceiling covers a plan of roughly 2,300 emails, which is more than three times the current warmup cap.

Q2Six planned emails did not go out. What stopped them?

Five were refused by us, not by SmartLead. The push prints SKIP for a planned email whose draft the writer's own quality gate rejected after its retries, and there were five: two for an em dash outside the sign-off, one for bare-title format in the five-ideas email, one for an opener that did not start the required way, one for a rank superlative ("Your Best") the channel-metric rule bans. The sixth was SmartLead refusing a lead that was already_added_to_campaign.

So the shortfall is 1.1% of plan and it is a copy problem now, not an infrastructure problem. That is a new shape. Every cycle since 10-01 lost emails to a timeout, an account rate limit or a duplicate flag. This one lost them to five drafts that could not pass the house voice rules on three tries. The gate itself is working as intended: 179 of 184 model-written drafts passed, 97.3%, for $0.67 of OpenRouter spend across the day.

Five of those six were invisible in the snapshot until this morning. The grounded metrics file read sent: 561, failed: 1 against a plan of 567, because the parser counted SENT and FAIL and ignored SKIP. Five sixths of the day's shortfall had no counter and no reason anywhere, even though the reasons were sitting in the log. Fixed this cycle, and the snapshot now carries skipped: 5 with the reasons attached.

Q3How long does the shelf actually last? Yesterday said under four weeks.

About five to six weeks. Yesterday's number counted the wrong thing and overstated the drain by roughly double. A push of 561 emails does not spend 561 people off the shelf, because more than half of them went to people who have been emailed before. Checked against leads.outreach_sends for all 567 planned leads: 272 were a first-ever email and 295 were a repeat offer, 175 of them a second, 117 a third, 3 a fourth.

The shelf arithmetic agrees to within one lead. already_sent rose 1,027 to 1,268, a gain of 241, while 493 of the day's emails came out of the approved_hold pool. The other 252 were people already marked sent, so they cost the shelf nothing.

The repeat stream reopened two days ago, which is why this is new. Repeat offers were 11% of the plan on 10-07 and 52% on 10-08, the first full day after Casey set ignore_duplicate_leads_in_other_campaign to false. That flag had been telling SmartLead to skip every repeat person. With it off, Siege can finally do what it was designed to do, which is work through several offers per person, and that structurally halves how fast the never-emailed pool empties.

So: 6,317 never-emailed people at 272 a day is 5.8 weeks across four sending days and 4.6 weeks across five. Still finite, still the decision this cycle hands over, but there is a month and a half of road rather than three weeks of it.
