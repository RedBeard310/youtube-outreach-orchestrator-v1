The three questions

Q1Why did the sendable fleet shrink on a day nothing was sent?

Because five mailboxes were moved from clear to watch, and a mailbox on watch is given no slots for a new email. Nothing was stopped, the stopped band held at 34, and the clear count still fell 18 to 13. That is the lowest it has been this month, against 34 a week ago.

Here is the part that changes how you read it. 11 of the 45 held mailboxes are healthy right now. Eight of them have a clean reading and are waiting out a rule that needs 48 unbroken hours at a score of 90 or better before the hold lifts. Three of those eight are at 47 of 48 hours, so they clear within the hour unless a new reading knocks them back. One at 96.7 has been stopped since Friday and is 40 hours into the same wait. Three more are on watch for no fault of their own: when two of a domain's three addresses are sick, the third is held too, and all three of those score 96.7.

So the two rules compound. The bar to go on watch is a score under 90, and the bar to come off a hold is 90 or better held for two full days. A single email landing in a spam folder drops a mailbox from 96.7 to about 89.9, which costs it two days, and through the sibling rule it can cost its two neighbours the same two days. contentgetsclientsdigital.com did exactly that at 04:05Z today: one address read 88.4, and a sibling scoring 96.7 that had been clean since 10-16 was held alongside it. Two of today's five lost slots are collateral, not sickness.

None of this lives in the five pipeline repos. The gate is scripts/inbox-health.py in automator, and both numbers it turns on are config.

Q2Is the recovery lane finding anything new?

Barely, and the reason is that it has read this book before. The collect pass started a fresh lap and is back in the easy half, so web search cost almost nothing: 4 searches against 440 yesterday, and only 4 of 150 leads had no website on file. It found 673 contact methods and 669 of them were already recorded. Four were new, from 3 of 150 people. Across the cycle the lane added 15 contact points and 2 email addresses, against 94 points and 21 addresses over the prior week.

That is not a fault. The lane has walked its 2,398-lead book 12 times, and a re-walk is supposed to be mostly recognition. It does mean the lane's output is now set by how much of its book is still unread, which is none of it, rather than by Brave credit or by the verify queue. Both of those are clear: zero search refusals, and stranded held at 3.

One real fault did show up in the method errors, and it is fixed. Of 175 errors in the judged pass, 100 were the same condition, cross-domain redirect, spread across five different methods. A redirect is what a link-shortening address does: pin.it answers by sending you to pinterest.com. When a lead's channel links one of those, the code was filing it as that creator's website, which did worse than waste a page fetch. It filled the website slot, so the lane never went on to check the site it had already found for that person, and never asked Brave either. Every page method on that lead then died on the same redirect, every lap, permanently. Nine of the hundred were pin.it, eight paypal.me, four selar.co, three openinapp.link.
