The shape of the day

Each bar is one hour, 07:00 UTC Sunday to 07:00 UTC Monday. Height is channels discovered, colour is how many of them scored 6 or better. The first thirteen hours were steady and good. Then every bar after 19:59 UTC is empty, and empty means zero channels, not a thin hour. Discovery ran for 13 hours and the key pool switched it off for the other 11.

The three questions

Q1Where did a record 488 leads come from on a half-dark day?

Almost none of it was new finding. The day discovered 1,604 channels, 81 of them worth pitching, and only 31 of those had a verified email by the close. The other 450-odd parks came out of the pile of creators we found weeks ago and could never reach.

Three things emptied that pile at once, and two of them were changes you shipped mid-cycle at 16:45 UTC. Counting each parked lead by where its address came from: 254 were addresses the Apify scraper pulled from behind YouTube's "View email address" button, 216 came from the free Bloodhound methods, mostly a plain mailto link on the creator's own website, and 237 had no stored contact point at all, which is the signature of the third thing.

That third thing was accepting role addresses. Addresses like info@ and team@ had been parked as "risky" and never used. Your rule was that a role address is fine as long as it is real and won't bounce, and the code now takes only the ones where ZeroBounce actually reached the mailbox, holding back the catch-all domains where nothing was proved. Reopening the backlog on that rule checked 227 addresses and recovered 187, an 87% hit rate on leads we had already written off. That backlog is now empty: zero risky-verdict leads are left waiting.

The pool of creators found but never contacted fell from 4,243 to 3,872, its third fall on record and by far the largest. It has now dropped on three of the last six days, so recovery outproducing discovery is a pattern rather than a good afternoon.

Q2Why did the finding side go dark at lunchtime and stay dark?

At 19:29 UTC the video-graph sweep stopped with "YouTube key pool exhausted" and did exactly what it is built to do: it slept 11.6 hours until the midnight-Pacific refill rather than retrying into a wall every fifteen minutes. Everything else that needs the YouTube API went quiet with it. That behaviour is correct, and it is why eleven of twenty-four hours produced not one channel.

The part that isn't normal is what happened at the refill. The sweep woke at 07:05 UTC, five minutes after midnight Pacific, and immediately retired twelve keys in a row on "daily quota exhausted". So I tested every key by hand at 07:35, thirty-five minutes past the reset: 15 working, 50 out of quota, 1 suspended project, out of 66. Keys the rotation had not touched all day were refusing too, so this is not the rotation mislabelling its own spending. The pool did not come back.

Two explanations fit, and they need different answers. Either Google's reset is running very late, in which case the pool fills later in the day and this costs us the morning. Or those projects have had their YouTube quota cut, which is what a compliance audit does, and no amount of waiting fixes it. One reading can't tell them apart, so the daily snapshot now takes that reading every morning twenty minutes after the reset, which is the moment the answer is worth the most. If tomorrow's number is 15 again, it's the projects.

Worth holding onto: the cheap lanes barely use this. A keyword search costs 100 quota units, while the sweeps spend 1 unit per channel and get their leads by reading watch pages for free. So a drained pool hurts the sweeps last and the keyword lane first, and more keys mainly buy keyword search.

Q3The Apify scraper is the best producer we have. It just ran out of money 23 days early.

It spent $83.89 of its $100 month and stopped itself on Saturday evening. The wrapper keeps $10 back on purpose, so with $16.11 showing it can no longer fit a $7.02 batch and now rests quietly until the billing month resets on 30 September. Nothing is broken. It is simply out.

The reason it ran out is the bug you fixed mid-cycle, and the fix changes the whole business case. A channel that publishes no email left no trace, and the queue is sorted best-first, so the same 82 misses sat at the top of every batch and were paid for again. 485 channels became 825 billed scrapes. What looked like a collapsing hit rate, 89% falling to 17% over eight batches, was never the good channels running out: on distinct channels the rate is 83% and flat. Now that each attempt is recorded, the first clean batch found 85 of 100 and parked 60 of them at $0.117 per lead, comfortably under your $0.15 to $0.20 limit and the cheapest recovery in the pipeline.

What that buys, in plain numbers. 3,644 leads in the parked pool have never been through this scraper. At $0.0702 a channel that is about $256 to run all of them, and at the measured rate roughly 2,100 more parked leads. Yesterday's honest planning figure of 25 parks per 100 channels was computed on the double-billed batches and is too pessimistic; the fixed lane runs at about 60. The lane also brakes itself now, halting after two consecutive batches over $0.20 a lead, so a raised cap can't quietly turn into a bad month.
