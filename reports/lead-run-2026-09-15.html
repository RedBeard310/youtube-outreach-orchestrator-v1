The three questions

Q1Is 789 a real number?

Yes, and it is worth being precise about what kind of number it is, because it will not repeat on its own.

Three of the four sources are decisions Casey made. 290 leads moved at 12:06Z and 423 at 02:59Z, the valid-email halves of the two email-finding runs over the 5,395 under-rated 10k+ leads. Both had been sitting scored, verified and refused by the old hold gate for days, waiting on the enrichment spend that parking starts. Saying yes released them in a single write.

The fourth source is the part that will repeat. The recovery lane parked 60 leads by itself, in three hourly passes of 57, 2 and 1. That is yesterday's lever #1 shipped and working: a lead whose email verifies now gets its Signal Score v2 re-run automatically, which adds the contact point it has earned and clears the hold gate. Yesterday the same shape produced 40 verified emails and 6 parks. The first pass alone took 57 of 57.

So the honest reading is two records in one day. The 789 is a backlog release and it is finished. The 60 is the lane's new floor, and it is ten times what the lane could do 48 hours ago.

Q2What happened when the OpenRouter account emptied?

Everything downstream of it failed, and nothing anywhere noticed for nine hours.

The account hit zero at 22:10Z. Every enrichment lead ends in a call to it, so from that moment every lead failed. But it failed late: a lead runs its YouTube harvest, then buys its transcripts from Decodo, then dies at stage 4 on openrouter HTTP 402. The paid work happens first and is thrown away.

The chain had no way to know. It has a preflight gate for Supadata and Decodo, and a preflight gate for the YouTube key pool, and none for the third paid dependency. So it launched nine more batches over nine hours: 89, 89, 89, 89, 89, 99, 99, then 500 and 500. 1,635 lead attempts, every batch exit=0 done=0.

Its own guards all behaved correctly and none of them could help. The hard-wall guard watches the exit code, which was 0 every time. The zero-progress guard fired on all nine, read the shape correctly as an infrastructure outage, refunded the attempts and backed off, which is exactly right, and then retried an hour later into the same dead account. By the seventh trip it passed its refund limit and real leads began counting attempts against a fault that had nothing to do with them. The batch size grew as it went, because the pool grew when Casey promoted the 423, so the last two attempts were 500 leads failing 499 apiece, two hours each.

And twelve hourly check-ins ran through all of it and printed healthy. The check-in did not watch the backfill chain at all. That was survivable while discovery ran. With every sweep off, enrichment is the pipeline, and it was the one lane nobody was looking at. Both halves are fixed below.

Q3The recovery lane's hit rate halved. Is Brave back to blame?

No, and the measurement says so clearly enough to retire an assumption that has led two debriefs.

The lane's collect hit rate fell across the day: 82% → 35% → 47% → 47%, against a peak of 97% on the last pass of 09-14. The reflex for a fortnight has been to blame website resolution, which nine of the ten collection methods depend on.

That is not what happened. The no-website rate over the last three passes was 2, 2 and 3 leads out of 150, which is 1% to 2%, the best on record, against 13% yesterday and 91% on 09-13. Brave refusal lines stopped appearing in the log entirely after the first pass. The websites are resolving; the leads behind them simply carry fewer contact points.

That is the book getting harder, not a fault. The 09-13 fix put leads with an already-paid-for website at the front of each lap, and those are now being consumed. What remains is thinner ground. 1,272 contact points off 316 of 600 leads is still the second-best collection day on record, so the lane is working, it is just past its own easy tier.

One consequence worth carrying forward: the standing recommendation to raise Brave's cap rested on resolution being the constraint. On today's numbers it is not, and a top-up would buy less than it would have a week ago. That call is Casey's either way, but the ranking behind it has changed.

Q4The key pool read 15 of 66 again. Does that settle anything?

No, and for the first time the reason is us rather than the measurement.

For five mornings the pool has probed identically at 15 working, 50 out of quota, 1 suspended, always about 20 minutes past the reset, with nothing of ours spending a unit overnight. That made the readings one measurement repeated, and the open question was whether the refill is late or the quota was cut.

Today genuinely spent the pool. The 290-lead enrichment batch ran from 12:20Z to 22:11Z and had already killed keys 31 through 40 by the time it finished. The 1,635 failing attempts that followed ate the rest, and by 06:26Z the run log reads All 66 direct YouTube key(s) exhausted/blocked. So today's 15-of-66 is consistent with ordinary spending and discriminates nothing either.

The untried test has not changed and is not worth running today: one probe six or more hours after the reset on a quiet day, 66 quota units total. It needs a day where nothing of ours is spending, and the enrichment backlog means the next few days will not be that.

The pattern behind the day. Three times in a week this pipeline has paid for work and then been refused at the last step: a missing campaign id on 09-09, the email pause on 09-14, and an empty LLM account today. Every guard was correct and every one of them sat at the end. A condition that will refuse every item in a batch identically belongs at the front, where it costs one probe instead of a thousand attempts. Both fixes today are that move, applied to the two places it was still missing.
