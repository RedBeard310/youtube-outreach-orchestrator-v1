2026-09-23: the first day the pipeline produced nothing, and nothing was broken

The pipeline parked zero leads. Not a low number, zero, for the first time on record. Every pool ended the day at exactly the size it started: 6,853 parked, 4,243 waiting for a contact, 6,586 researched and ready to write. No process crashed, no key expired, no money ran out, no guard misfired. Every lane ran on schedule and found there was nothing left in front of it. The recovery lane spent 600 lead slots to move 4 leads out of its book, because it is reading that book for a second time and a second reading of a lead it already emptied finds nothing. Enrichment ran no batches at all, because its queue is empty. This is what a pipeline looks like when the work runs out and the only remaining inputs are decisions.

The three questions

1Why did a day of correctly running processes produce zero?

Because the recovery lane is on lap 6 of its list, and a lap is a second reading. When the lane works a lead, it takes whatever contact details that lead is willing to give. If it comes away with a phone number and a social handle but no email, the lead stays in the book, because the book holds every lead that still has no email. So the next lap reads it again, runs the same thirteen methods against the same website, and gets the same answer.

The numbers say it plainly. The book started the cycle at 3,348 leads and ended at 3,344. Four leads left it, and the lane spent 600 lead slots to get them. That is 150 readings per lead gained. On 09-20 the same four passes produced 1,063 contact points. Same code, same schedule, same search keys, different part of the book.

Yesterday's debrief measured the shape of this: 3,016 of the book's 3,347 leads already hold a contact point from an earlier lap, and only 331 have never given up anything. The lane walks 600 slots a day, so those 331 are about half a day of genuinely new reading. This cycle spent most of its day past that point.

2Is anything actually broken?

No, and that is worth stating carefully because it is unusual. Zero fatal error signatures. Zero halts. Zero quota stops. $0.00 of Anthropic spend against a $150 ceiling. OpenRouter is running at $1.10 a day with $210.16 left, which is 191 days of runway. The YouTube key pool read 15 working of 66 for the tenth morning in a row, which is the settled state and harmless while nothing is searching. The do-not-contact sync, the check-ins and the debrief timer all ran.

The stranded counter, which is the number that would say a lead has fallen into a gap between the two halves of the lane, sat at 3 all cycle. It has been 3 since the gap was closed on 09-13. Nothing is being hidden.

Enrichment ran no batches, and that is also correct. Its last run was one lead, finishing at 07:13 UTC on 09-22, and it has logged no pending inflow, idling ever since. Its pool reads 70 of which 69 are permanently excluded, so the real queue is one lead. Enrichment has nothing to do because the lane that feeds it produced nothing to feed it.

3So what actually changes the number?

Three things, and none of them is code. They are all decisions, and they are ranked below.

The lane has roughly 331 unread leads left, about half a day. After that it produces single digits until something new arrives. The three sources of something new are: the Apify lane, which recovered 62 leads in 52 minutes on 09-21 and cannot run again until its billing cycle rolls on 30 September; lifting the discovery pause, which is Casey's word alone; and funding Brave Search, which is the smallest of the three and is explained below.

Meanwhile the output side has been shut for two weeks. 6,586 finished leads are researched, bundled and one command from a written email. The 07:20 send attempted 18 and sent 0, refused before composing by the fleet-wide email pause that has held since 09-10. That is day 14. The shelf is the largest thing in the pipeline and it has not moved in three days.

Brave Search, ranked honestly

Brave is the web search the recovery lane uses to find a creator's own website, and nine of its ten collection methods need one. One of its two keys has sat at a $5 monthly cap since early September, so a refusal line prints on most passes. It is tempting to read that line as the cause of a quiet day. It is not the cause of this one.

Website resolution worked fine this cycle. The four passes resolved a site for 119, 147, 148 and 147 of their 150 leads, which is 79% to 99%. Search was up. Yield collapsed anyway: on the worst pass, 0 of the 119 leads with a working website produced a single contact point. You cannot blame the search when the search succeeded and the answer was still empty.

Funding Brave is still worth something, just less than it looks. Of the book's 3,347 leads, 620 need a paid search to get a website at all. The other 2,727 already have one stored or linked, and reading those back costs nothing. So a top-up buys attempts at 620 leads, most of which have already been read on an earlier lap. Rank it below the discovery decision and below Apify.

What broke, what's fixed

Yesterday's fix proved itself within a day

The 09-22 debrief shipped collectPassAttribution() (9dc4eb6) because all three of that day's yield alarms had told Casey to raise the Brave cap on passes where search was working. The only test behind that advice was whether the words Brave Search API key appeared anywhere in the log tail, and since one key is permanently capped, that line prints on nearly every pass. A test that is always true is not a test.

This cycle the alarm fired four times, and all four correctly attributed the fall to book_rewalk. Not one blamed Brave. Two of the four ran on passes where no refusal line appeared at all, and the other two named the refusal and then ruled it out in the same sentence, quoting the pass's own 98% resolution rate. Zero wrong spending recommendations, against three the day before.

A third alarm fired 13 times about one window, and blamed something the lane's own state file refutes

bloodhound_collect_walking_in_place watches whether the lane is re-reading leads. It fired 13 times between 08:11 and 20:11, every firing byte-identical: 600 lead slots, 305 distinct leads, ratio 0.508. Thirteen reports of one event.

This is the same noise fixed on 09-21 for the other two lane alarms (da4b849 keyed them on the pass they judge). This alarm was left out of that fix. Third outing of that class, one layer down.

Worse, its text asserted a cause: "this is the cursor failing to advance rather than the lane running out of work, check for a collectCursor that has not moved and for collectRewinds climbing." The lane state at the time read collectRewinds: 0, collectSearchDeadRewinds: 0, and a cursor that had moved. The alarm was sending Casey to hunt a fault that its own state file said did not exist. The real cause was the lap boundary, and it cleared itself: the walk went back to a clean 1.00 two passes later, with nobody touching anything.

Shipped: 0874d8a in the orchestrator

The alarm is now keyed on the window it judges, using the same alreadyObserved mechanism its two siblings got on 09-21. Thirteen firings become one. It loses the repetition, not the signal.

And it reads its cause off the lane state instead of asserting one. The new collectRewalkCause() picks between four answers and records the readings behind the choice in the observation itself, so the next reader can check the work:

  • cursor_pinned, when the cursor has not moved across a completed pass. This is direct evidence, not inference, and it is the fault the alarm exists for.
  • rewind_loop, when a rewind counter rose since the last firing. A rise, not a standing count: the counters are lifetime totals, so one historical rewind would otherwise read as a live loop forever. That is the same "always true, so not a test" shape as the Brave line.
  • lap_rewalk, when neither holds and the lane has closed a lap. It says plainly that nothing is faulting and that the constraint is leads to walk, not search credit or a cursor bug.
  • unexplained, when the lane has closed no lap and nothing rose. It names no remedy, which is the right answer when there is no evidence for one.

Verified: typecheck clean; 70/70 tests pass, 7 of them new, including a case where the cursor genuinely does not move which must still read as cursor_pinned, so the fix cannot silence the fault it came from. Run against the real lane state and the real 09-22 log window, the alarm now reads lap_rewalk where it used to assert a pinned cursor. The full check-in was run end to end on live logs and exited healthy, staying correctly silent because the current window is a clean 1.00 walk.

The thing worth remembering from this cycle. A pipeline with nothing left to do and a pipeline that is broken look identical from the outside: both produce zero. Every guard held, every timer fired, every process exited clean, and the output was nothing. The only numbers that told the difference were the ones nobody alarms on, which are the size of the book and how much of it has been read before. Two of the three alarms that did fire were about a genuine slowdown with no fix, and the third spent the day pointing at a fault that did not exist.

Deliberately not done: nothing touching discovery, no .env or secrets, no alarm threshold lowered to make a known-cause quiet day look better, no release of held leads, no email sent, no halt flag written. The loop is left running for the next cycle.
