The three questions

Q1Why did nothing park at all?

Because both halves of the recovery lane had finished the work they could see, and what they could see was a small fraction of what was there.

The collect half selected leads from needs_contact that had no contact point of any kind. That pool was 251 leads. The lane walks 600 lead-slots a day, so it re-read that book about twice daily. Measured across the last six passes: 903 lead-slots spent on 253 distinct leads, and four of those six passes ended Collected 0 contact points from 0/150 leads. Twenty-four hours of work produced 6 contact points across 2 leads.

The verify half was drained from the other direction: its queue stands at 4 leads against a 200-lead batch. A lane with nothing in either queue parks nothing. No process crashed, no quota ran out, and no alarm fired for a fault, because there was no fault of that kind to find.

Q2Where did the other 2,780 leads go?

Nowhere. They were sitting in a gap between the lane's two queries, and the gap was one clause wide.

Collect skipped any lead holding any contact point. Verify only ruled on leads holding an email contact point. So a lead the collector had worked once and come away from with a website, a phone number or a social handle satisfied neither: collect would never look at it again, and verify could never rule on it. That is 2,780 of the 3,735 leads in needs_contact, and 2,282 of them already had a website found and stored.

That last number is the galling one. Resolving a creator's website is the expensive half of the job, the half that spends Brave. For 2,282 leads it was already paid for, stored in the database, and then skipped over. All 2,780 clear the score bar and 2,777 carry an outreach_status the lane accepts, so the contact-point clause was the only thing hiding them.

This is the same mistake as 08-24 and 08-27, a third time in the same lane: a query that reads "we got something for this lead" as "this lead is finished." It is not finished until it has an email, because an email is the only thing that can flip it to approved_hold.

Q3Is Brave's cap still the problem?

It is a real problem and it was never the main one. Both answers matter, in that order.

Brave is genuinely refusing. Every collect pass opened with 402 Usage limit exceeded, and 825 of 903 leads resolved no website, a 91% failure rate against a healthy 15%. Nine of the collector's eleven methods need a website, so those passes were close to no-ops.

But raising the cap this morning would have bought 251 leads, not the 3,735 in needs_contact. For nine days the hourly check-in told Casey to top up a search plan, and the top-up would have unblocked well under a tenth of the pool. An alarm that names the wrong remedy is worse than no alarm when the remedy it names costs money.

After today's fix the ranking flips, and Brave becomes worth paying for. With a 3,028-lead book, roughly 1,332 leads have no site to reuse and no declared link, so Brave is the only way to reach them. That is now a real spend decision with a real return behind it, where this morning it was not.
