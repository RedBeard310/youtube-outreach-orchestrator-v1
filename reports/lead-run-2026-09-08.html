The three questions

Q1Parking fell from 488 to 142. Is something wrong?

No. Yesterday was the backlog emptying, and it emptied. The 488 came from three one-off pushes hitting at once: the Apify scraper, the free Bloodhound methods, and reopening every lead that had been filed as a "risky" address. All three of those are now spent. The risky backlog is at zero, and the Apify scraper ran out of money on Saturday and rests until the billing month turns on 30 September. It made no runs at all this cycle, exactly as designed.

Strip the one-offs out and the ordinary machine got better, not worse. Of the 142 leads parked today, 52 are channels we found today. Yesterday only 31 of 488 were. So the plain path, find a creator and reach them the same day, roughly held its rate on a much larger discovery day.

The free half of the recovery lane is still the strongest thing we own. Its collect pass pulled 697 contact points off 132 of 150 leads. Every one of those is free: it reads the creator's own website. Only 18 of 150 leads came back with nothing, so website resolution is healthy and the Brave search cap is not biting.

The pool of creators found but never contacted fell again, 3,872 to 3,854, while taking in 46 new arrivals. Small, but it is now the fourth fall in eight days, so recovery keeping pace with arrivals is holding as a pattern.

Q2The key pool read 15 of 66 for a second morning. What does that settle?

Less than we agreed it would, and the reason is worth knowing. Yesterday's rule was simple: if the number comes back 15 again, the quota on those projects has been cut and buying more keys from the same Google accounts buys nothing. It came back 15 again. Fifty keys out of quota, one suspended project, measured with 66 separate live calls twenty minutes after the reset. That reading is Google refusing, not our bookkeeping.

But both readings were taken at the same moment of the morning, twenty and thirty-five minutes past the reset, so both are equally consistent with a refill that simply lands late. Two identical measurements of the same instant on two days are one measurement repeated, not two independent tests.

What the dig did turn up is a bug on our side, and it is a costly one. When a key answers "daily quota exhausted", we write it down as dead until the next reset, which is the right rule almost all the time. The trouble is when we take that reading. The sweeps sleep until the reset and then touch every key within seconds of waking. Today 11 keys were written off between 07:11 and 07:19 UTC, all of them inside the first twenty minutes of the new day, each one pinned shut for the following 23 hours and 49 minutes on the strength of one early answer.

Fixed and already applied to today. A quota refusal in the first three hours after a reset now sets a 30 minute cooldown instead of a full-day lock. If the key really is spent it gets refused again on its next turn, which costs one web request and no quota at all, because an exhausted key rejects the request rather than charging for it. If the refill was just late, the key comes back the same morning instead of tomorrow. Today's 11 were released back into rotation by hand under the new rule rather than left locked until Wednesday.

It also answers the original question for free. From tomorrow, a key that gets refused at 07:11 and then stops reappearing was a late refill. A key that keeps getting refused all morning belongs to a project whose quota was genuinely cut. The file that records this is youtube-lead-finder-v1/logs/youtube-dead-keys.json, and it holds fingerprints, never key values.

Q3All three graph lanes ran out of road on the same day.

These three lanes find leads by walking a list of seed videos and looking at who shows up next to them. They are the cheapest source we have, about three cents a lead, and between them they found 2,759 of today's 3,046 channels. Today all three finished their lists at once.

Video-graph has 53 seeds left of 90,139. The recommended-videos feed has 57 left of 12,970. The peer network has zero left of 12,876, and it found 142 channels today with not one of them worth pitching. Video-graph is the only one still healthy, and only because it rebuilds its own seed list every hour out of channels the scorer newly approved.

This is not a bug and there is nothing to repair. A lane runs out of road when it has looked at everything on its list. The options are to re-walk the list, which pays less each time round (the feed lane's laps went from 0.45 good leads per seed on the first lap to 0.014 on the sixth), or to feed it genuinely new seeds, which only the other lanes can produce. Which of those to do is a spend decision, so nothing was changed unattended.
