The re-walking is the design working, not a loop

When a pass finishes and the log shows that every search key refused, the lane rewinds its cursor and reads the same batch next time. The reasoning is in the code: a lead is only marked as walked once somebody actually searched for it, and the re-walk doubles as the probe that notices Brave coming back, so the lane resumes on its own with nobody diagnosing it.

It also refuses to do that forever. After one re-walk that collects nothing at all, the lane accepts the evidence and moves on, which is what the 00:00 pass did. So the pattern across the day is two re-walks, one advance, one re-walk. Both halves of that are prior fixes from this same series, added on 09-10 and 09-12 after the lane spent a cycle re-reading one 58-lead batch three times while 3,700 leads waited.

What broke, what's fixed

A finished email was refused over the writer's own note to itself

Twenty minutes after this cycle closed, the session-start send composed five emails and pushed four. The fifth was refused, and the reason it gave was 5 unresolved placeholder(s): [56] [61] [66] [69] [68]. Those five numbers were sitting on the ends of the five video titles the email proposed:

3. Divorce Before Selling Your Home? This Legal Order Could Prevent Disaster [66]

They are character counts. The title rules injected into both writers ask for a title of 55 to 65 characters, so the model counted its own work and showed the count. The guard that stops an email leaving with an unfinished merge tag in it treats any square bracket as unfinished, which is right, and it stopped the send.

The cost of being right here was a lead. Pacific Cascade Legal is researched, has a verified address and a written email, and it landed on failed. It will be re-fired, and the next writer is free to print the count again. The prompt already forbids printing it and names two shapes it must not use; the model invented a third. Telling a model not to do something is not a guard.

Shipped (f6f9a3f, youtube-email-outreach-v1). A bracketed one-to-three-digit number sitting alone at the end of a line is now removed instead of refused, because it is scratch paper and never content. It is stripped in resolveMergeTags, which is Siege's push path, and in buildAddLeadRequest, the one function every send path builds its request in. Nothing else is rewritten: a bracket holding any other text, or one in the middle of a sentence, still stops the send exactly as before.

Verified: tsc clean, the full suite of 271 tests passes with 5 new ones, including a replay of the real refused email off disk and a proof that a clean body comes back byte-identical.

The lesson. Today's two findings are the same shape. The lane could not read 94% of its leads and still reported a yield, and the writer's own character count was read as an unfinished email. Measuring the cause is what turned one of them into a three dollar decision and the other into a four line fix.
